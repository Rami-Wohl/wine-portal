# Explore foundation roadmap

Status: active execution roadmap  
Last reviewed: 2026-09-24

This roadmap turns Oenocademy's existing concept collection into a systematic,
globally useful foundation for viticulture, vinification and wine science. It
orders the work; it does not replace the binding research, authoring, knowledge
architecture or visual policies.

## Intended outcome

At completion, a reader should be able to understand how a vine functions, how
site and grower decisions affect grapes, how grapes become different kinds of
wine, and how composition and maturation affect what is perceived. Region and
producer pages can then link to these concepts instead of repeatedly explaining
the same mechanisms.

The work starts from the current graph. Existing entities and articles are
reviewed, extended and linked before new packages are proposed. A missing
paragraph is not automatically a missing entity, and a term mentioned in prose
is not automatically entitled to its own page.

## Non-negotiable authoring rules

- Treat the foundation as global wine knowledge. Bordeaux may provide an
  example, but may not silently become the model for all climates, varieties or
  wine styles.
- Explain mechanism before sensory consequence. Separate demonstrated effects,
  plausible interpretations, producer observations and tradition.
- Preserve one canonical owner for reusable facts. Hubs synthesize; satellite
  entities explain concepts that are independently searchable, reusable and
  substantial enough to maintain.
- Keep knowledge depth local to each block. `basis`, `verdieping` and
  `gevorderd` describe the learning burden of that explanation, not the
  prestige or obscurity of the subject.
- Dutch and English must convey the same reviewed knowledge and block structure.
- Iterate every section until its scoped questions are answered, or record the
  remaining gap explicitly. Completeness means complete for the declared scope,
  not exhaustive at any cost.
- Use reviews and authoritative synthesis for shared mechanisms. Add primary or
  specialist sources for disputed, quantitative, recent or unusually specific
  claims; do not create a citation cloud for uncontested background knowledge.
- Prefer documentary photography for observable subjects and educational
  diagrams for structures, flows, timelines, comparisons and invisible
  mechanisms.

## Publication forms

| Form | Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hub page | A coherent system, decision field or method family whose parts need comparison and navigation | A directory with only one-line definitions |
| Concept entity | A durable idea with its own explanation, relations and reuse across several contexts | Every technical noun or soil nickname |
| Section on an existing page | Context that only makes sense inside its parent mechanism | Duplicating a canonical fact in several articles |
| Glossary-style register item | Brief disambiguation or a stable target that does not yet justify a full article | Publishing thin pages merely to increase entity count |

A proposed satellite becomes an independent entity only when it satisfies at
least two of these tests: readers search for it independently; several pages
need the same explanation; it owns meaningful relations or sources; it needs
more than a compact hub entry; its lifecycle differs from the hub.

## Educational diagram standard

The six supplied vine diagrams are visual references, not factual sources. The
desired family is a schematic-naturalist educational plate:

- botanically credible watercolor or gouache rendering on a calm off-white
  field, with aubergine and muted green as structural colors;
- concrete plant parts, tools and materials rather than abstract decoration;
- restrained arrows, numbered stages, callouts and small comparisons that make
  a process readable in one scan;
- visible nuance where a simplified model could otherwise become misleading;
- one teaching question per plate, with enough quiet space to preserve
  hierarchy at desktop and mobile sizes.

English is the preferred shared language for the few labels that must be baked
into an image. Labels must be short and checked by an editor. The localized
caption and adjacent HTML provide the Dutch explanation or translation and own
all essential teaching content. A reader must not lose the lesson when the
image cannot be seen, enlarged or read. Do not put paragraphs, citations or
complex legends into raster pixels.

Every diagram needs a content brief before production: teaching question,
required objects and relations, prohibited implications, acceptable
simplifications, label list, aspect ratio and narrow-screen plan. Review it for
botanical or process accuracy, scale, accessibility, rights and consistency
with the prose before publication.

## Special content contract: vineyard soils

The roadmap includes one substantial hub provisionally identified as
`concept.vineyard-soils`, under **Wijngaardomgeving en wijnbouwbeslissingen**.
Its purpose is not to assign a taste to every rock. It should let readers reason
from physical and biological properties to vine response, and understand where
evidence ends.

### Required narrative

1. Distinguish soil, subsoil, parent material and bedrock.
2. Explain what roots actually encounter: water, air, temperature, penetrable
   volume, texture, structure, chemistry, nutrients and living organisms.
3. Trace indirect routes from site to vine growth and grape composition,
   especially water availability, drainage and retention, root-zone
   temperature and nutrient availability.
4. Separate geological minerals, plant nutrients, measured elements in wine and
   the sensory metaphor `minerality`.
5. Explain what soil can influence, what usually depends on interacting factors,
   and what cannot responsibly be inferred from a label, photograph or rock
   sample alone.
6. Compare major soil families with a consistent property set rather than a
   list of promised aromas.
7. End with a visual, alphabetical directory that supports scanning and links
   to approved satellites without turning the body into a glossary.

### Candidate alphabetical inventory

The initial research inventory is: alluvium, basalt and other volcanic parent
materials, chalk, clay, flint/chert, granite, gravel, limestone, loam, marl,
sand, schist, shale, silt, slate, terra rossa, tuff and volcanic ash. This is a
research list, not a pre-approved set of entities. `EXP-010` must normalize
overlap, translations and geological versus soil terminology, then apply the
satellite test above.

Photography should show real soil profiles, texture, structure, roots,
drainage, erosion and vineyard-floor conditions with location, scale and
provenance. Diagrams may compare pore space, water movement, heat and root
constraints. A generated close-up may illustrate a model but may not serve as
evidence of a real soil or named place.

The evidence base starts with Alex Maltman's geological critique and broader
soil-terroir and soil-health literature. Maltman is an important corrective,
not a single authority that replaces plant physiology, soil science or
viticultural evidence.

Starting research anchors (to be converted into canonical source records during
`EXP-010`) are:

- Alex Maltman, [_Vineyards, Rocks, and Soils_](https://academic.oup.com/book/40999)
  (Oxford University Press);
- Alex Maltman,
  [“Minerality in Wine: Towards the Reality behind the Myths”](https://www.mdpi.com/2306-5710/4/4/77);
- Cornelis van Leeuwen, Jean-Philippe Roby and Laure de Rességuier,
  [“Soil-related terroir factors: a review”](https://doi.org/10.20870/oeno-one.2018.52.2.2208);
- Robert White,
  [“The Value of Soil Knowledge in Understanding Wine Terroir”](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-science/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2020.00012/full);
  and
- Cristina Lazcano and colleagues,
  [“Defining and Managing for Healthy Vineyard Soils, Intersections With the Concept of Terroir”](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-science/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2020.00068/full).

This list establishes the opening frame, not the final bibliography. `EXP-010`
must add the sources needed for soil physics, plant nutrition, hydrology and
each specific disputed claim.

#### EXP-001 — Audit the existing conceptual corpus

**Status:** complete on 2026-09-24. The maintained result lives in
`explore-foundation-coverage.md`.

**Work:** map every relevant concept, narrative, relation, media asset and
content block to the curriculum spine: vine, environment, decisions,
vinification, composition and sensory development. Record canonical ownership,
depth coverage, source quality, visual gaps and Bordeaux bias.

**Done when:** a maintained coverage matrix names every planned hub and
satellite, its current status, reuse dependencies and priority; no ticket below
depends on memory alone.

#### EXP-002 — Approve the foundation authoring contract

**Depends on:** EXP-001.

**Work:** turn this roadmap into reusable page templates and coverage questions
for system hubs and concept satellites. Define boundaries, depth calibration,
global example selection and the completeness review.

**Done when:** authors can start any following ticket without deciding its
structure from scratch, and validation or review checklists cover the contract.

#### EXP-003 — Pilot the educational diagram contract

**Depends on:** EXP-001–002.

**Done when:** expert, editorial and visual review approve the reusable style;
the media record contains production prompt, sources, rights and review status.

### Phase B — the vine as a living system

#### EXP-004 — Vine anatomy, transport and source–sink relations

**Depends on:** EXP-002–003.

**Coverage:** perennial structure, roots, trunk, shoots, buds, leaves,
inflorescences and berries; xylem, phloem, reserves, photosynthesis,
transpiration and changing sources and sinks. Reconcile rather than duplicate
rootstock and grape entities.

**Visual:** living-vine cutaway plus a focused transport comparison.

**Done when:** a reader can follow water, assimilates and reserves through the
plant and understand the limits of directional-arrow models.

#### EXP-005 — Annual cycle and phenology

**Depends on:** EXP-004.

**Coverage:** dormancy, bleeding, budburst, shoot growth, flowering, fruit set,
berry growth, veraison, ripening, harvest, leaf fall and reserve storage;
variation by hemisphere, climate, cultivar and season.

**Visual:** annual cycle with English phase labels and localized explanation.

**Done when:** the sequence, overlap, timing variability and management stakes
are clear without implying a universal calendar.

#### EXP-006 — Flowering, fruit set and yield formation

**Depends on:** EXP-005.

**Coverage:** flower biology, pollination, fruit set, coulure, millerandage,
cluster architecture, yield components and relevant weather and grower
decisions.

**Done when:** existing draft concepts have been assessed and yield is explained
as a system rather than a single harvest number.

#### EXP-007 — Berry development, veraison and ripeness

**Depends on:** EXP-004–006.

**Coverage:** growth phases, acids, sugars, water, aromas and precursors,
phenolics, seeds and skins; technological, phenolic and aromatic maturity;
sampling and harvest trade-offs.

**Visual:** berry-development sequence plus a multi-variable ripeness model.

**Done when:** veraison is presented as a transition period and ripeness as a
decision across interacting variables, not one reading.

### Phase C — vineyard environment and decisions

#### EXP-008 — Climate, weather, site and microclimate

**Depends on:** EXP-005–007.

**Coverage:** timescales, heat, light, rainfall, humidity, wind, elevation,
aspect, slope, water bodies, canopy and bunch-zone microclimate; measurement
limits and climate-change context.

**Done when:** terms are distinguished and their causal routes to vine response
are globally useful.

#### EXP-009 — Water relations, drought and irrigation

**Depends on:** EXP-004 and EXP-008.

**Coverage:** soil-water storage, roots, transpiration, stomata, water status,
timing and intensity of deficit, excess water, irrigation strategies and legal
or sustainability context.

**Visual:** soil–plant–atmosphere pathway and contrasting water states.

**Done when:** readers can distinguish controlled deficit from harmful stress
without treating less water as automatically better.

#### EXP-010 — Research and model the vineyard-soils hub

**Depends on:** EXP-008–009.

**Work:** build the source set, terminology table and claim matrix; normalize
the candidate soil inventory; decide hub entries versus independent entities;
write the bilingual outline and media shot list.

**Decision gate:** `DEC-EXP-003` approves the satellite list before scaffolding.

**Done when:** every planned comparison uses the same physical-property model,
myth claims have an evidence response and no rock-to-flavour shortcut remains.

#### EXP-011 — Publish the vineyard-soils hub and directory

**Depends on:** EXP-010 and DEC-EXP-003.

**Work:** scaffold approved satellites, write and link the hub, add the
alphabetical image-rich directory, register media and complete NL/EN parity.

**Done when:** the page is useful both as a continuous lesson and as a scanning
reference; every entry has a stable destination or is deliberately hub-owned;
mobile layout, captions, sources and related topics pass review.

#### EXP-012 — Pruning, training, canopy and crop-load decisions

**Depends on:** EXP-004–009.

**Coverage:** winter pruning, training systems, shoot positioning, leaf and
cluster exposure, hedging, leaf removal, thinning, mechanization and trade-offs
among yield, ripening, disease and longevity.

**Done when:** techniques are linked to objectives and constraints rather than
ranked as universally superior.

#### EXP-013 — Nutrition, soil management and vineyard floor

**Depends on:** EXP-009–011.

**Coverage:** essential nutrients, diagnostics, pH and availability, organic
matter, microbiology, tillage, cover crops, compaction, erosion and amendments.

**Done when:** it connects to the soil hub without duplicating geology or making
unsupported microbiome and flavour claims.

#### EXP-014 — Weather hazards, diseases, pests and disorders

**Depends on:** EXP-005–013.

**Coverage:** frost, hail, heat, sunburn, drought, waterlogging, major fungal
and viral threats, insects including phylloxera, integrated management and key
physiological disorders.

**Done when:** existing phylloxera and botrytis material is integrated, hazards
are distinguishable and imagery documents symptoms or mechanisms accurately.

#### EXP-015 — Vineyard systems, sustainability and adaptation

**Depends on:** EXP-008–014.

**Coverage:** conventional, organic, biodynamic and regenerative claims;
inputs, biodiversity, carbon, water, labor, certification, resilience and
adaptation choices.

**Done when:** practices, certifications and outcomes are kept distinct and the
article does not turn contested labels into quality rankings.

### Phase D — vinification foundation

#### EXP-016 — Audit and reframe the existing vinification graph

**Depends on:** EXP-001–002.

**Work:** map the many active method concepts to the production sequence,
identify duplication and gaps, and define the smallest coherent set of hubs.

**Done when:** fermentation, extraction, pressing, MLF, élevage, lees ageing,
clarification, oxidation, sulfur and bottling each have an explicit owner and
place in the system.

#### EXP-017 — Reception, sorting, crushing and must preparation

**Depends on:** EXP-016.

**Coverage:** harvest condition, transport, sorting, destemming, crushing,
whole clusters, pressing order, juice handling, protection and corrective must
treatments with legal context.

**Done when:** red, white and rosé choices are compared without presenting one
universal line.

#### EXP-018 — Yeast, microbiology and alcoholic fermentation

**Depends on:** EXP-016–017.

**Coverage:** yeast ecology, inoculated and spontaneous starts, sugar-to-ethanol
conversion, temperature, nutrients, kinetics, stuck fermentation, by-products
and monitoring.

**Visual:** fermentation process with inputs, transformations and outputs.

**Done when:** the existing fermentation entity is complete enough to support
later method and lesson pages without repeating its core mechanism.

#### EXP-019 — Main production routes, skin contact and extraction

**Depends on:** EXP-017–018.

**Coverage:** red, white, rosé and orange routes; maceration, cap management,
pressing decisions, carbonic-family methods and extraction trade-offs.

**Done when:** shared stages and meaningful divergences are visually and
textually clear, and the draft carbonic-maceration entity has a decision.

#### EXP-020 — Post-fermentation choices, oxygen and maturation

**Depends on:** EXP-018–019.

**Coverage:** MLF, lees, vessels, oak, oxygen exposure, topping, racking,
blending and élevage as a connected decision field.

**Done when:** existing concepts remain canonical and the hub explains their
interaction without promising a fixed sensory result.

#### EXP-021 — Stabilization, clarification, filtration and packaging

**Depends on:** EXP-020.

**Coverage:** microbial, tartrate and protein stability; settling, fining,
filtration, sulfur management, dissolved gases, bottling and closure choices.

**Done when:** risk, intervention and trade-off are understandable and existing
pages are linked rather than copied.

#### EXP-022 — Cellar hygiene, microbial risk and wine faults

**Depends on:** EXP-018–021.

**Coverage:** prevention and diagnosis framework for oxidation, reduction,
volatile acidity, Brettanomyces, cork taint, refermentation and other central
fault families; distinguish fault, style choice and threshold.

**Done when:** readers understand causal routes and controls without using an
aroma checklist as diagnosis.

#### EXP-023 — Sweet, sparkling and fortified method families

**Depends on:** EXP-017–022.

**Coverage:** concentrating or retaining sugar, noble rot and drying, arresting
fermentation, dosage, traditional and tank methods, carbonation, fortification
timing and biological or oxidative maturation where relevant.

**Done when:** method families form a reusable global overview and existing
specialized concepts retain their own depth.

### Phase E — composition, perception and integration

#### EXP-024 — Wine composition as a system

**Depends on:** EXP-007 and EXP-018–023.

**Coverage:** water, ethanol, acids, sugars, phenolics, aroma compounds,
polysaccharides, proteins, minerals and dissolved gases; origin, interaction
and appropriate measurement claims.

**Done when:** acidity, tannin, methoxypyrazines and thiols fit one navigable
model without reducing wine to a flavor-component list.

#### EXP-025 — Sensory perception, balance and bottle development

**Depends on:** EXP-020 and EXP-024.

**Coverage:** taste, aroma, trigeminal sensations, texture, thresholds,
interactions, adaptation, context, balance, evolution and ageing potential.

**Done when:** chemical composition, perception and quality judgement remain
distinct and bottle development builds on rather than duplicates oxidation.

#### EXP-026 — Integrate graph, links, media and terminology

**Depends on:** EXP-004–025.

**Work:** audit reciprocal relations, contextual links, related-topic groups,
media records, captions, terminology, NL/EN parity and search discoverability.

**Done when:** every foundational concept has an intentional place and the user
can move from regions and lessons into the foundation without dead ends.

#### EXP-027 — Global perspective stress test

**Depends on:** EXP-026.

**Work:** test examples and claims against a non-Bordeaux set spanning cool and
warm, humid and arid, Old and New World contexts. Loire is the provisional first

**Done when:** regional assumptions, legal shorthand and climate defaults have
been removed or bounded; examples illuminate rather than dominate.

#### EXP-028 — Integral editorial and product QA

**Depends on:** all prior tickets.

**Work:** run factual, source, completeness, depth, media, accessibility,
responsive, search, link and performance reviews; log findings through the
quality register and maintenance backlog.

**Done when:** checks pass, intentional gaps are documented, the status index is
current and the foundation is ready to support new regions and Learn paths.

## Definition of done for every content ticket

- scoped questions are explicitly answered or logged as gaps;
- knowledge depth has been reviewed against the WSET 2+/3+/4+ calibration
  without treating it as an exam syllabus;
- NL and EN carry equivalent knowledge and block structure;
- sources support the claims at the appropriate level of specificity;
- entity boundaries, links, backlinks and related-topic groups are intentional;
- images have a teaching or documentary purpose, valid rights, media metadata,
  accurate captions and useful alternative text;
- diagrams have a factual brief and accessible HTML equivalent;
- `content:check`, link audit and relevant application tests pass;
- mobile and desktop presentation have been visually reviewed; and
- status, audit findings and follow-up actions are recorded in their canonical
  documents.
