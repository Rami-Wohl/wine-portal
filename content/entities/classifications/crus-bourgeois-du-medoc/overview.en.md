:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.crus-bourgeois-medoc-classification-2025 source.crus-bourgeois-medoc-press-kit-2025"}
The **Crus Bourgeois du Médoc** are a periodically revised classification for red wine from selected [[concept.chateau|estates]] in eight [[region.medoc|Médoc]] appellations. The 2025 edition contains 170 châteaux and applies to the 2023 through 2027 vintages. It has three tiers: *Cru Bourgeois*, *Cru Bourgeois Supérieur* and *Cru Bourgeois Exceptionnel*. [@source.crus-bourgeois-medoc-classification-2025]

This makes the designation more current than the nearly fixed [[classification.bordeaux-1855|1855 Classification]], but not timeless. Estate, tier, vintage and applicable edition belong together on a bottle.
:::

:::section{#reikwijdte depth="foundation" source_refs="source.crus-bourgeois-medoc-classification-2025"}
## Scope and structure — three tiers across eight appellations

The classification can include red wine from [[appellation.medoc|Médoc]], [[appellation.haut-medoc|Haut-Médoc]], [[appellation.listrac-medoc|Listrac-Médoc]], [[appellation.moulis-en-medoc|Moulis-en-Médoc]], [[appellation.margaux|Margaux]], [[appellation.pauillac|Pauillac]], [[appellation.saint-estephe|Saint-Estèphe]], and [[appellation.saint-julien|Saint-Julien]]. It replaces none of those protected origins: the appellation states where and under which production rules the wine was made; Cru Bourgeois says that the estate was placed within a dated edition of this additional system.

The three tiers do form an internal hierarchy. *Supérieur* and *Exceptionnel* are therefore not free-standing advertising terms, but levels above the base category within the same edition.
:::

:::detail{#editie-2025 parent="reikwijdte" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.crus-bourgeois-medoc-classification-2025"}
### The 2025 edition in numbers

Of the 170 included estates, 120 are Cru Bourgeois, 36 Cru Bourgeois Supérieur and 14 Cru Bourgeois Exceptionnel. Distribution by appellation is uneven: 91 are in Médoc, 53 in Haut-Médoc, 7 in Listrac-Médoc, 5 in Moulis-en-Médoc, 4 in Margaux, 1 in Pauillac and 9 in Saint-Estèphe. No Saint-Julien estate was included in this edition. [@source.crus-bourgeois-medoc-classification-2025]

That zero does not make Saint-Julien “lower”, nor do the large totals make Médoc automatically “better”. The figures only describe which applicants entered this edition.
:::

## History — from trade designation to revisable classification

The name looks back to a broad 1932 list naming 444 Médoc estates as crus bourgeois. That list became influential, but it was not the same as today's legally framed and periodically controlled classification. After several institutional forms, the system regained a three-tier hierarchy and five-year cycle in 2020. The 2025 edition is the second under that model. [@source.crus-bourgeois-medoc-specification]
:::

:::detail{#breuk-van-2007 parent="geschiedenis" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.crus-bourgeois-medoc-specification"}
### Why the history is not one continuous list

An official 2003 classification divided 247 estates into three tiers, but an administrative court annulled it in 2007. Annual recognition without the same hierarchy followed. Only the five-year model introduced in 2020 restored the three tiers. [@source.crus-bourgeois-medoc-specification]

An old designation must therefore not be silently treated as current status. “Cru Bourgeois” on a bottle from another period belongs to the rules and list then in force.
:::

## Assessment — more than one successful tasting

The procedure combines tasting with estate requirements. Wines are assessed blind across several vintages so that one exceptional year does not carry the entire result. Traceability, presentation, business practice and environmental certification are among the additional factors. An independent control body oversees parts of admission and follow-up. [@source.crus-bourgeois-medoc-press-kit-2025]

The classification thus seeks to recognise consistency and a verifiable production base. It does not say that all wines within one tier taste alike: appellation, soil, grapes, blend and estate choices remain evident in the glass.
:::

:::detail{#grenzen-van-de-methode parent="beoordeling" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.crus-bourgeois-medoc-press-kit-2025"}
### Criteria answer their own question

Blind tasting limits the influence of a famous name, while assessment over several years reduces chance. Non-sensory criteria then examine whether the estate meets the system's wider conditions. That is stronger than a one-off medal, but still no natural law of quality.

The organising alliance describes the system through its own objectives. Claims about guarantees, value or excellence should therefore be read as institutional positioning; the verifiable facts are the rules, procedure, edition and list.
:::

:::section{#etiket-lezen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.crus-bourgeois-medoc-classification-2025 source.crus-bourgeois-medoc-press-kit-2025"}
## Label and use — start with the vintage

When you see *Cru Bourgeois*, *Supérieur* or *Exceptionnel*, check the [[concept.vintage|vintage]] first. The current edition applies to 2023–2027; an older bottle may have been released under an earlier recognition. Then read the full château name and appellation. This prevents a changing status from being treated as though it belonged to a building forever.

Use the classification as a discovery route towards lesser-known Médoc estates, not as an automatic buying decision. An unlisted château may not have applied, may not have qualified, or may fall outside the relevant edition; absence alone does not prove inferior wine.
:::

:::detail{#naast-andere-systemen parent="etiket-lezen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.crus-bourgeois-medoc-classification-2025"}
### Do not confuse it with 1855 or Cru Artisan

The 1855 Classification is a nearly frozen historical ranking of a much smaller group of estates. [[classification.crus-artisans-du-medoc|Crus Artisans du Médoc]] is a separate five-year category centred on the direct role of the owner-operator and has no three-tier hierarchy. The names coexist in the same region, but answer different questions.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Cru Bourgeois is a dated, revisable classification of red Médoc wines. Always read tier, château, appellation and vintage together.
:::
