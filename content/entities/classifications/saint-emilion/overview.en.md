:::summary{#wat-is-de-classificatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022 source.saint-emilion-grand-cru-specification-2023"}
The **Saint-Émilion Classification** is a periodically revised ranking of selected wine estates within [[appellation.saint-emilion-grand-cru|Saint-Émilion Grand Cru]]. It has two principal levels: *Premier Grand Cru Classé* and *Grand Cru Classé*. The distinction A may also be awarded within the premier tier.

The classification is not an appellation, and “Grand Cru” on its own is not a rank. A wine may be Saint-Émilion Grand Cru without being classified. Only the full additions *Grand Cru Classé* or *Premier Grand Cru Classé* refer to this list and its applicable edition.
:::

:::section{#geschiedenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022 source.saint-emilion-grand-cru-specification-2023"}
## History — a ranking that was never meant to stand still

The first classification appeared in 1955 and was supplemented in 1958. Unlike [[classification.bordeaux-1855|Bordeaux 1855]], its founding principle was that the list could be reassessed. An edition is formally valid for ten years, although historically the revisions have not always followed a perfectly regular rhythm. [@source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022]

That makes the system unusually alive. A château can enter, rise, fall or disappear from a new edition. The ranking can therefore move with producers and their assessment, but every revision also brings substantial reputational and economic interests into the room.
:::

:::detail{#waarom-herzien parent="geschiedenis" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### Keeping current comes at a price

A revisable list can recognize changes that a static classification misses: newly achieved quality, greater consistency or an estate that no longer convincingly performs at its former level. At the same time, every method of measurement becomes part of the debate. How much weight should go to blind tasting, reputation, visitor facilities, vineyard holdings or business management?

The classification is therefore both vinous and institutional. It attempts to order present-day excellence, but does so through predefined criteria and a formal procedure — never through a single universal glass of wine.
:::

:::detail{#edities-zijn-tijdlagen parent="geschiedenis" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### An old bottle belongs to the history of its own edition

A château may have a different formal position in 1955, 1986, 2012 and 2022. Historical labels and descriptions must therefore be read against the classification that applied at the time. The current list cannot be projected backwards onto older wine.

In the knowledge graph, the current relationships carry `valid_from: 2022`. A later edition should add new time-bound relationships rather than overwrite the history.
:::

:::section{#reikwijdte depth="foundation" source_refs="source.saint-emilion-grand-cru-specification-2023"}
## Scope — one classification within one appellation

The ranking belongs exclusively to Saint-Émilion Grand Cru. It classifies wine estates, not communes, individual soil types or all of [[appellation.saint-emilion|Saint-Émilion]]. A classified growth must first meet every production condition of Grand Cru. [@source.saint-emilion-grand-cru-specification-2023]

The logic therefore runs from broad to narrow: [[region.bordeaux|Bordeaux]] is the region, Saint-Émilion Grand Cru the protected origin, and the classification an additional rank for selected estates within it.
:::

:::detail{#drie-termen parent="reikwijdte" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.saint-emilion-grand-cru-specification-2023"}
### Saint-Émilion, Grand Cru and Classé

- **Saint-Émilion** is a distinct appellation for red wine.
- **Saint-Émilion Grand Cru** is a second appellation covering the same geographical area, with stricter production rules.
- **Grand Cru Classé** and **Premier Grand Cru Classé** are classification ranks within that second appellation.

In other wine regions, *grand cru* may mean something else again. The complete local name is therefore more informative than the prestigious phrase in isolation.
:::

:::section{#beoordeling depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
## Assessment — wine quality and the estate behind it

For the 2022 edition, a classification commission assessed applicants with support from an independent certification body and INAO. The principal criterion was the quality and consistency of the wine, established through tasting; it accounted for fifty percent of the final score at both ranking levels. [@source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022]

Reputation, the characterization of the estate — including its land base — and the management of vineyard and cellar also counted. The precise analysis and weighting differed according to the rank sought.
:::

:::detail{#vier-criteria parent="beoordeling" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### Four lenses on sustained performance

The four criteria try to answer a question that one tasting cannot settle: can an estate maintain a recognizable high standard over time? Tasting examines the wine; consistency looks across years; land and management investigate the production base; reputation measures the public standing an estate has built.

This combination makes the classification broader than a wine competition. It also makes it debatable, because reasonable people may disagree about which non-sensory factors truly belong in a wine ranking.
:::

:::detail{#procedure-en-onafhankelijkheid parent="beoordeling" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### Procedure is part of credibility

For 2022, INAO appointed a commission of seven members. The work took almost two years, and Bureau Veritas Certification France was engaged as an external body through a public tender. [@source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022]

Such safeguards make an outcome more auditable, but they do not remove interpretation. Being transparent about criteria, edition and formal status is therefore stronger than simply declaring that a château is “among the best”.
:::

:::section{#opbouw depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
## Structure — fourteen Premiers and a broad group of Grands Crus Classés

The 2022 edition contains **14 Premiers Grands Crus Classés**, **2 of them with distinction A**, and **71 Grands Crus Classés**: 85 classified growths in total. Château Figeac and Château Pavie hold the A distinction. [@source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022]

The other twelve premiers do not form an official sub-list labelled “B” in the published edition. Read A as an additional distinction within the premier tier, therefore, not as proof that every other premier must display “B” on its label.

:::section{#premier-profielen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
## The 2022 Premiers — places and people behind the rank

Of the fourteen Premiers, [[producer.chateau-canon|Château Canon]], [[producer.chateau-figeac|Château Figeac]] and [[producer.chateau-pavie|Château Pavie]] have their own articles. The other eleven appear below as short profiles. This is the official 2022 cohort, not a new ranking by our own taste. [@source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022]
:::

:::detail{#producent-chateau-beau-sejour-becot parent="premier-profielen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.saint-emilion-beau-sejour-becot source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### Château Beau-Séjour Bécot

[[producer.chateau-beau-sejour-becot|Château Beau-Séjour Bécot]] lies west of the village on the Saint-Martin de Mazerat limestone plateau. Michel Bécot bought the estate in 1969 and gave it the family name now carried by the [[concept.grand-vin|Grand Vin]]. Beneath the vines, he had old limestone quarries turned into a cool place to store bottles. The site thus has a tangible family history as well as a geological one. [@source.saint-emilion-beau-sejour-becot]

Limestone affects such things as water availability and ripening; it does not impart a recognizable flavour directly to wine. Beau-Séjour Bécot is therefore instructive as a plateau estate where ground, old underground spaces and successive cellar decisions meet. The present family built on a growth that was already listed as a Premier in 1955, yet a bottle from that year and one under the 2022 edition belong to different chapters of the story. The rank provides context, not a fixed tasting recipe. [@source.saint-emilion-beau-sejour-becot] [@source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022]
:::

:::detail{#producent-chateau-beausejour-duffau-lagarrosse parent="premier-profielen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.saint-emilion-beausejour-duffau-lagarrosse source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### Château Beauséjour Duffau-Lagarrosse

[[producer.chateau-beausejour-duffau-lagarrosse|Château Beauséjour Duffau-Lagarrosse]] is a small estate on the limestone slope south of the historic village. Its family history runs unusually deep: the château records vines here from the fifteenth century and connects its name with successive Duffau-Lagarrosse generations. The [[concept.grand-vin|Grand Vin]] shows how a small site can nevertheless occupy a distinctive place in a large classification. [@source.saint-emilion-beausejour-duffau-lagarrosse]

The slope matters for more than its view. Elevation, exposure and soil structure mean that parcels respond to rain and heat differently from flatter vineyards. That helps explain why the estate is often associated with freshness and precision, though such a style description is an expectation, not a promise for every vintage. Compare it with other Premiers on the plateau and at the foot of the slope: the same formal rank can embrace very different stories rooted in place. [@source.saint-emilion-beausejour-duffau-lagarrosse]
:::

:::detail{#producent-chateau-belair-monange parent="premier-profielen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.saint-emilion-belair-monange source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### Château Bélair-Monange

At [[producer.chateau-belair-monange|Château Bélair-Monange]], an old site meets a comparatively new name. The estate lies close to the historic centre on the limestone plateau. The Moueix family acquired Bélair in 2008, added Monange in tribute to a family member, and united the property with neighbouring Magdelaine in 2012. Its present identity is thus also the result of a deliberate reshaping of two known estates. [@source.saint-emilion-belair-monange]

The [[concept.grand-vin|Grand Vin]] bears the Bélair-Monange name. Its site and origin are more informative than an unchanging tasting label: plateau, slope and human selection together shape what reaches the bottle. The profile also prevents a common misunderstanding. A name in a classification need not describe a business unchanged for centuries; parcels, ownership and trade names can move while the place itself remains recognizable. Read historical bottles and the 2022 edition within their respective periods. [@source.saint-emilion-belair-monange]
:::

:::detail{#producent-chateau-canon-la-gaffeliere parent="premier-profielen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.saint-emilion-canon-la-gaffeliere source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### Château Canon la Gaffelière

[[producer.chateau-canon-la-gaffeliere|Château Canon la Gaffelière]] lies south of the village, at the foot of the slope. Clay-limestone and clay-sand soils meet here. The estate itself stresses the unusually important role of cabernet alongside [[grape.merlot|merlot]], making it a useful counterexample to the simplified notion that Saint-Émilion is only about [[grape.merlot|merlot]]. [@source.saint-emilion-canon-la-gaffeliere]

The von Neipperg family acquired the château in 1971. Stephan von Neipperg later took charge and associated the estate with parcel-by-parcel and ecological work. The [[concept.grand-vin|Grand Vin]] is the best way to follow that combination of site, grapes and decisions. Descriptions such as elegance or complexity remain the producer's language, however: soil and grape variety cannot predict an exact taste. It is more useful to compare this wine with its neighbour [[producer.la-mondotte|La Mondotte]], also linked to the family but rooted in a different site. [@source.saint-emilion-canon-la-gaffeliere]
:::

:::detail{#producent-clos-fourtet parent="premier-profielen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.saint-emilion-clos-fourtet source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### Clos Fourtet

[[producer.clos-fourtet|Clos Fourtet]] almost touches Saint-Émilion's old town walls. Its enclosed vineyard on the western limestone plateau makes the word *clos* unusually tangible: a historic boundary is part of the estate's identity. The Cuvelier family acquired the property in 2001. Its [[concept.grand-vin|Grand Vin]], Clos Fourtet, is the reference point for the château, rather than the medieval scenery. [@source.saint-emilion-clos-fourtet]

The setting invites care in separating landscape from wine. A clearly bounded vineyard can still contain different parcels and harvest decisions; limestone explains part of the growing conditions but cannot, on its own, account for aroma, structure or quality. Precisely because its position is so recognizable, Clos Fourtet is useful for learning about place without reducing it to a tasting formula. The 2022 classification places it among the Premiers alongside estates with very different histories and soils. [@source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022]
:::

:::detail{#producent-chateau-larcis-ducasse parent="premier-profielen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.saint-emilion-larcis-ducasse source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### Château Larcis Ducasse

[[producer.chateau-larcis-ducasse|Château Larcis Ducasse]] sits on Saint-Émilion's southern slope, where terraces and limestone-rich soils shape the vineyard. The Raba family bought the estate in 1893. The château describes how the family preserved the terraces and a gravity-fed cellar; this is a story of continuity, not merely prestige. [@source.saint-emilion-larcis-ducasse]

The [[concept.grand-vin|Grand Vin]] connects that hillside with decisions in the vineyard and cellar. Terracing matters for drainage, exposure and vineyard work, but here too a wine is not made directly by a single geological word. What makes Larcis Ducasse instructive is the interaction between a small site and long-term stewardship. Compare a bottle with wines from the flatter plateau: any differences need to be tested in a particular vintage and against producer choices, not read in advance from a map. Its premier status belongs to the 2022 edition. [@source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022]
:::

:::detail{#producent-la-mondotte parent="premier-profielen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.saint-emilion-la-mondotte source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### La Mondotte

[[producer.la-mondotte|La Mondotte]] is the von Neipperg family's small plateau holding east of Saint-Émilion village. The château describes roughly 4.5 hectares of clay-limestone soils, planted predominantly with [[grape.merlot|merlot]] alongside [[grape.cabernet-franc|cabernet franc]]. The family acquired it in 1971; La Mondotte later became part of its wider, parcel-focused work in the area. [@source.saint-emilion-la-mondotte]

Its [[concept.grand-vin|Grand Vin]] is a useful comparison with [[producer.chateau-canon-la-gaffeliere|Canon la Gaffelière]]. Both names belong to the same family and appear as Premiers in the 2022 edition, yet their positions and soils differ. This helps keep *producer* and *[[concept.terroir|terroir]]* from becoming synonyms. The producer uses terms such as power and minerality for La Mondotte; treat those as style language, not a measurable consequence of limestone. The actual bottle still depends on vintage, grape selection and winemaking. [@source.saint-emilion-la-mondotte]
:::

:::detail{#producent-chateau-pavie-macquin parent="premier-profielen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.saint-emilion-pavie-macquin source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### Château Pavie Macquin

[[producer.chateau-pavie-macquin|Château Pavie Macquin]] lies on limestone-rich plateau and its edges around Pavie, southeast of the village. The estate bears the name of Albert Macquin, who played an important part in replanting French vineyards after [[concept.phylloxera|phylloxera]]. The château thus connects a local site with a much larger chapter in European winegrowing history. [@source.saint-emilion-pavie-macquin]

Its [[concept.grand-vin|Grand Vin]] comes from vineyards where clay and limestone together influence growing conditions. The producer emphasizes a balance between ripe fruit and freshness; that is a recognizable style image, not an outcome that every vintage must deliver. Pavie Macquin is especially instructive because its name points to a historical figure while the glass is shaped by present-day choices. Confusion with [[producer.chateau-pavie|Château Pavie]] is easy, but they are distinct estates and distinct producer records in the 2022 classification. [@source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022]
:::

:::detail{#producent-chateau-trolong-mondot parent="premier-profielen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.saint-emilion-troplong-mondot source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### Château Troplong Mondot

[[producer.chateau-trolong-mondot|Château Troplong Mondot]] occupies one of the higher parts of Saint-Émilion. The estate describes an extensive vineyard with several soil types, making it a useful reminder that one château need not mean one uniform [[concept.terroir|terroir]] zone. The [[concept.grand-vin|Grand Vin]] is its central wine. The estate has belonged to SCOR since 2017; the château presents this period as the continuation of a much older vineyard, not as a fresh start. [@source.saint-emilion-troplong-mondot]

The vines extend around the hill, with differences in elevation and exposure to sunlight. Understanding and combining individual parcels is therefore a substantial part of its winemaking choices. This variation does not fix an unchanging taste profile; it makes Troplong Mondot particularly interesting to compare across vintages. Its premier status in 2022 says something about the estate's formal assessment, not that altitude automatically brings a higher rank or a better wine. [@source.saint-emilion-troplong-mondot] [@source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022]
:::

:::detail{#producent-chateau-trottevieille parent="premier-profielen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.saint-emilion-trottevieille source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### Château TrotteVieille

[[producer.chateau-trottevieille|Château TrotteVieille]] lies east of the village on the limestone plateau. Marcel Borie bought the estate in 1947; it already belonged to the Premiers in the first classification of 1955. That long presence makes it a useful reference point for the system's history, without implying that a rank has been guaranteed unchanged since then. [@source.saint-emilion-trottevieille]

[[grape.cabernet-franc|Cabernet franc]] plays an unusually substantial role alongside [[grape.merlot|merlot]] in the [[concept.grand-vin|Grand Vin]]. TrotteVieille thus challenges the idea of a Saint-Émilion made from just one grape. [[concept.assemblage|Blending]] proportions and élevage can change from year to year, so treat any general character description as orientation. It is more interesting to read the wine as the result of a longstanding site, historical continuity and present-day selection. The château still appears as a Premier Grand Cru Classé in the published 2022 edition. [@source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022]
:::

:::detail{#producent-chateau-valandraud parent="premier-profielen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.saint-emilion-valandraud source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### Château Valandraud

[[producer.chateau-valandraud|Château Valandraud]] began in 1991 with a plot of about 0.6 hectares bought by Murielle Andraud and Jean-Luc Thunevin. From that modest start it became one of the best-known examples of a so-called *garage wine*: a small, deliberately ambitious wine that attracted attention outside the traditional château hierarchy. The present estate is based around Saint-Étienne-de-Lisse; the [[concept.grand-vin|Grand Vin]] still bears the original name. [@source.saint-emilion-valandraud]

Valandraud's rise tells a different story from that of an enclosed historic vineyard or a centuries-old family estate. It shows how new producers, investment, style and attention could change Saint-Émilion's reputation. Its premier status in 2022 is a dated formal recognition, not a definition of the wine. Since ownership and the team later changed, Andraud and Thunevin are described here as founders, not assumed to be the current owners. [@source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022]
:::

:::section{#betekenis-en-grenzen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
## Meaning and limits — influential, but never the whole glass

A rank says that an estate met the system's requirements in a particular edition. That is meaningful information about consistency, production base and reputation. It does not say that every vintage or bottle will taste better than every unclassified wine.

Producer, site, blend, [[concept.vintage|vintage]], cellar choices and bottle condition remain separate layers of information. The classification orders estates; it does not replace critical observation.
:::

:::detail{#beroemde-afwezigen parent="betekenis-en-grenzen" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.decanter-cheval-blanc-ausone-withdrawal-2021 source.chateau-angelus-withdrawal-2022 source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### Not taking part is not the same as being demoted

[[producer.chateau-cheval-blanc|Château Cheval Blanc]] and [[producer.chateau-ausone|Château Ausone]] were historically Premiers Grands Crus Classés A, but did not apply in 2022 because they did not recognize themselves in the criteria. [@source.decanter-cheval-blanc-ausone-withdrawal-2021]

[[producer.chateau-angelus|Château Angélus]], which had been promoted to category A in 2012, also withdrew its application in early 2022. It likewise does not appear on the current list. [@source.chateau-angelus-withdrawal-2022]

Their absence from the list does not mean that a jury demoted their wines in 2022. It does show that a voluntary, revisable classification also depends on participation, trust and agreement about what it measures.
:::

:::section{#etiket-en-gebruik depth="foundation" source_refs="source.saint-emilion-grand-cru-specification-2023 source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
## Label and use — rank has a full name and a date

On a label, first look for **Saint-Émilion Grand Cru**. *Grand Cru Classé* or *Premier Grand Cru Classé* may then state the formal rank. Historical and current claims must finally be tied to the edition in which the château held that status.

Use the rank as a route into producer knowledge, not as an automatic buying decision. When assessing a particular bottle, also read château, [[concept.cuvee|cuvée]], vintage, bottler, storage and condition.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
The Saint-Émilion Classification is valuable precisely because it can change. Read it as a dated, formal assessment of participating estates — not as an eternal definition of the best wine.
:::

:::caveat{#geen-smaakgarantie depth="advanced" variant="simplification" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
A higher or current rank does not guarantee preference in the glass. The classification assesses according to its own criteria; your bottle still depends on the wine, year, development and storage.
:::
