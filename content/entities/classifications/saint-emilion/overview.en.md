:::summary{#wat-is-de-classificatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022 source.saint-emilion-grand-cru-specification-2023"}
The **Saint-Émilion Classification** is a periodically revised ranking of selected wine estates within [[appellation.saint-emilion-grand-cru|Saint-Émilion Grand Cru]]. It has two principal levels: *Premier Grand Cru Classé* and *Grand Cru Classé*. The distinction A may also be awarded within the premier tier.

The classification is not an appellation, and “Grand Cru” on its own is not a rank. A wine may be Saint-Émilion Grand Cru without being classified. Only the full additions *Grand Cru Classé* or *Premier Grand Cru Classé* refer to this list and its applicable edition.
:::

:::section{#geschiedenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022 source.saint-emilion-grand-cru-specification-2023"}
## History — a ranking that was never meant to stand still

The first classification appeared in 1955 and was supplemented in 1958. Unlike [[classification.bordeaux-1855|Bordeaux 1855]], its founding principle was that the list could be reassessed. An edition is formally valid for ten years, although historically the revisions have not always followed a perfectly regular rhythm. [@source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022]

That makes the system unusually alive. A château can enter, rise, fall or disappear from a new edition. The ranking can therefore move with producers and their assessment, but every revision also brings substantial reputational and economic interests into the room.
:::

:::detail{#waarom-herzien parent="geschiedenis" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### Keeping current comes at a price

A revisable list can recognize changes that a static classification misses: newly achieved quality, greater consistency or an estate that no longer convincingly performs at its former level. At the same time, every method of measurement becomes part of the debate. How much weight should go to blind tasting, reputation, visitor facilities, vineyard holdings or business management?

The classification is therefore both vinous and institutional. It attempts to order present-day excellence, but does so through predefined criteria and a formal procedure — never through a single universal glass of wine.
:::

:::detail{#edities-zijn-tijdlagen parent="geschiedenis" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### An old bottle belongs to the history of its own edition

A château may have a different formal position in 1955, 1986, 2012 and 2022. Historical labels and descriptions must therefore be read against the classification that applied at the time. The current list cannot be projected backwards onto older wine.

In the knowledge graph, the current relationships carry `valid_from: 2022`. A later edition should add new time-bound relationships rather than overwrite the history.
:::

:::section{#reikwijdte depth="foundation" source_refs="source.saint-emilion-grand-cru-specification-2023"}
## Scope — one classification within one appellation

The ranking belongs exclusively to Saint-Émilion Grand Cru. It classifies wine estates, not communes, individual soil types or all of [[appellation.saint-emilion|Saint-Émilion]]. A classified growth must first meet every production condition of Grand Cru. [@source.saint-emilion-grand-cru-specification-2023]

The logic therefore runs from broad to narrow: [[region.bordeaux|Bordeaux]] is the region, Saint-Émilion Grand Cru the protected origin, and the classification an additional rank for selected estates within it.
:::

:::detail{#drie-termen parent="reikwijdte" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.saint-emilion-grand-cru-specification-2023"}
### Saint-Émilion, Grand Cru and Classé

- **Saint-Émilion** is a distinct appellation for red wine.
- **Saint-Émilion Grand Cru** is a second appellation covering the same geographical area, with stricter production rules.
- **Grand Cru Classé** and **Premier Grand Cru Classé** are classification ranks within that second appellation.

In other wine regions, *grand cru* may mean something else again. The complete local name is therefore more informative than the prestigious phrase in isolation.
:::

:::section{#beoordeling depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
## Assessment — wine quality and the estate behind it

For the 2022 edition, a classification commission assessed applicants with support from an independent certification body and INAO. The principal criterion was the quality and consistency of the wine, established through tasting; it accounted for fifty percent of the final score at both ranking levels. [@source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022]

Reputation, the characterization of the estate — including its land base — and the management of vineyard and cellar also counted. The precise analysis and weighting differed according to the rank sought.
:::

:::detail{#vier-criteria parent="beoordeling" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### Four lenses on sustained performance

The four criteria try to answer a question that one tasting cannot settle: can an estate maintain a recognizable high standard over time? Tasting examines the wine; consistency looks across years; land and management investigate the production base; reputation measures the public standing an estate has built.

This combination makes the classification broader than a wine competition. It also makes it debatable, because reasonable people may disagree about which non-sensory factors truly belong in a wine ranking.
:::

:::detail{#procedure-en-onafhankelijkheid parent="beoordeling" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### Procedure is part of credibility

For 2022, INAO appointed a commission of seven members. The work took almost two years, and Bureau Veritas Certification France was engaged as an external body through a public tender. [@source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022]

Such safeguards make an outcome more auditable, but they do not remove interpretation. Being transparent about criteria, edition and formal status is therefore stronger than simply declaring that a château is “among the best”.
:::

:::section{#opbouw depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
## Structure — fourteen Premiers and a broad group of Grands Crus Classés

The 2022 edition contains **14 Premiers Grands Crus Classés**, **2 of them with distinction A**, and **71 Grands Crus Classés**: 85 classified growths in total. Château Figeac and Château Pavie hold the A distinction. [@source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022]

The other twelve premiers do not form an official sub-list labelled “B” in the published edition. Read A as an additional distinction within the premier tier, therefore, not as proof that every other premier must display “B” on its label.
:::

## Meaning and limits — influential, but never the whole glass

A rank says that an estate met the system's requirements in a particular edition. That is meaningful information about consistency, production base and reputation. It does not say that every vintage or bottle will taste better than every unclassified wine.

Producer, site, blend, [[concept.vintage|vintage]], cellar choices and bottle condition remain separate layers of information. The classification orders estates; it does not replace critical observation.
:::

:::detail{#beroemde-afwezigen parent="betekenis-en-grenzen" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.decanter-cheval-blanc-ausone-withdrawal-2021 source.chateau-angelus-withdrawal-2022 source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### Not taking part is not the same as being demoted

[[producer.chateau-cheval-blanc|Château Cheval Blanc]] and [[producer.chateau-ausone|Château Ausone]] were historically Premiers Grands Crus Classés A, but did not apply in 2022 because they did not recognize themselves in the criteria. [@source.decanter-cheval-blanc-ausone-withdrawal-2021]

[[producer.chateau-angelus|Château Angélus]], which had been promoted to category A in 2012, also withdrew its application in early 2022. It likewise does not appear on the current list. [@source.chateau-angelus-withdrawal-2022]

Their absence from the list does not mean that a jury demoted their wines in 2022. It does show that a voluntary, revisable classification also depends on participation, trust and agreement about what it measures.
:::

:::section{#etiket-en-gebruik depth="foundation" source_refs="source.saint-emilion-grand-cru-specification-2023 source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
## Label and use — rank has a full name and a date

On a label, first look for **Saint-Émilion Grand Cru**. *Grand Cru Classé* or *Premier Grand Cru Classé* may then state the formal rank. Historical and current claims must finally be tied to the edition in which the château held that status.

Use the rank as a route into producer knowledge, not as an automatic buying decision. When assessing a particular bottle, also read château, [[concept.cuvee|cuvée]], vintage, bottler, storage and condition.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
The Saint-Émilion Classification is valuable precisely because it can change. Read it as a dated, formal assessment of participating estates — not as an eternal definition of the best wine.
:::

:::caveat{#geen-smaakgarantie depth="advanced" variant="simplification" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
A higher or current rank does not guarantee preference in the glass. The classification assesses according to its own criteria; your bottle still depends on the wine, year, development and storage.
:::
