:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.bordeaux-crus-artisans-overview source.legifrance-crus-artisans-order-2022"}
The **Crus Artisans du Médoc** form a single official, periodically revised category for red wine from Médoc estates where the owner-operator remains directly involved throughout the business. The current classification contains 33 businesses and applies to the five vintages from 2022 through 2026. [@source.bordeaux-crus-artisans-overview]

Here, *artisan* does not simply mean “small”, “old-fashioned” or “made by hand”. It identifies an organised model in which the winegrower is present from vineyard and winemaking through bottling, sales and management.
:::

:::section{#reikwijdte depth="foundation" source_refs="source.bordeaux-crus-artisans-overview source.legifrance-crus-artisans-order-2022"}
## Scope and structure — one category, not a ladder

A Cru Artisan can come from [[appellation.medoc|Médoc]], [[appellation.haut-medoc|Haut-Médoc]], [[appellation.listrac-medoc|Listrac-Médoc]], [[appellation.moulis-en-medoc|Moulis-en-Médoc]], [[appellation.margaux|Margaux]], [[appellation.pauillac|Pauillac]], [[appellation.saint-estephe|Saint-Estèphe]], or [[appellation.saint-julien|Saint-Julien]]. Only red wine falls within the system. [@source.bordeaux-crus-artisans-overview]

Unlike [[classification.crus-bourgeois-du-medoc|Crus Bourgeois]], it has no tiers such as Supérieur or Exceptionnel. Every admitted business carries the same classification name. The appellation remains the protected origin alongside it; Cru Artisan is an additional, time-bound recognition.
:::

:::detail{#de-mens-in-het-bedrijf parent="reikwijdte" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.bordeaux-crus-artisans-overview"}
### The owner remains close to every stage

The distinctive idea is not that every action is literally performed by hand. Modern equipment, outside expertise or employees do not preclude craftsmanship. What matters is the owner's central, active role: managing the business and taking part in grape growing, winemaking, bottling and sales. [@source.bordeaux-crus-artisans-overview]

Cru Artisan therefore speaks primarily about scale and organisation. It imposes no uniform grape blend, cellar style or flavour beyond the rules of the relevant appellation.
:::

## History — an old name organised anew

The term appeared as early as 1868 in the Cocks & Féret wine guide. It referred to craftspeople in the Médoc countryside — coopers, blacksmiths or bakers, for example — who also tended vines of their own. The designation faded from view amid the economic disruption of the first half of the twentieth century. [@source.bordeaux-crus-artisans-overview]

Winegrowers formed a new association in 1989. European recognition for use on labels followed in 1994, and the first modern classification appeared in 2006. Under rules introduced in 2017, the list is revised every five years.
:::

:::detail{#van-beroep-naar-voorwaarde parent="geschiedenis" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.bordeaux-crus-artisans-overview source.legifrance-crus-artisans-order-2022"}
### A historical word acquired a current definition

In the nineteenth century, *artisan* chiefly described the social reality of people combining a trade with a small vineyard. The present classification uses the word as a controlled eligibility condition for a wine business. Those meanings are related, but not identical.

In 2022 the French state approved the current specifications and control plan. The designation is therefore more than an informal club name, without becoming an appellation or a ranking of every Médoc wine. [@source.legifrance-crus-artisans-order-2022]
:::

:::section{#controle-en-editie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.legifrance-crus-artisans-order-2022 source.bordeaux-crus-artisans-overview"}
## Control and edition — recognition with an end date

The official rules determine who may apply and how compliance is checked. The 2022–2026 edition deliberately links status to five harvests. A business included on an older list is therefore not automatically a Cru Artisan forever; each new edition must be read and recorded again. [@source.legifrance-crus-artisans-order-2022]

Periodic revision keeps the list close to current business practice. It remains an assessment against specific criteria, however, not a universal measure of what every drinker will enjoy most.
:::

:::detail{#wat-de-status-niet-zegt parent="controle-en-editie" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.bordeaux-crus-artisans-overview source.legifrance-crus-artisans-order-2022"}
### Craft is not a flavour profile

An owner's direct role may produce short lines of decision and a recognisable business identity, but it does not automatically produce one wine style. Soil, location, [[concept.vintage|vintage]], grapes, selection and cellar choices differ by estate.

Nor are “family-run” or “modest in size” independent proof of quality. The classification says that a business met its admission and control conditions in the applicable edition; the particular bottle still needs its own context.
:::

:::section{#etiket-lezen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.bordeaux-crus-artisans-overview source.legifrance-crus-artisans-order-2022"}
## Label and use — read name, origin and year together

Begin with the full [[concept.chateau|estate]] name, appellation and vintage. Then check whether *Cru Artisan* applied to that vintage. For 2022 through 2026, the current list is the appropriate reference; older and future bottles require their own edition.

Do not confuse the term with the five ranks of 1855 or the three Crus Bourgeois tiers. Cru Artisan primarily describes how a selected Médoc business is organised and controlled. That makes it a useful discovery route beyond the most famous château names.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Cru Artisan is one time-bound category for red Médoc wine, centred on the actively involved owner-operator. It is neither an internal ranking nor a guarantee of one style.
:::
