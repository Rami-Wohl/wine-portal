:::summary{#wat-is-1855 depth="foundation" source_refs="source.gcc1855-classification-overview"}
The **1855 classification** is a historical ranking of a defined group of [[region.bordeaux|Bordeaux growths]]. It orders selected estates and wines; it is not an appellation and therefore does not divide the entire wine region. [@source.gcc1855-classification-overview]
:::

:::section{#reikwijdte depth="foundation" source_refs="source.gcc1855-classification-overview"}
## Which wines does it cover?

For red wine, the classification covers growths from five [[region.medoc|Médoc]] appellations, plus Château Haut-Brion from [[appellation.graves|Graves]]. These red growths are divided into five levels, from Premier to Cinquième Cru. For sweet white wine, it covers growths from [[appellation.sauternes|Sauternes]] and [[appellation.barsac|Barsac]], divided into Premier Cru Supérieur, Premier Cru and Second Cru. [@source.gcc1855-classification-overview]

That also makes the boundary clear: a wine from Bordeaux, or even from one of these appellations, is not automatically an 1855 Grand Cru Classé.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Read “Grand Cru Classé en 1855” as membership and rank within this one historical system, not as a general quality category for Bordeaux.
:::

:::caveat{#geen-smaakgarantie depth="advanced" variant="simplification" source_refs="source.gcc1855-classification-overview"}
A historical rank tells you where a growth sits within the classification. Understanding a particular bottle still requires its appellation, producer, [[concept.vintage|vintage]] and the wine itself.
:::
