:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.bordeaux-graves-classification source.legifrance-graves-classification-1959"}
The Graves Classification is compact, colour-specific and frozen in time. It has no first, second or lower ranks, but one title: *Cru Classé de Graves*. That title may apply to an estate's red wine, dry white wine or both. All fourteen current estates are in [[appellation.pessac-leognan|Pessac-Léognan]], although the classification's name recalls the larger Graves from which that appellation was separated only in 1987.
:::

:::section{#geschiedenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.bordeaux-graves-classification source.legifrance-graves-classification-1959"}
## History — twentieth-century recognition of older reputations

The first official list appeared in 1953. A ministerial order in 1959 confirmed its only revision. It gave legal recognition to estates long known for red, dry white or both. Unlike Saint-Émilion's periodically revised classification, the Graves system has not been reranked since.

When Pessac-Léognan became a separate appellation in 1987, the estates did not move: the legal map changed around them. A modern label may therefore carry both “Pessac-Léognan” and “Cru Classé de Graves” without contradiction.
:::

:::detail{#van-zestien-naar-veertien parent="geschiedenis" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.bordeaux-graves-classification"}
### Sixteen historic names became fourteen current estates

The original history counts sixteen classified châteaux. La Tour Haut-Brion and Laville Haut-Brion were later absorbed into Château La Mission Haut-Brion. The current presentation therefore contains fourteen estates, while old records and bottles may retain the historic names. This was a change in estate identity, not a fresh 2014 ranking.
:::

:::section{#opbouw-en-betekenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.bordeaux-graves-classification source.pessac-leognan-classified-growths"}
## Structure and meaning — one title, two colours

The classification is non-hierarchical: every listed estate is a *Cru Classé de Graves*. Colour nevertheless matters. Five current estates are classified for red, two for white and seven for both. Status for one colour must not be transferred carelessly to another wine from the same château.

Classification attaches to named properties and the relevant wine, not to every parcel or producer in Pessac-Léognan. It offers historical orientation, but guarantees no fixed quality in every vintage and does not automatically make unclassified neighbours inferior.
:::

:::detail{#verschil-met-1855 parent="opbouw-en-betekenis" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.gcc1855-classification-overview source.bordeaux-graves-classification"}
### Château Haut-Brion belongs to two systems

[[producer.chateau-haut-brion|Château Haut-Brion]] is both a Cru Classé de Graves and a first growth in the [[classification.bordeaux-1855|1855 Classification]]. That overlap is exceptional but does not make the systems equivalent. The 1855 list has ranks and covers red Médoc wines plus sweet Sauternes and Barsac; Graves has one title and can recognise dry white independently.

## Label and use — check estate, colour and appellation

Read “Cru Classé de Graves” together with the producer's full name and the wine colour. The present appellation is Pessac-Léognan. “Grand Cru Classé” is sometimes used in public-facing material, but the essential point is the officially recognised classification, not a rank like those of 1855.

Old bottles may carry former estate names. Vintage, storage and condition remain decisive for every bottle: an historic title does not replace assessing the wine in front of you.
:::
