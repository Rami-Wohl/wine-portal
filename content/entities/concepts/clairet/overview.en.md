## Clairet — a deep-coloured rosé

A glass of clairet may have a vivid raspberry or cherry hue. By sight alone, you might call it red. The Bordeaux specification, however, reserves *clairet* for **deep-coloured rosé**; the separate mention [[concept.claret|claret]] is for **light red wine**. The names differ by one letter, but the categories are distinct. [@source.boagri-bordeaux-specification-2026]

The label carries the [[appellation.bordeaux|Bordeaux]] appellation with the additional mention Clairet. That tells you more than a colour swatch: the name identifies a defined wine category with its own production conditions. The current Bordeaux rules were officially approved again in May 2026. [@source.legifrance-bordeaux-order-2026]

## Colour and making

Much of the colour in dark-skinned grapes is in the skins. During [[concept.maceration|maceration]], juice and skins remain in contact. More or more intensive contact can bring more colour and structure. That helps explain why clairet can look deeper and feel more substantial than a pale rosé, without becoming as weighty as red Bordeaux. [@source.oiv-maceration]

The current Bordeaux description portrays clairets as vividly coloured and fruity, with more vinous weight than its other rosés. That is a style portrait, not a promise that every bottle smells or tastes alike. [@source.boagri-bordeaux-specification-2026]
:::

:::detail{#kleur-is-geen-recept parent="kleur-en-maakwijze" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.oiv-maceration"}
### Colour is not a recipe

A deeper colour does not prove that the wine spent a particular number of hours on its skins. Grape variety, ripeness, temperature, pressing and later winemaking all matter. Light and the shape of the glass also alter what you see. Read the legal category on the label and in the specification, not from a photograph.
:::

## The name and its neighbour

The name carries an older story: wine from [[region.bordeaux|Bordeaux]] was traded to England for centuries, where *claret* became a familiar word. Today's French mentions *clairet* and *claret* keep that family of words alive, but apply it to two distinct, defined styles. Do not read one as an alternative spelling of the other. [@source.inao-bordeaux-clairet-product-page]
:::

:::detail{#regels-veranderen parent="naam-en-grenzen" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.legifrance-bordeaux-order-2026"}
### A regulated name, not a colour contest

The specification governs more than the broad colour category: permitted production and analytical limits matter too. Such rules can change. The order of 13 May 2026 approved a new Bordeaux specification; a legal assessment must use the final text in force at the relevant time, not an old style description or the appearance of a glass. [@source.legifrance-bordeaux-order-2026]
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Clairet is the deep-rosé Bordeaux mention; claret is the light-red one. Colour helps orient you, but the label and the rules determine the name.
:::
