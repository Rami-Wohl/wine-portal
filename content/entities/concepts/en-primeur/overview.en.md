:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.civb-bordeaux-wine-trade source.ugcb-primeurs-week"}
In **en primeur**, young wine is offered commercially while it is still maturing in the cellar. Professionals taste barrel samples, producers then release their wines in stages, and buyers commit money to bottles that are generally delivered much later. The system is famous in [[region.bordeaux|Bordeaux]], but buying en primeur guarantees no advantage and the sample is not yet the final bottled wine. [@source.civb-bordeaux-wine-trade]
:::

:::section{#tijdlijn depth="foundation" source_refs="source.civb-bordeaux-wine-trade source.ugcb-primeurs-week"}
## From harvest to delivery

After harvest, the young components are vinified and [[concept.elevage|maturation]] begins. In the following spring, producers receive merchants, brokers, press and other professionals to taste the new [[concept.vintage|vintage]]. The organised primeurs week is an important tasting occasion, but the commercial campaign—with price releases and offers—follows the timing of individual producers. [@source.ugcb-primeurs-week]

After purchase, the wine remains in Bordeaux to mature further, be blended where necessary and eventually bottled. Only then do the bottles move through the trading chain; roughly eighteen months to two years may separate tasting from physical receipt. [@source.civb-bordeaux-wine-trade]
:::

:::detail{#monster-is-geen-eindproduct parent="tijdlijn" depth="intermediate"}
### A sample is not a finished product

A barrel sample gives a serious view of fruit, structure, balance and possible development, but remains a snapshot. The [[concept.assemblage|final blend]] may not be fixed exactly, individual barrels evolve differently and continued maturation changes texture and aroma.

Drawing and selecting a representative sample also requires care. A professional assessment therefore communicates uncertainty as well as quality. The younger and less complete the wine, the more experience is required to avoid confusing potential with certainty.
:::

## Trading chain

Within the [[concept.place-de-bordeaux|Place de Bordeaux]], a château offers wine, often with a [[concept.courtier|courtier]] acting as intermediary. [[concept.negociant|Négociants]] purchase or receive allocated volumes and offer them onwards to importers and other professional buyers. A consumer may ultimately reserve wine through a reputable merchant.

“Reserve” is a useful word here: delivery follows later, while the precise legal arrangement and payment schedule can vary by seller and country. Check whom you are buying from, when payment and delivery occur, what tax and storage cost, and what protection applies if a party does not meet its obligations.
:::

:::section{#waarom-het-systeem-bestaat depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.civb-bordeaux-wine-trade"}
## Why the system exists

For a producer, the campaign provides an early view of demand and revenue before the wine can be delivered. For négociants and importers, it distributes scarce volumes among markets at an early stage. For collectors, en primeur may provide access to formats or wines that will be difficult to find later.

Something substantial is given in return: the buyer finances stock and accepts time and uncertainty. The eventual market price may rise, fall or remain level; a wine may develop differently than expected; storage, currencies, tax and margins affect the real cost. En primeur is therefore a method of trade, not an embedded discount or an investment with guaranteed returns.
:::

:::detail{#prijs-vraag-en-reputatie parent="waarom-het-systeem-bestaat" depth="advanced"}
### Price, demand and reputation

A release price does not emerge from one formula. Expected vintage quality, the château's reputation, available volume, earlier prices, stock already in the market, currencies and global demand all play a part. Producers may also release at different times and in different quantities.

Tasting notes and scores influence attention, but neither determine the price alone nor remove uncertainty. The campaign is a negotiation between reputation, scarcity and willingness to buy—not an objective ranking of the vintage.
:::

:::section{#niet-hetzelfde-als-primeurwijn depth="foundation"}
## Not the same as a primeur wine

Buying *en primeur* concerns the timing of sale before delivery. A *vin primeur* or *vin nouveau*, by contrast, is a wine style bottled and drunk shortly after harvest. Bordeaux en primeur may involve powerful ageworthy wine that will spend much longer in barrel and bottle; the word *primeur* does not make it early-drinking or immediately ready.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
En primeur separates the purchase date from the delivery date. You assess and finance a wine in development; possible access is balanced by uncertainty about the finished product, price, waiting time and seller.
:::
