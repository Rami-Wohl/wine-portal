Spätlese is a German Prädikat with requirements for harvested grapes. It does not independently describe how sweet the finished wine is. Read the harvest term and an indication such as trocken as two different pieces of information.
:::

:::section{#overzicht depth="foundation" source_refs="source.german-wine-law-pradikate-2026"}
## Overview

Under German wine law, Spätlese requires grapes harvested late in a fully ripe condition. It is a regulated Prädikat, rather than a free translation for all [[concept.late-harvest|late-harvest wine]] worldwide. This page addresses its German scope.
:::

:::section{#werking depth="foundation" source_refs="source.dwi-praedikate source.dwi-wine-taste-categories"}
## How it works

Prädikate relate to ripeness and minimum must weight before fermentation. Must weight expresses must density and is closely related to sugar content. During [[concept.fermentation|fermentation]], sugar is converted; remaining sugar is a different measurement. The Prädikat alone therefore does not fix a sweet finished style.
:::

:::section{#omstandigheden-en-variatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.dwi-praedikate"}
## Conditions and variation

Minimum must weights vary by grape variety and German wine-growing area. One general Oechsle figure would conceal that variation. For a particular wine, use the applicable rules alongside information about actual vinification.
:::

:::section{#toepassing-en-keuzes depth="foundation" source_refs="source.dwi-wine-taste-categories"}
## Application and decisions

For sweetness, seek additional information such as **trocken**, another taste category or residual sugar on the technical sheet. This answers a different question from Spätlese. Ripe fruit in a tasting note does not replace residual-sugar information either.
:::

:::section{#bewijs-en-grenzen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.german-wine-law-pradikate-2026"}
## Evidence and limits

The legal basis is Germany’s WeinG, §20, in the reviewed version amended in January 2026. Prädikatswein may not be enriched to increase alcohol content. This requirement differs from residual sugar in the finished wine. Similar words outside Germany require investigation of their own legal context.
:::

:::section{#betekenis-voor-wijn depth="foundation"}
## Significance for wine

Spätlese helps explain raw material and label. Combine it with origin, sweetness information and the fermentation account. The page on [[concept.sweet-wines|sweet wines]] connects this distinction with sugar concentration and the course of fermentation; [[concept.late-harvest|late harvest]] addresses leaving fruit hanging longer.
:::
