A calcareous vineyard soil contains enough calcium carbonate to influence the chemical and sometimes physical root environment. It may occur above limestone, chalk or marl, but rock and soil profile are not the same. The central questions concern pH, available nutrition, water, structure and suitable plant material—not a supposed taste of limestone. [@source.maltman-vineyards-rocks-soils-2018] [@source.tagliavini-rombola-iron-chlorosis-2001]
:::

:::section{#overzicht depth="foundation" source_refs="source.maltman-vineyards-rocks-soils-2018"}
## Overview — calcareous is a property of the profile

**Limestone** is carbonate rock; **chalk** is a soft, porous form of it; **marl** mixes carbonate and clay-rich material. A soil is calcareous when carbonates are materially present in its fine earth or profile. This can coincide with clay, silt, sand or stones and says nothing fixed about depth or drainage.

Weathering, deposition and soil formation make each profile different. Carbonates may occur at the surface, appear only at depth or have been locally leached and reprecipitated. The general framework lives on [[concept.vineyard-soils|vineyard soils]]; this page focuses on the distinctive chemical and viticultural questions raised by carbonates.
:::

:::figure{#kalkprofiel depth="foundation" media_id="media.concept.calcareous-vineyard-soils.limestone-profile"}
**Soil and carbonate rock are different layers of the story**

At this Yorkshire exposure, thin soil overlies mudstone and limestone. In a vineyard, depth, fine earth, fractures and carbonates may be distributed very differently; a rock photograph therefore cannot replace profile description.

:::section{#werking depth="foundation" source_refs="source.tagliavini-rombola-iron-chlorosis-2001 source.diaz-de-la-torre-iron-chlorosis-2010"}
## How it works — high pH changes availability

Carbonates commonly buffer soil on the alkaline side. Iron can then be present yet poorly soluble and difficult to absorb. A susceptible vine responds with **iron chlorosis**: young leaves yellow between greener veins, while photosynthesis and growth may decline. Availability of other nutrients also depends on pH, water, organic complexes and root activity. [@source.tagliavini-rombola-iron-chlorosis-2001]

Risk does not follow total lime content alone. Research in Spanish vineyard soils found relationships with calcium carbonate equivalent, active lime and certain iron forms, while measuring the plant response over several seasons with a specific rootstock. Soil tests, plant material and observed response must therefore be read together. [@source.diaz-de-la-torre-iron-chlorosis-2010]
:::

:::detail{#water-en-structuur parent="werking" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.van-leeuwen-soil-terroir-2018"}
### Water behaviour follows the profile, not the word lime

Porous chalk, fractured limestone, clay-rich marl and deep calcareous loam possess different pores and rooting volumes. Some profiles take in winter water and release it gradually; others are shallow, stony and quickly limiting. [[concept.vine-water-relations|Vine water status]] must therefore be measured or inferred from the whole profile and climate, not from the carbonate label.
:::

:::section{#omstandigheden-en-variatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.van-leeuwen-soil-terroir-2018"}
## Conditions and variation

Fine-pored chalk, fractured limestone beneath a clay-rich layer and deep calcareous loam share carbonates but not the same water or root environment. Climate and season determine whether storage, drainage or chlorosis weighs most heavily; variety and rootstock change the plant response. ‘Calcareous’ is therefore a relevant family, not one uniform soil type.
:::

:::section{#keuzes-en-afwegingen depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.tagliavini-rombola-iron-chlorosis-2001 source.diaz-de-la-torre-iron-chlorosis-2010"}
## Application and decisions — plant material before symptom control

Before planting, profile mapping, pH, carbonates and chlorosis risk belong in [[concept.rootstock|rootstock]] choice. Rootstocks differ in tolerance of lime-induced iron deficiency, while local drought, salt, vigour and graft compatibility also matter. Existing chlorosis requires diagnosis: waterlogging, root injury or other deficiencies may reinforce similar growth problems.

Iron chelates, organic inputs and soil or foliar treatments may be used in particular systems, but cannot structurally repair a poor match between profile and plant material. The appropriate intervention follows cause, severity, scale and regulation.
:::

:::section{#bewijs-en-grenzen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.maltman-vineyards-rocks-soils-2018 source.van-leeuwen-soil-terroir-2018"}
## Evidence and limits — limestone is not a tasting note

Calcareous soils can influence vines through nutrition, root growth, water and temperature. Grape acids are formed and transformed through plant metabolism, ripening, climate, crop load and cellar choices. High carbonate content therefore does not prove that a wine automatically becomes more acidic or tastes ‘chalky’.

Regional relationships can be valuable when comparable sites and seasons are studied. They remain relationships inside a larger system, not a transfer of limestone molecules into a similarly named sensation. [@source.maltman-vineyards-rocks-soils-2018] [@source.van-leeuwen-soil-terroir-2018]
:::

:::section{#betekenis-voor-wijn depth="foundation" source_refs="source.van-leeuwen-soil-terroir-2018"}
## Significance for wine

A useful calcareous-soil claim states where carbonates occur, how deep and structured the profile is, which water and nutrient status the vine reaches and which plant material is used. Carbonate can then form part of a supported [[concept.terroir|terroir explanation]]. Without those intermediate steps it remains mainly a geological image.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Calcareous soils matter because carbonates can alter the root environment and nutrient availability. Their influence runs through the plant; limestone itself is neither acid nor chalk aroma.
:::
