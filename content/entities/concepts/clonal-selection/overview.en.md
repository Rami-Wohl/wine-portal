:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-clonal-selection-2017"}
A **clone** is the vegetatively propagated progeny of one vine. In **clonal selection**, researchers search within a grape variety for a vine with useful characteristics and sound health status. That single vine is then propagated, checked and compared before its material can be distributed as a selected clone. [@source.oiv-clonal-selection-2017]
:::

:::section{#wat-is-een-kloon depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-clonal-selection-2017"}
## Meaning — one vine, many descendants

Vines are generally not raised from seed because every seedling forms a new genetic combination. Nurseries propagate an existing variety through cuttings or grafting: a piece of shoot continues as a genetic extension of its mother vine. When that line starts from one chosen plant, it is called a clone.

A clone remains part of the same variety. A clone of [[grape.cabernet-franc|Cabernet Franc]] is not a new grape variety, nor does it become [[grape.cabernet-sauvignon|Cabernet Sauvignon]] because its bunches are smaller or its ripening earlier.
:::

:::detail{#variatie-binnen-een-ras parent="wat-is-een-kloon" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.oiv-clonal-selection-2017"}
### Variation within a variety

Across many generations of vegetative propagation, spontaneous mutations can arise and persist. Vines of the same old variety may therefore differ in bunch architecture, yield, ripening, colour or aromatic behaviour. Disease and virus status also cause differences, but are not desirable genetic traits. [@source.oiv-clonal-selection-2017]

The first task is to distinguish what is inherited, what comes from site or season and what is a health problem. A striking vine is a candidate, not yet a demonstrably better clone.
:::

:::section{#selecteren-en-toetsen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-clonal-selection-2017 source.oiv-grape-variety-identification-2019"}
## Selection and testing — identity, performance and health

A selection programme starts with an objective. It may target ripening time, loose bunches, stable yield, wine quality or a combination of traits. The candidate must first be identified as true to variety through [[concept.ampelography|ampelography]] and, where needed, genetic analysis. Screening for harmful pathogens follows. [@source.oiv-clonal-selection-2017]

Healthy does not automatically mean outstanding quality, while an attractive field trait does not guarantee healthy material. Clonal selection assesses both dimensions before larger-scale propagation is justified.
:::

:::detail{#meerdere-jaren-en-plaatsen parent="selecteren-en-toetsen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.oiv-clonal-selection-2017 source.plantgrape-cabernet-franc"}
### Several years are stronger than one harvest

Candidates are propagated and followed over several seasons. A trial may compare growth cycle, yield, bunch and berry, vulnerabilities and resulting wine. This reveals whether a difference recurs or belonged to a single season or site. [@source.oiv-clonal-selection-2017]

After approval, identity and health remain controlled through mother vines and successive propagation stages. For France, Plantgrape records details such as origin, selection partners, approval year, agronomic reference and availability of registered clones. [@source.plantgrape-cabernet-franc]
:::

:::section{#gebruiken-en-begrenzen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-cabernet-franc source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon"}
## Use and limits — suitable material, not a ranking

A clone lets a grower choose plant material purposefully. Earlier ripening may help on a cool site; looser bunches may be useful where rot is recurrent. The same choice may fit less well in a hot or dry place. [[concept.rootstock|Rootstock]], soil, climate, spacing and management remain at least as influential.

Clone numbers also belong to national registration and certification systems. A number without country, selection history and technical description says little and is never a quality guarantee on its own.
:::

:::detail{#selectie-en-diversiteit parent="gebruiken-en-begrenzen" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.oiv-clonal-selection-2017"}
### Selecting without making everything identical

Using one clone can make a parcel more predictable, but planting very few lines widely may narrow variation within an old variety. Conservatories therefore exist alongside commercial clones, while growers may choose several clones or [[concept.massal-selection|massal selection]] from a broader group of healthy vines.

This is not a simple contest between modern and traditional. Certified clones offer traceable identity and sanitary control; broader selections can preserve variation. The best choice follows from purpose, available evidence and site.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
A clone is selected planting material within a variety. Its value emerges only when traits, health, origin and suitability for a particular vineyard are read together.
:::
