:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.blanco-ulate-noble-rot-2015 source.vaczy-noble-grey-rot-2024"}
**Botrytis cinerea** is usually a feared fungal disease. Under an exceptional combination of ripe grapes, moisture and subsequent drying, however, the same fungus can produce **noble rot**: berries lose water, shrivel and yield intensely concentrated juice for certain sweet wines. [@source.blanco-ulate-noble-rot-2015]

“Noble” is not the name of a friendly fungal strain. It describes a favourable outcome that can fail afresh in every [[concept.vintage|vintage]].
:::

:::section{#een-schimmel-twee-uitkomsten depth="foundation" source_refs="source.vaczy-noble-grey-rot-2024 source.awri-managing-botrytis-fruit-2023"}
## One fungus, two outcomes — noble or destructive

On ripe, sound white grapes, Botrytis may develop slowly while berries dry between damp periods. The skin becomes more permeable, water escapes and the grape wrinkles without immediately collapsing. Winemakers call this noble rot.

When clusters remain wet for too long, suffer early damage or endure uncontrolled infection, grey rot can result: soft, leaking or mouldy fruit that harms yield and wine quality. Research also shows biologically different responses by grape and fungus under noble and grey rot. [@source.vaczy-noble-grey-rot-2024]
:::

:::detail{#weer-rijpheid-en-timing parent="een-schimmel-twee-uitkomsten" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.blanco-ulate-noble-rot-2015 source.awri-managing-botrytis-fruit-2023"}
### Moisture starts the process; drying gives it direction

Humid nights, morning mist or short periods of rain help spores germinate and infection develop. Sun, wind or drier air must then let berry surfaces dry. Ripeness matters too: a ripe berry has different skin, chemistry and defences from hard green fruit. [@source.blanco-ulate-noble-rot-2015]

The familiar rhythm of “misty morning, sunny afternoon” is a useful model, not a weather recipe. Moisture duration, temperature, cluster compactness, skin condition, air movement and prior infection vary within one parcel and even one bunch.
:::

:::detail{#geen-twee-soorten parent="een-schimmel-twee-uitkomsten" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.vaczy-noble-grey-rot-2024"}
### No switch between two separate fungi

Botrytis cinerea is a necrotrophic fungus: it damages plant cells and uses dead tissue. “Noble rot” and “grey rot” are therefore not two tidy biological boxes or separate species. They are distinguishable outcomes within a dynamic interaction among fungus, ripening berry and environment.

A noble-rotted bunch may contain areas of unwanted decay and can deteriorate further when weather turns. Pickers assess individual berries and moments, not merely the presence of the species.

:::section{#wat-er-met-de-druif-gebeurt depth="foundation" source_refs="source.blanco-ulate-noble-rot-2015"}
## The grape — less volume, greater concentration

The fungus affects skin and cell walls. Water escapes more readily and the berry shrivels, leaving sugar and various other components in far less juice. Yield falls sharply: a bunch that looks impressively ripe may produce remarkably little liquid when pressed.

Noble rot is more than drying. Ordinary [[concept.passerillage|passerillage]]—berries shrivelling on the vine without desirable fungal action—can also concentrate fruit, but does not automatically create the same composition.
:::

:::detail{#metabolisme-en-aroma parent="wat-er-met-de-druif-gebeurt" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.blanco-ulate-noble-rot-2015"}
### The berry keeps responding as it shrivels

Research on Sémillon shows that noble rot changes gene activity and metabolism in both grape and fungus. Pathways involving stress response, ripening and secondary metabolites become differently active; terpenes, fatty-acid-derived aroma precursors and phenolic compounds are among those affected. [@source.blanco-ulate-noble-rot-2015]

The result is therefore not simply a concentrated version of a healthy grape. Some compounds concentrate as water is lost, while others are formed, broken down or transformed. Variety, infection stage and season influence the outcome.
:::

:::section{#oogst-en-wijnmaken depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026 source.magyar-bor-tokaj-wine-region"}
## Harvest and winemaking — selection matters more than speed

Noble rot spreads unevenly. Pickers therefore often return several times, taking only suitable berries or bunches. These successive passes are known as *tries*. In Sauternes, successive hand-picked tries form part of the appellation rules; in Tokaj, aszú berries may be selected individually. [@source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026] [@source.magyar-bor-tokaj-wine-region]

The thick, sugar-rich juice is hard to extract. During [[concept.fermentation|fermentation]], yeast slowly converts part of the sugar into alcohol; substantial natural residual sugar generally remains. Acidity, concentration and cellar choices must work together to keep the wine from becoming merely heavy or sticky.
:::

:::detail{#tries-en-ongelijke-trossen parent="oogst-en-wijnmaken" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026 source.magyar-bor-tokaj-wine-region"}
### One bunch may contain three harvest decisions

Berries do not ripen or become nobly rotted together. A picker may take suitable fruit, leave sound berries hanging and reject grey-rotted parts. Another pass costs labour and exposes the remaining fruit to more rain, wind and desiccation.

This explains both low, variable yields and the cost of the process better than prestige alone. Even in a favourable year, not every affected grape is usable.
:::

:::detail{#kelderrisicos parent="oogst-en-wijnmaken" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.awri-managing-botrytis-fruit-2023"}
### Unwanted Botrytis travels into the winery

For ordinary dry or red wine, Botrytis infection is usually damage. The fungus can produce laccase, an enzyme that promotes oxidative browning and quality loss in the presence of oxygen. Damaged fruit may also bring mouldy characters and polysaccharides that make clarification and filtration more difficult. [@source.awri-managing-botrytis-fruit-2023]

“Botrytis present” is therefore never enough to call fruit noble. Purpose, berry condition, selection and processing determine whether a lot is valuable or problematic.
:::

## Wine traditions — the same organism, different wines

In [[appellation.sauternes|Sauternes]] and neighbouring appellations within [[region.bordeaux|Bordeaux]], [[grape.semillon|Sémillon]] is closely associated with noble sweet wine. In [[region.tokaj|Tokaj]], [[grape.furmint|Furmint]] plays a leading role in wines including Tokaji Aszú. In Germany and Austria, noble rot appears in categories such as Beerenauslese and Trockenbeerenauslese; [[grape.riesling|Riesling]] is a familiar, but certainly not the only, possible grape. [@source.magyar-bor-tokaj-wine-region] [@source.austrian-wine-key-facts-2023] [@source.wines-germany-pradikate] [@source.wines-germany-riesling-guide]

These traditions share concentration from selected affected fruit. They do not share one universal grape, harvest method, legal category or flavour.
:::

:::detail{#verschillende-tradities parent="waar-edele-rotting-wijn-maakt" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026 source.magyar-bor-tokaj-wine-region source.austrian-wine-key-facts-2023 source.wines-germany-pradikate"}
### Botrytis is a building block, not an international recipe

Sauternes ferments juice from successively selected overripe grapes. Tokaji Aszú has its own method and legal tradition around selected aszú berries. Beerenauslese and Trockenbeerenauslese belong to German-language ripeness and harvest categories.

“Botrytized” therefore says something essential about the fruit, but not precisely how the wine was made. Origin, grapes, selection, [[concept.extraction|extraction]], fermentation and [[concept.elevage|maturation]] still need to be read alongside it.
:::

:::section{#in-het-glas depth="foundation" source_refs="source.blanco-ulate-noble-rot-2015 source.magyar-bor-tokaj-wine-region"}
## In the glass — sweetness with more than ripe fruit

Botrytized wines may show dried apricot, citrus or orange peel, honey, saffron-like spice, flowers and waxy notes. Texture can be rich and viscous, but compelling examples retain tension through acidity, gentle bitterness and aromatic layering.

This is a stylistic range, not a checklist. Grape, place, proportion of affected berries, fermentation, maturation, bottle age and serving conditions change what is actually perceptible.
:::

:::detail{#aroma-is-geen-bewijs parent="in-het-glas" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.blanco-ulate-noble-rot-2015"}
### Honey does not prove a fungus

Honey, dried fruit or marmalade can also arise from very ripe fruit, drying, oxidative development, oak or bottle age. Conversely, not every young botrytized wine shows all the classic aromas.

Recognition becomes more reliable when flavour is considered beside label, origin and production method. One aroma is never a laboratory test.
:::

:::section{#herkennen-en-selecteren depth="foundation" source_refs="source.awri-managing-botrytis-fruit-2023 source.vaczy-noble-grey-rot-2024"}
## Recognition and selection — look at the whole berry

Noble-rotted berries are generally brown to purplish brown, wrinkled and greatly reduced, yet largely intact. Grey rot more often presents soft or split fruit, dampness, leakage and conspicuous grey fungal growth. Sound, noble-rotted and unusable berries may hang together within one cluster.

Colour alone is insufficient. A picker also considers smell, firmness, skin condition, moisture and the wine's purpose. The photographs show recognisable examples, but together they are neither a timeline nor a field protocol.
:::

:::detail{#klimaat-en-oogstvenster parent="herkennen-en-selecteren" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.awri-managing-botrytis-fruit-2023 source.vaczy-noble-grey-rot-2024"}
### The right harvest moment moves with autumn

Warmer weather can advance ripeness while rain and mist shift on a different timetable. A dry autumn may limit Botrytis; prolonged wetness can push desirable infection rapidly beyond its useful stage. Producers must follow berry health, acidity, forecast and available pickers rather than sugar alone.

Noble rot thus remains an opportunity, not an annual production programme.
:::

:::caveat{#grens-van-herkenning depth="advanced" variant="uncertainty" source_refs="source.vaczy-noble-grey-rot-2024"}
Research can distinguish noble and grey rot through field observation, fungal activity and the grape's response. An isolated photograph or aroma in a glass cannot independently provide that diagnosis. Borderline cases require context and expert assessment.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Noble rot is not another fungus, but a rare favourable relationship among Botrytis, ripe grapes, moisture, drying and human selection. When one of those conditions goes awry, the same infection can bring loss instead of great sweet wine.
:::
