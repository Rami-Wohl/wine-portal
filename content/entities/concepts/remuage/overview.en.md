:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.champagne-riddling"}
**Riddling** (*remuage*) is the gradual turning and tilting of sparkling-wine bottles to move yeast sediment into their necks. It follows ageing on the lees and precedes [[concept.disgorgement|disgorgement]], which actually removes the sediment. Riddling alone does not clarify the wine. [@source.champagne-riddling]
:::

:::section{#werkwijze depth="foundation" source_refs="source.champagne-riddling"}
## Process — guiding sediment to one place

After the [[concept.second-fermentation|second fermentation]] and ageing, spent yeast cells remain in the bottle. Turning the bottle a little at a time while gradually pointing its neck down collects the sediment near the closure. It can then be removed in one operation, with as little wine lost as possible. [@source.champagne-riddling]
:::

:::detail{#hand-en-machine parent="werkwijze" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.champagne-riddling"}
### By hand or machine

Traditionally, bottles sit in an angled rack called a *pupitre*, where a worker turns them step by step. A *gyropalette* performs the same movement mechanically for many bottles at once. Both serve the same purpose; hand riddling alone is not proof of superior wine. [@source.champagne-riddling]
:::

:::section{#plaats-in-proces depth="foundation" source_refs="source.champagne-riddling source.champagne-disgorgement"}
## Place in the process — collecting is not removing

Riddling belongs to sparkling wine made with bottle fermentation and yeast sediment, as in the [[concept.traditional-method|traditional method]]. After the turning, the sediment is still in the bottle. Only at disgorgement is the neck opened and the collected deposit expelled. [@source.champagne-riddling] [@source.champagne-disgorgement]
:::

:::detail{#uitzonderingen parent="plaats-in-proces" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.champagne-riddling"}
### Not every sparkling wine needs it

A wine fermented a second time in a pressure tank and filtered before bottling has no bottles full of yeast sediment to riddle. Even with bottle fermentation, producers vary in how they do it. The word names an operation, not a legally fixed duration that is the same everywhere. [@source.champagne-riddling]
:::

:::key-idea{#kern depth="foundation"}
Riddling moves sediment into the neck; disgorgement takes it out.
:::
