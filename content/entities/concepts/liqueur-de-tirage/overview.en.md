:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.champagne-bottling-second-fermentation"}
**Liqueur de tirage** is the mixture including yeast and sugar added to a still base wine to start the [[concept.second-fermentation|second fermentation]]. In the [[concept.traditional-method|traditional method]] it is added just before bottling. Its sugar fuels the yeast; it is not simply a sweetener for the finished wine. [@source.champagne-bottling-second-fermentation]
:::

:::section{#functie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.champagne-bottling-second-fermentation"}
## Purpose — starting fermentation in the bottle

The base wine has already finished fermenting. Adding yeast and a measured amount of fermentable sugar allows [[concept.fermentation|alcoholic fermentation]] to begin again inside the sealed bottle. The carbon dioxide produced dissolves in the wine. ‘Tirage’ refers to bottling for this stage; the liquid is part of the production process, not a separate wine style. [@source.champagne-bottling-second-fermentation]
:::

:::detail{#samenstelling parent="functie" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.champagne-bottling-second-fermentation"}
### More than sugar poured into a bottle

The yeast must work under pressure in a base wine that already contains alcohol. Producers therefore choose the yeast and the quantities carefully; besides wine, sugar and yeast, the mixture may include processing aids for later stages. The aim is controlled refermentation, not arbitrary residual sweetness. [@source.champagne-bottling-second-fermentation]
:::

:::section{#onderscheid depth="foundation" source_refs="source.champagne-bottling-second-fermentation source.champagne-dosage"}
## Distinction — before or after second fermentation

Liqueur de tirage goes into the wine **before** the second fermentation. [[concept.dosage|Dosage]] comes **after** fermentation and removal of the yeast sediment. That later addition can change the final sweetness balance. The two French terms sound related, but serve different purposes at different moments. [@source.champagne-bottling-second-fermentation] [@source.champagne-dosage]
:::

:::detail{#geen-recept parent="onderscheid" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.champagne-bottling-second-fermentation"}
### No universal recipe

The sugar required depends on the desired pressure and on what is already present in the base wine. An exact formula from Champagne or another appellation is therefore not a general definition of liqueur de tirage. Production rules and cellar decisions must be read separately. [@source.champagne-bottling-second-fermentation]
:::

:::key-idea{#kern depth="foundation"}
Liqueur de tirage starts the second fermentation; dosage follows later and helps determine how dry or sweet the finished wine tastes.
:::
