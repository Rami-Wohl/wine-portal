In the **traditional method**, a still base wine acquires its bubbles through a **[[concept.second-fermentation|second fermentation]] in the bottle**. It then ages on its yeast sediment. Only after that sediment has been removed and the bottle sealed is the wine ready for release. Champagne is the best-known example, but the method is also used elsewhere, including for [[appellation.cremant-de-bordeaux|Crémant de Bordeaux]]. [@source.champagne-bottling-second-fermentation] [@source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026]

## Process — how the bubbles enter the bottle

The producer first makes a still base wine. Yeast and sugar are added at bottling as the [[concept.liqueur-de-tirage|liqueur de tirage]]. The yeast ferments the sugar; the resulting carbon dioxide cannot escape and dissolves in the wine. After ageing on the yeast, [[concept.remuage|riddling]] moves the sediment to the neck. [[concept.disgorgement|Disgorgement]] removes it. The producer can then use [[concept.dosage|dosage]] to set the final sweetness balance. [@source.champagne-bottling-second-fermentation] [@source.champagne-riddling] [@source.champagne-disgorgement] [@source.champagne-dosage]
:::

### What time on the lees can add

During bottle ageing, the wine remains in contact with spent yeast cells, or *lees*. This can influence texture and aroma; bread or dough notes are possible, but not guaranteed. The time on lees and the character of the base wine both matter. [[concept.autolysis|Autolysis]] is one of the processes that can occur. [@source.awri-lees-contact-2020]
:::

## Scope — a method is not an origin

‘Traditional method’ tells you **how** the bubbles form, not **where** the wine comes from or how good it is. With the tank method, the second fermentation takes place in a sealed tank instead. Champagne and individual crémants also have their own origin and production rules; those rules do not automatically apply to every traditionally made sparkling wine. [@source.champagne-bottling-second-fermentation] [@source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026]
:::

:::detail{#grenzen parent="afbakening" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
### Rules vary by appellation

The Crémant de Bordeaux specification, for example, requires bottle fermentation and a minimum period on the lees. That is a local requirement, not a universal definition of the traditional method. Always read figures for ageing, grapes and harvest in the context of the relevant appellation. [@source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026]
:::

:::key-idea{#kern depth="foundation"}
In the traditional method, the sparkle forms in the same bottle in which the wine ages on its lees. Origin, style and quality do not follow automatically from the method’s name.
:::
