:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.sanmartin-postharvest-water-loss-2021"}
Passito is an Italian wine term for wine made with dried grapes. Appassimento names the drying process; passito helps identify the resulting wine type. Its precise meaning on a label belongs to the relevant origin and product rules.
:::

:::section{#overzicht depth="foundation" source_refs="source.sanmartin-postharvest-water-loss-2021"}
## Overview

The distinction is between an operation and a wine name. [[concept.appassimento|Appassimento]] explains grape treatment before vinification. Passito refers to wine in which dried grapes play a role. The term does not specify one fixed drying protocol or aroma.
:::

:::section{#werking depth="foundation" source_refs="source.sanmartin-postharvest-water-loss-2021"}
## How it works

Water loss concentrates the fruit; biological changes also take place during drying. Subsequent fermentation helps determine which sugars are converted. [[concept.passerillage|Passerillage]] and appassimento address this process side. A drying method and the wine’s name are not interchangeable.
:::

:::section{#omstandigheden-en-variatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.sanmartin-postharvest-water-loss-2021"}
## Conditions and variation

Drying environment, grapes and subsequent processing can differ. Use passito as a starting point for questions: where and how was the fruit dried, and what happened during vinification? The name alone does not supply those answers.
:::

:::section{#toepassing-en-keuzes depth="foundation" source_refs="source.masaf-pantelleria-specification-2024"}
## Application and decisions

The **Pantelleria** specification distinguishes Passito di Pantelleria from Pantelleria passito liquoroso. The latter involves alcohol addition during or after fermentation. [[concept.fortification|Fortification]] and dried grapes can therefore overlap. This applies within the named appellation; it is not a recipe for all passito.
:::

:::section{#bewijs-en-grenzen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.masaf-pantelleria-specification-2024"}
## Evidence and limits

Check the full wine name and applicable specification. The reviewed Pantelleria version was consolidated in August 2024. It supports this local example, rather than a general Italian rule for every use of passito. [[concept.vin-de-paille|Vin de paille]] also has its own French name context.
:::

:::section{#betekenis-voor-wijn depth="foundation"}
## Significance for wine

Read the full name first, then the drying and fermentation account and any alcohol addition. The pages on [[concept.sweet-wines|sweet wines]] and [[concept.fortified-wines|fortified wines]] help distinguish sugar retention from alcohol addition. Passito thus remains a useful wine term without a flavour guarantee or duplicate explanation of the drying mechanism.
:::
