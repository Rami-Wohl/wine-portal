:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation"}
A vegetated alley, a certification mark on a bottle and a measured reduction in water use each tell a different story about a vineyard. Farming systems connect everyday work with an approach to agriculture. Sustainability then asks how those choices affect soil, water, nature, people and the viability of the business — today and across successive harvests.
:::

:::section{#overzicht depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-sustainability-principles-2016 source.oiv-sustainability-implementation-2020"}
## Overview

Three questions help explain a farming system. **Practice:** what does the grower do, such as sowing a cover crop or recording water use? **Certification:** which standard applies, and who checks compliance? **Outcome:** what change has actually been established, where and over what period? A standard may also require outcome measurements; its precise scope belongs to the particular programme.

The OIV approaches sustainability through environmental, social and economic considerations. These encompass soil and biodiversity, safe work, fair treatment and sufficient income to maintain the business. Progress in one area may bring extra costs or pressure elsewhere. Assessment therefore starts with specific goals and a known starting point. [@source.oiv-sustainability-principles-2016]
:::

:::section{#opbouw-en-samenhang depth="foundation" source_refs="source.ec-organic-production-products source.demeter-biodynamic-standard-2026 source.newton-regenerative-definitions-2020 source.oiv-plant-protection-good-practices-2018"}
## Structure and relationships

Common approaches share many operations but differ in principles and rules:

- **Conventional:** a broad description of farming outside a specific organic or biodynamic system. The name alone reveals little about a business's input use or conservation measures.
- **Integrated:** combines prevention, observation and targeted intervention. Integrated pest management, or IPM, selects measures according to the assessed risk. Its decision cycle is explained under [[concept.vineyard-hazards-diseases-pests-disorders|vineyard hazards, diseases, pests and disorders]].
- **Organic:** follows rules governing matters such as soil fertility and permitted inputs. In the EU, organic production falls under Regulation (EU) 2018/848; processing grapes into organic wine also has specific requirements. Permitted crop protection remains possible: organic does not mean unsprayed. [@source.ec-organic-production-products]
- **Biodynamic:** treats the farm as an interconnected organism and uses particular compost and spray preparations. The 2026 Demeter standard sets specific requirements alongside its organic basis, including the application of horn manure and horn silica preparations. This describes the method; investigation of its effects is a separate question. [@source.demeter-biodynamic-standard-2026; chapter 6.2]
- **Regenerative:** emphasises restoration, for example of soil functions or biodiversity. Practices mentioned include ground cover, reduced tillage and combining crops with livestock. In their 2020 review, Newton and colleagues found definitions centred on practices, outcomes or both. Ask which programme, measures and measured changes the term refers to in each case. [@source.newton-regenerative-definitions-2020]

An organic business can therefore practise integrated pest management and participate in a broader sustainability programme. These names do not form successive steps on a single quality ladder.
:::

:::detail{#certificering-en-scope parent="opbouw-en-samenhang" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.ec-organic-production-products source.swnz-certification-handbook-2026 source.demeter-biodynamic-standard-2026"}
### Read the certificate's scope

Check the standard's name and version, the certified activity, the parcels or products covered and the validity period. A statement about grape growing differs from certification of the finished wine. In the EU, for example, additional rules govern organic winemaking. SWNZ also distinguishes parts of the production chain and combines annual data submissions with independent site audits. [@source.ec-organic-production-products] [@source.swnz-certification-handbook-2026; Certification requirements and The certification process]

An audit establishes compliance with the relevant requirements. Which environmental performance, employment conditions or measurements it covers follows from those requirements. A producer's unsupported promise and a verifiable certificate consequently provide different kinds of evidence.
:::

## How it works

A measure acts through a specific process. Vegetation between rows covers the soil, contributes plant material and provides habitat. Reviews find benefits for infiltration, soil structure, erosion control and biodiversity — the variety of organisms. Living cover also uses water and nutrients. Species, the area covered and the timing of termination help determine the balance. [@source.abad-cover-crops-viticulture-soil-2021] [@source.abad-cover-crops-agronomic-performance-2021]

[[concept.vine-nutrition-soil-management-vineyard-floor|Nutrition and vineyard floor management]] explain these processes in detail; [[concept.vineyard-soils|vineyard soils]] establish the starting conditions. A beneficial soil measure becomes a supported business decision when vine growth, work and harvest are also monitored.

Carbon also follows several routes. Fuel use causes emissions; carbon can be stored in soil and perennial biomass. A climate assessment brings emissions and storage together within the same boundaries. The presence of green plants or compost alone does not establish that balance. [@source.oiv-ghg-inventory-2015]

For [[concept.vine-water-relations|water use]], the vine's needs matter alongside the source, local availability and water quality. Reduced consumption becomes meaningful when considered with vine performance and pressure on water resources. People also belong in this chain of cause and effect: a measure must be feasible with sufficient time, knowledge and safe working conditions.
:::

:::detail{#middelen-en-arbeid parent="werking" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.oiv-plant-protection-good-practices-2018 source.oiv-sustainability-principles-2016"}
### Assess inputs and work together

Inputs are resources used by the business, such as water, fuel, fertilisers and plant protection products. In crop protection, product choice, dose, timing, exposure and application technique jointly determine effectiveness and risks. The number of sprays alone therefore says too little. Useful records connect applications with disease observations, weather and protection of workers and the environment. [@source.oiv-plant-protection-good-practices-2018]

A mechanical operation also requires labour, equipment and energy. Include working hours, available skills, safety and operating costs in the comparison. This makes it possible to assess whether a change can be maintained and who bears the additional workload.
:::

:::section{#omstandigheden-en-variatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.abad-cover-crops-agronomic-performance-2021 source.van-leeuwen-climate-adaptation-2017 source.oiv-plant-protection-good-practices-2018"}
## Conditions and variation

[[concept.climate-weather-site-microclimate|Climate, weather and microclimate]] alter the balance. During a wet growing season, fungal risk and access to the vineyard may require considerable attention. During a dry season, competition for water from cover crops becomes more important. The same cover may consequently be retained longer in one parcel, terminated earlier in another or used in fewer alleys. [@source.abad-cover-crops-agronomic-performance-2021]

Soil depth, planting material and management history also matter. Compare a newly sown strip with conditions before the change and with a suitable reference, such as a comparable strip retaining the earlier management. Allow for differences between years. A wet year can mask a water problem; one harvest reveals only part of the consequences of a long-term change.
:::

:::section{#keuzes-en-afwegingen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-sustainability-implementation-2020 source.van-leeuwen-climate-adaptation-2017 source.oiv-sustainable-water-viticulture-2018"}
## Decisions and trade-offs

A workable improvement plan has a specific goal, an initial measurement, a measure to implement, a responsible person and a review date. If erosion control is the goal, for example, record both ground cover and evidence of soil loss using consistent methods at comparable times. Include costs and unwanted effects. The OIV recommends a repeated cycle of planning, doing, checking and adjusting. [@source.oiv-sustainability-implementation-2020; section 3.2]

**Adaptation** means adjusting to changing conditions. **Mitigation** means limiting the contribution to climate change, for example by reducing emissions. Shading bunches can be an adaptation to heat; using less fossil energy is mitigation. The two aims may work together but require separate assessment.

For a grower, options range from different [[concept.pruning-training-canopy-crop-load|canopy management]] and ground cover to suitable planting material, [[concept.rootstock|rootstocks]] and a different layout at replanting. Water availability, investment costs, local rules and the intended grape profile determine which options are useful. [@source.van-leeuwen-climate-adaptation-2017]
:::

:::detail{#adaptatie-over-meerdere-oogsten parent="keuzes-en-afwegingen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.van-leeuwen-climate-adaptation-2017 source.oiv-sustainability-implementation-2020"}
### Decisions spanning several harvests

A change in the timing of canopy work can be tested within a season. A different variety, rootstock or row layout commits the business for longer. Compare these choices under several plausible future conditions, preserving opportunities to adjust where possible. Van Leeuwen and Destrac-Irvine distinguish changes in cultivation techniques and planting material as routes to adaptation. [@source.van-leeuwen-climate-adaptation-2017]

Resilience is the ability to withstand disruption and recover. An improvement plan therefore also asks what happens during a dry vintage, rising costs or staff shortages. A measure that is workable only under ideal conditions needs an alternative.
:::

## Global context

A label must be read within its jurisdiction. **In the EU**, organic wine rules cover grape production and processing, with limits on sulphites. **In the United States**, added sulphites place grape wine in the *made with organic grapes* category, provided the other requirements are met; it may not carry the USDA organic seal. The same vineyard practice consequently does not lead to identical wine labelling everywhere. [@source.ec-organic-production-products] [@source.usda-organic-alcohol]

**Sustainable Winegrowing New Zealand (SWNZ)** illustrates a broader programme approach. Its May 2026 handbook covers climate, water, waste, soil, plant protection and people. Annual submissions and independent site audits form part of certification. This shows how a programme follows several parts of the business; the precise requirements remain those of SWNZ. [@source.swnz-certification-handbook-2026; Sustainability Focus Areas and The certification process]

These examples describe different kinds of assurance. Local conditions remain central when choosing an agricultural measure: an approach from a humid area needs reassessment in a dry one.
:::

:::section{#bewijs-en-grenzen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.doering-organic-biodynamic-review-2019 source.newton-regenerative-definitions-2020"}
## Evidence and limits

Research compares measurable outcomes. Döring and colleagues' 2019 systematic review found greater biodiversity under organic management in many of the comparisons examined, but no general difference in berry, juice or wine quality among the systems studied. This supports a specific statement about measured characteristics, rather than an encompassing verdict on every vineyard. [@source.doering-organic-biodynamic-review-2019]

A strong sustainability claim identifies the goal, measure, measurement method, starting point and period. Also consider what the measurement leaves out. The word regenerative becomes testable when connected with an explicit definition and evidence for the intended outcome.
:::

:::detail{#proef-en-bedrijfssysteem parent="bewijs-en-grenzen" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.rienth-biodynamic-preparations-2023"}
### A preparations trial is a bounded comparison

In a Swiss vineyard, Rienth and colleagues compared biodynamic preparations 500 and 501 with an organic control. Across 2016–2020, they found no significant differences for most measured vine and berry parameters; photosynthesis differed at one measurement time. The soil physical properties and fungal diversity examined also showed no significant differences. [@source.rienth-biodynamic-preparations-2023]

The trial is useful because it tests the additional treatment separately. Its scope remains the preparations used, location, duration and variables measured. The authors did not investigate a complete biodynamic farm programme. Claims about the whole system also require consideration of compost, soil management, other operations and longer timescales.
:::

:::detail{#koolstof-en-systeemgrenzen parent="bewijs-en-grenzen" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.fao-soil-carbon-mrv-protocol-2020 source.oiv-ghg-inventory-2015"}
### Carbon stocks and footprints have different boundaries

The percentage of organic carbon in a soil sample is a concentration. The **stock** also accounts for the amount of soil per unit area and the depth sampled. Establishing change reliably requires comparable sampling, bulk density, an initial measurement and repeated observations. The FAO measurement protocol sets out these elements and comparison with a baseline scenario. [@source.fao-soil-carbon-mrv-protocol-2020; definitions and monitoring]

A greenhouse gas footprint also tracks emissions within an agreed **system boundary**: cultivation alone, production to the winery gate or the entire product chain. The OIV distinguishes business and product calculations and requires explicit boundaries. Energy, inputs, packaging and transport may make different contributions within them. [@source.oiv-ghg-inventory-2015]

Compare like units too. Emissions per hectare and emissions per quantity of product answer different questions: a change in harvest size alters the relationship between them. An increase in soil carbon consequently cannot be equated with the climate performance of a bottle without an appropriately combined calculation.
:::

## Significance for wine

A producer's account becomes more informative when it connects operations with goals. Ask which parcels or products a claim covers, what verification exists and what has been established over several seasons. This makes both modest, well-documented improvements and ambitious programmes understandable.

The connection to wine runs through vine health, water and nutrient status, yield and [[concept.berry-development-ripeness|berry development and ripeness]], followed by harvest and cellar decisions. A farming label does not predict this entire chain. Use it as an entry point to practices and evidence, and assess wine style through the wine itself.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Read a sustainability claim in three steps: what was done, what was verified and what was measured? Together with place, time and business conditions, these answers give meaning to a farming system.
:::
