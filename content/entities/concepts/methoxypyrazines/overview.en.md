**Methoxypyrazines** are potent aroma compounds that may suggest capsicum, green bean, leaf or herbs in grapes and wine. They play a recognisable role in varieties including [[grape.sauvignon-blanc|Sauvignon Blanc]], [[grape.cabernet-sauvignon|Cabernet Sauvignon]], [[grape.cabernet-franc|Cabernet Franc]] and [[grape.carmenere|Carménère]]. “Green”, however, is broader than this one family and is not automatically a fault. [@source.awri-green-flavour-cabernet-sauvignon]
:::

:::section{#betekenis-en-herkenning depth="foundation" source_refs="source.zhao-methoxypyrazines-review-2019"}
## Meaning and recognition — much aroma from very little material

Methoxypyrazines are a group of aromatic nitrogen compounds with very low perception thresholds. An extremely small amount may therefore be detectable. **IBMP** is studied particularly often in wine; its long chemical name matters less than the insight that one potent compound can leave a large sensory trace. [@source.zhao-methoxypyrazines-review-2019]

How that trace is experienced depends on concentration and context. A subtle herbal or leafy note may support freshness and varietal recognition; dominant capsicum may obscure fruit. The transition has no universal boundary for every wine or taster.
:::

:::detail{#groen-is-geen-molecuul parent="betekenis-en-herkenning" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.awri-green-flavour-cabernet-sauvignon"}
### “Green” is not a molecule

Tasters also use green for grass, mint, eucalyptus, stems, herbs, unripe tannin or high acidity. AWRI research on Cabernet Sauvignon shows that different volatile and non-volatile components may prompt these words. A green tasting note therefore does not prove that methoxypyrazines are the cause. [@source.awri-green-flavour-cabernet-sauvignon]

Conversely, measurable IBMP need not leap from the glass as capsicum. Fruit, wood, [[concept.tannin|tannin]], [[concept.acidity|acidity]] and other aromas influence what draws attention and the concentration at which the compound becomes recognisable.
:::

:::section{#in-de-druif depth="foundation" source_refs="source.zhao-methoxypyrazines-review-2019"}
## In the grape — built before ripening, diminished along the way

Methoxypyrazines form in the green tissues and berries of the grapevine. Their concentration in grapes generally rises before véraison — the onset of colour change and softening — and then falls towards harvest. Very early or unevenly ripe fruit may consequently carry a more pronounced vegetal character. [@source.zhao-methoxypyrazines-review-2019]

“Riper” does not automatically mean “zero”. Variety, season and site each provide a different starting point and pace. In some wines, a modest green line belongs to their recognisable balance.
:::

:::detail{#licht-temperatuur-en-blad parent="in-de-druif" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.zhao-methoxypyrazines-review-2019"}
### Light, temperature and foliage work together

Bunch exposure and temperature are closely entangled within a canopy. Research often connects greater light around berries with lower methoxypyrazines, but timing, variety, heat and experimental design influence the result. Leaf removal is therefore no simple chemical switch. [@source.zhao-methoxypyrazines-review-2019]

An open bunch zone may promote ripening and drying, yet also cause scorching and heating under strong sun. The grower does not steer one aroma compound alone: health, acidity, fruit, water and protection move with it.
:::

:::section{#in-de-wijn depth="foundation" source_refs="source.zhao-methoxypyrazines-review-2019 source.awri-green-flavour-cabernet-sauvignon"}
## In the wine — concentration gains meaning inside a mixture

During winemaking, methoxypyrazines can enter must and wine from grapes and other plant material. They then sit with considerable potency among hundreds of other volatile compounds. Perception depends not only on their concentration, but on the surrounding aromatic and structural setting.

In Sauvignon Blanc, green methoxypyrazines may sit beside tropically scented [[concept.volatile-thiols|volatile thiols]]. In dark-skinned varieties they mingle with fruit, tannin and possibly oak. The same compound carries different weight in each whole.
:::

:::detail{#stijlkenmerk-of-onrijpheid parent="in-de-wijn" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.awri-green-flavour-cabernet-sauvignon source.zhao-methoxypyrazines-review-2019"}
### Style marker or underripeness is a question for the whole wine

Calling every green note “unripe” is too simple. A wine may have sufficient sugar, acidity and tannin ripeness while retaining a recognisable herbal line. Conversely, hard texture may feel unripe without methoxypyrazines playing the leading role.

A careful judgement therefore asks three questions: what exactly do I smell or feel, how strong is it beside fruit and structure, and does that balance suit the origin and intended style? The molecule's name cannot answer a quality question by itself.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Methoxypyrazines can give wine a powerful green voice, but “green” is a much larger language. Variety and ripening determine how much potential the grape carries; the full aromatic mixture determines whether that voice sounds fresh, characteristic or dominant.
:::
