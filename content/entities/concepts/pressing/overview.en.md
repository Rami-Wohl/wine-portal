:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-pressing"}
Pressing separates liquid from the solid parts of a grape or from the mass of skins left after red-wine fermentation. The same word can therefore describe two very different points in the winery route: before fermentation for many white and rosé wines, or after it for red wine.
:::

:::section{#wat-de-pers-scheidt depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-pressing"}
## What pressing separates

A wine press applies controlled pressure to whole or crushed grapes, or to the solid mass of skins, seeds and sometimes stems. Liquid released before fermentation is **must**; after red fermentation on skins, it is press wine. The OIV includes both purposes within the same winery operation. [@source.oiv-pressing]

A press is therefore not meant to grind grapes into purée. Slow, progressive pressure helps release juice or wine without unnecessarily breaking seeds and other solid tissue.
:::

:::section{#moment-in-de-route depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-pressing"}
## Pressing before or after fermentation

In the usual white-wine route, grapes are pressed early so that the juice ferments largely without skins. For rosé, black grapes generally receive brief skin contact before the pink juice is separated and fermented off skins. Red wine instead ferments with skins and is normally pressed afterwards.

That order explains why pressing cannot be understood apart from [[concept.maceration|maceration]] and [[concept.extraction|extraction]]. The moment of separation determines how long liquid and grape solids can affect one another.
:::

:::section{#fracties-en-druk depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.oiv-pressing"}
## Free-run liquid, press fractions and pressure

Liquid that runs from grapes or fermented skins with little pressure can be kept apart from what is released later under compression. As pressure and pressing time rise, successive fractions may differ in composition. Winemakers can taste, analyse and hold them separately before deciding what belongs in the final wine.

Yield is therefore not the only aim. The pressing programme, pressure increase, loading and condition of the fruit are chosen to achieve the intended separation.
:::

:::in-the-glass{#waarom-in-het-glas depth="foundation"}
Pressing time and selected fractions can contribute to differences in colour, tannin structure, bitterness, texture and aromatic clarity. Their effect operates together with grape variety, ripeness, skin contact and later winery choices.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Pressing is a controlled separation. The central question is not how hard a press can squeeze, but which liquid is separated from which grape solids, and when.
:::
