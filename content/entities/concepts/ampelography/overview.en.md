:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-grape-descriptors-2023"}
**Ampelography** is the systematic description and comparison of grape varieties on the vine. Not one attractive leaf, but a pattern of shoot, leaf, flower, bunch, berry and seed helps identify a variety. The method gives names a firm footing in a plant world where local synonyms and closely resembling varieties readily cause confusion. [@source.oiv-grape-descriptors-2023]
:::

:::section{#wat-is-ampelografie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-grape-variety-identification-2019"}
## Meaning and purpose — learning to read the vine

Ampelography does for grape varieties what a careful field guide does for birds or plants: it records visible traits in consistent language so that observations can be compared. This supports vineyards, nurseries, collections and research. It helps investigate unknown material and check established plantings.

A description is not yet a certain identification. Observers first record what they see, then compare the profile with reliable references. Provenance records and genetic data may strengthen or correct the conclusion. [@source.oiv-grape-variety-identification-2019]
:::

:::detail{#beschrijven-is-niet-raden parent="wat-is-ampelografie" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.oiv-grape-variety-identification-2019"}
### Describing is not guessing

An ampelographer observes at suitable growth stages and uses several vines. A young shoot tip, mature leaf and ripe berry do not appear at the same moment. Traits are recorded before a name is chosen, so that an early hunch does not completely direct the observation.

This makes the method repeatable, but not infallible. Experience is still required to avoid mistaking normal variation, disease or damage for a variety trait.
:::

:::section{#kenmerken-lezen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-grape-descriptors-2023"}
## Characteristics — from shoot tip to grape seed

Leaves are conspicuous, but form only one chapter. A description may examine colour and hairiness of the young shoot, shape and lobes of the mature leaf, the flower's sexual organs, bunch size and density, and the berry's shape, colour, flavour and seeds.

The combination provides discrimination. Two varieties may have almost identical leaves yet differ in shoot hair or bunch architecture. Conversely, drought may alter leaf and berry size without changing genetic identity.
:::

:::detail{#een-gedeelde-woordenschat parent="kenmerken-lezen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.oiv-grape-descriptors-2023"}
### A shared vocabulary

The OIV assigns codes and defined states to characteristics. This lets observers record a “densely hairy” shoot or “deeply lobed” leaf against the same reference even when they work in different countries. The list contains both priority descriptors for quick characterisation and supplementary detail. [@source.oiv-grape-descriptors-2023]

Not every trait carries equal weight. Quantitative properties such as dimensions respond relatively strongly to environment and cultivation. Identification therefore rests on a carefully chosen whole, not the longest possible form.
:::

:::section{#grenzen-en-dna depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-grape-variety-identification-2019 source.inrae-vassal-ampelography"}
## Limits and DNA — looking remains useful, evidence goes further

Season, soil, pruning, nutrition, virus disease and damage can alter a vine's appearance. Close relatives often resemble one another as well. A photograph without ripe bunches, several leaves and field context is therefore rarely enough to name a variety with certainty.

Modern collections combine ampelographic description with traceable records and DNA analysis. INRAE Vassal uses both morphological traits and molecular markers to identify and trace plant material. [@source.inrae-vassal-ampelography]
:::

:::detail{#dna-vervangt-de-wijngaard-niet parent="grenzen-en-dna" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.oiv-grape-variety-identification-2019"}
### DNA does not replace the vineyard

Genetic markers can show that two samples represent the same variety or investigate family relationships. They are especially powerful when names conflict or related varieties look very similar. The OIV protocol therefore treats genetic and ampelographic characters as parts of one identification process. [@source.oiv-grape-variety-identification-2019]

DNA does not tell us how vigorously a vine grows this season, how healthy it is or how its grapes ripen on a particular site. Ampelography remains necessary to understand the plant as a plant. The methods do not compete: together they answer more than either can alone.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
A grape variety is not identified from one leaf. Ampelography builds a verifiable profile from several parts of the vine; records and DNA help attach that profile to the right identity.
:::
