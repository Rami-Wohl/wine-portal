:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-terroir-definition-2010"}
**Terroir** is not a grand word for soil. It describes how a place and the people growing wine there can together give what grows and is made a recognisable character. Climate, soil, topography, living environment and viticultural practice are all part of that whole. None tells a wine's complete story on its own. [@source.oiv-terroir-definition-2010]
:::

:::section{#plaats-en-mensen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-terroir-definition-2010"}
## Place and people

Walk between two rows of vines: the ground holds water or lets it drain away, a slope meets sun and wind differently from a valley, and a grower chooses where, what and how to plant. That interplay is what wine people mean by terroir. The International Organisation of Vine and Wine also includes landscape, biodiversity and accumulated, shared know-how. The idea is as much about learning to work *with* a place as about the place itself. [@source.oiv-terroir-definition-2010]

Terroir is neither exclusively French nor confined to famous vineyards. Every winegrowing place has conditions and practices; the question is which differences can demonstrably matter to grapes and wine.
:::

## How the factors work together

Climate shapes the seasons a grape variety can usually complete. Soil affects, among other things, water available to roots; elevation, slope and aspect can alter temperature, light and drainage. The grower then chooses, for example, variety, [[concept.rootstock|rootstock]] and ways of working. A vineyard is not a sum of isolated ingredients: the same soil can behave very differently under another climate or management approach. [@source.oiv-terroir-definition-2010] [@source.white-soil-terroir-2020]

One growing season's weather is different again from a place's longer-term climate. So [[concept.vintage|vintage]] and terroir are not competing explanations. One tells us when the grapes grew; the other helps explain why that place responded to that year as it did. [@source.oiv-climate-variability-2015] [@source.oiv-terroir-definition-2010]
:::

:::detail{#van-bodem-naar-druif parent="samenwerking-factoren" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.white-soil-terroir-2020 source.oiv-terroir-definition-2010"}
### From soil to grape is not a straight line

Soil can affect water supply, root growth and nutrients. That influences vine growth and may affect grape ripening. But between a soil profile and the taste in the glass lie variety, weather, vineyard work and winemaking. To say that a wine simply ‘tastes of limestone because it grew on limestone’ skips those steps. Research on terroir must try to disentangle these influences. [@source.white-soil-terroir-2020]
:::

:::section{#herkomst-en-bewijs depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-terroir-definition-2010"}
## Terroir and origin designation

An [[concept.aop|AOP]] is a protected origin designation with a legally delimited area and production rules. Terroir describes the interplay of environment and practice. The two often relate, but are not identical: a line on a map does not prove that every vineyard inside it tastes the same, and terroir does not stop at a legal border. The OIV explicitly warns against confusing its descriptive definition with a geographical indication. [@source.oiv-terroir-definition-2010]

Use terroir as a question, then, not a magic word: which features of this place and which human choices help explain this wine?
:::

:::detail{#toetsen-van-verschillen parent="herkomst-en-bewijs" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.oiv-terroir-definition-2010 source.white-soil-terroir-2020"}
### What can you actually demonstrate?

Comparisons grow stronger when variety, vintage and methods are as similar as possible. Even so, a vineyard can vary from plot to plot, and a tasting may leave several explanations open. Translating soil minerals directly into a ‘mineral’ taste is scientifically too simple. Terroir is valuable as an inquiry into causes and origin; it loses meaning when every character difference is attributed to soil without evidence. [@source.white-soil-terroir-2020] [@source.oiv-terroir-definition-2010]
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Terroir is the interplay of place, living environment and human practice. It helps investigate wine differences, but does not replace evidence or mean the same thing as a protected origin designation.
:::
