:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.champagne-disgorgement"}
At **disgorgement**, a producer removes yeast sediment from the neck of a sparkling-wine bottle. It follows [[concept.remuage|riddling]], which brought the sediment there. Pressure inside the bottle expels the deposit when the temporary closure is opened. [@source.champagne-disgorgement]
:::

:::section{#handeling depth="foundation" source_refs="source.champagne-disgorgement"}
## Process — from yeast deposit to clear wine

In the [[concept.traditional-method|traditional method]], wine has rested on its yeast after bottle fermentation. For disgorgement, the bottle is held neck-down with the deposit by its closure. Often the producer briefly freezes the neck: on opening, a plug of ice carries the sediment out. The bottle can then be topped up, sometimes with [[concept.dosage|dosage]], and given its final closure. [@source.champagne-disgorgement]
:::

:::detail{#varianten parent="handeling" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.champagne-disgorgement"}
### Freezing is common, not the definition

There is also *dégorgement à la volée*, done by hand without first freezing the neck. Both approaches remove the collected yeast deposit. The defining act is the removal of sediment, not the technique used to prepare the neck. [@source.champagne-disgorgement]
:::

:::section{#betekenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.champagne-disgorgement source.champagne-dosage"}
## Significance — a new moment in the wine's life

After disgorgement the wine no longer has prolonged contact with that yeast deposit. A disgorgement date therefore says something different from a [[concept.vintage|vintage]]: it marks the end of this period of bottle [[concept.lees-ageing|lees ageing]]. The final taste also depends on the base wine, time on lees, any dosage and storage afterwards. [@source.champagne-disgorgement] [@source.champagne-dosage]
:::

:::detail{#zuurstof parent="betekenis" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.champagne-disgorgement source.awri-wine-faults"}
### Opening the bottle requires care

The wine briefly meets air during this operation. Producers seek to limit unwanted oxygen pickup and wine loss; closure and storage also shape how the wine develops afterwards. A later disgorgement date is therefore not a simple quality ranking. [@source.champagne-disgorgement] [@source.awri-wine-faults]
:::

:::key-idea{#kern depth="foundation"}
Riddling collects yeast sediment; disgorgement removes it, just before the bottle is finished.
:::
