Clarification makes cloudy must or wine clearer by allowing suspended particles to settle or by removing them. **Fining** is a targeted clarification or corrective treatment in which an aid binds or gathers components so that the resulting material can then be separated.
:::

:::section{#helder-worden depth="foundation" source_refs="source.awri-pre-packaging source.oiv-filtration-wine"}
## From sediment to clear wine

Particles from grapes, yeast and other winery processes can remain suspended in young wine. Time and gravity allow some of them to settle. By racking clear wine away from the sediment, a winemaker can separate wine and deposit. Centrifugation or [[concept.bottling|filtration before bottling]] can accelerate or refine that separation.

Clarity and stability are related but not identical. A wine that looks clear may still contain proteins, crystal-forming substances or microorganisms that later cause change; a deliberately unfiltered wine may nevertheless be sufficiently stable for its intended route.
:::

:::section{#wat-fining-doet depth="foundation" source_refs="source.awri-fining-agents source.oiv-fining-practices"}
## What a fining agent does

Fining introduces a carefully selected processing aid to must or wine. It may attach to particular components through electrical or chemical interaction or through adsorption. The larger particles or complexes settle or are filtered, after which the deposit is removed. [@source.oiv-fining-practices]

The purpose may be clarity, but also reducing excessive bitterness or astringency, removing unstable proteins or correcting oxidative discolouration. Different aims call for different agents.
:::

:::section{#proefbehandeling depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.awri-fining-agents source.oiv-fining-practices"}
## Test first, then treat

A fining agent does not act identically in every wine. Agent, dose, preparation, temperature, pH and wine composition all affect the result. Small bench trials are therefore commonly carried out before a treatment is scaled to the full lot.

Too little may miss the objective; too much can also remove desirable aroma, colour or structural components. The material formed by the treatment must then be separated carefully from the wine.
:::

:::section{#filtratie-is-anders depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-filtration-wine"}
## Fining and filtration are different operations

Fining allows an added aid to react with wine components. Filtration passes wine through a suitable filter medium or membrane that retains particles. Filtration can provide clarity and, when designed for it, remove microorganisms. [@source.oiv-filtration-wine]

A wine may undergo both, one or neither. The decision follows from the wine's condition, intended presentation, microbiological risk and subsequent packaging and storage.
:::

:::in-the-glass{#waarom-in-het-glas depth="foundation"}
Clarification and fining can affect clarity, texture, colour and aromatic purity. A controlled treatment aims to solve a defined problem while preserving as much of the intended character as possible.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Clarification is an objective; fining and filtration are possible tools. Appropriate preparation begins with what this wine needs, not a fixed list of compulsory cellar operations.
:::
