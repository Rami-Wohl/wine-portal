:::summary{#wat-is-assemblage depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-blending-code"}
In **blending**, a winemaker brings different wines together into a new, final composition. [@source.oiv-blending-code] The word therefore describes a choice in the cellar — not automatically the number of grape varieties in the bottle.
:::

:::section{#wat-kan-verschillen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.eu-wine-coupage-regulation-2019 source.civb-bordeaux-blending"}
## Different paths into one wine

Components may have been kept separate for many reasons. They can come from different grape varieties, places or harvest years. European wine rules use precisely those differences to define certain forms of mixing. [@source.eu-wine-coupage-regulation-2019]

Separate lots can also arise within a single [[concept.vintage|vintage]] and estate. In [[region.bordeaux|Bordeaux]], grapes or parcels are regularly fermented apart and tasted side by side later. [@source.civb-bordeaux-blending] A blend can therefore contain several varieties, but it can also unite different lots of the same variety.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Blending is not primarily about a fixed recipe. It is the decision about which separate components will form the final wine together.
:::

:::section{#proeven-en-kiezen depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.oiv-blending-code source.civb-bordeaux-blending"}
## Taste, compare, choose

While components remain separate, the winemaker can compare them and decide which combination suits the intended wine. The international wine organisation states the purpose in broad terms: composing a wine with the desired characteristics, within the rules that apply to it. [@source.oiv-blending-code]

This makes blending more than adding up percentages. The ratio is the visible result of a series of tasting and selection choices. In another year, the available components — and therefore the final combination — may be different. The Bordeaux wine council accordingly describes its regional practice as a recurring process of making components separately, tasting them and bringing them together. [@source.civb-bordeaux-blending]
:::

:::section{#wat-percentages-niet-vertellen depth="advanced" source_refs="source.eu-wine-coupage-regulation-2019 source.civb-bordeaux-blending"}
## What percentages leave unsaid

A ratio such as 70% [[grape.merlot|Merlot]] and 30% [[grape.cabernet-sauvignon|Cabernet Sauvignon]] states only the division between grape varieties. It does not say which parcels supplied the components, how each lot was made, when they were brought together or what selection preceded the final blend.

Conversely, a single-variety wine need not come from one single lot. Separate parcels or cellar lots of the same variety can also be united later. [@source.civb-bordeaux-blending] Grape percentages are useful information, but they are not a complete explanation of style or quality.
:::

:::caveat{#taal-en-regels depth="advanced" variant="exception" source_refs="source.oiv-blending-code source.eu-wine-coupage-regulation-2019"}
Everyday wine language sometimes uses `blending` more broadly than a specific legal rule does. The OIV defines it as mixing different wines; European rules for `coupage` also mention must and distinguish, among other things, origin, grape variety and harvest year. [@source.oiv-blending-code] [@source.eu-wine-coupage-regulation-2019] For a particular appellation, its own product specification therefore remains decisive.
:::
