**Cuvée** is a word whose meaning changes with context. On a label it may identify a producer's particular wine or selection. In Champagne pressing it also specifically means the first portion of juice. The word alone guarantees neither special quality, a blend of grapes nor a production method. [@source.champagne-blending] [@source.champagne-pressing]
:::

:::section{#etiket depth="foundation" source_refs="source.champagne-blending"}
## On a label — a recognisable wine

A producer may use ‘cuvée’ for a distinct bottling: perhaps a particular selection, vineyard, [[concept.vintage|vintage]] or [[concept.assemblage|blend]] (a combination of different wine lots). Which is meant must be clear from the rest of the label or the producer's information. ‘Cuvée’ is not, by itself, a legal quality rank. [@source.champagne-blending]
:::

:::detail{#geen-garantie parent="etiket" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.champagne-blending"}
### A name does not reveal the recipe

A ‘special cuvée’ may be a special selection, but the term says nothing about how many varieties, vineyards or vintages it contains. A wine from one grape or one origin can carry the name too. Read the stated origin and composition, not an assumed meaning of the word. [@source.champagne-blending]
:::

:::section{#persen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.champagne-pressing"}
## At the press — a specific meaning in Champagne

In Champagne production, the first permitted press fraction is called the *cuvée*; juice obtained later is the *taille*. This concerns **which portion of the juice** comes from the press, not the name of a finished bottle. This technical meaning should not simply be projected onto other regions. [@source.champagne-pressing]
:::

:::detail{#context parent="persen" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.champagne-pressing source.champagne-blending"}
### One word, two decision points

The press fraction is produced early, before juice becomes wine. A named cuvée is a commercial or cellar selection of wine lots. Thus a producer can make a named cuvée from more than one press fraction or base wine, subject to the applicable rules. Context tells you which meaning is useful. [@source.champagne-pressing] [@source.champagne-blending]
:::

:::key-idea{#kern depth="foundation"}
When you read ‘cuvée’, ask: does this mean a **bottling**, or, in Champagne, the **first press fraction**?
:::
