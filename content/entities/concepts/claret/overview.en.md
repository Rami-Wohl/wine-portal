*Claret* has two lives. In English it has long been a word for Bordeaux wine, especially red Bordeaux. On a present-day French Bordeaux label, **Claret** is a specific additional mention for **light red** wine. It is not [[concept.clairet|Clairet]], the deep-rosé style. [@source.inao-bordeaux-claret-product-page] [@source.boagri-bordeaux-specification-2026]
:::

## From English wine word to Bordeaux mention

Trade with England made *claret* a familiar name for wine from [[region.bordeaux|Bordeaux]]. That historical English usage is broad: someone ordering claret in an old book is not necessarily asking for today's French category. The modern **Bordeaux Claret** mention, by contrast, has a defined meaning within the [[appellation.bordeaux|Bordeaux]] appellation. [@source.inao-bordeaux-claret-product-page]

The Bordeaux specification reserves the mention for light red wine. It is not a separate appellation, nor a casual English nickname that may be printed on any bottle. [@source.boagri-bordeaux-specification-2026]

## A lighter red style

The current Bordeaux description places claret among reds with less intense ruby-to-purple colour and a lighter [[concept.tannin|tannin]] structure. It describes a fruity wine suited to drinking young and, if desired, a little cool. That opens up another side of Bordeaux; it does not promise that every bottle tastes the same. [@source.boagri-bordeaux-specification-2026]

*Light red* does not mean rosé. It locates the wine within the red spectrum and the category under which it is produced.
:::

:::detail{#stijl-en-techniek parent="lichte-rode-stijl" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.boagri-bordeaux-specification-2026 source.oiv-maceration"}
### A style is not a fixed recipe

[[concept.maceration|Skin contact]] can shape colour and tannin, but *claret* does not tell you the exact number of hours in the vat. Grapes and cellar choices matter. For precise production conditions, consult the Bordeaux specification in force; a tasting description is not a technical standard. [@source.oiv-maceration]
:::

:::section{#clairet-is-anders depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
## Not to be confused with Clairet

The two words now even appear side by side in the Bordeaux rules. **Clairet** belongs to the deep-coloured rosés; **Claret** to the light reds. A glass may appear close to the visual boundary, but its legal category rests on production and the label mention, not our guess from its colour. [@source.boagri-bordeaux-specification-2026]
:::

:::detail{#huidige-regels parent="clairet-is-anders" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.legifrance-bordeaux-order-2026"}
### The current specification decides

The French order of 13 May 2026 approved a renewed Bordeaux specification. For a legal assessment of a particular bottle or technical limit, consult the final text applicable to that harvest and date. The historical English word and the modern French mention remain different meanings. [@source.legifrance-bordeaux-order-2026]
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
In an English text, *claret* can mean Bordeaux wine broadly. On a modern Bordeaux label, Claret is a defined light-red style; Clairet is rosé.
:::
