Sulfur dioxide, usually abbreviated to SO₂, is one of the principal protective tools in the winery. It helps control unwanted oxidation and microbial growth, but it is not a universal shield: timing, dose, wine composition, hygiene and oxygen management still work together.
:::

:::section{#waarom-wijnmakers-so2-gebruiken depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-sulphiting-wine source.awri-sulfur-dioxide"}
## Protection from oxygen and microorganisms

SO₂ is used at several stages of winemaking for its antioxidant and antimicrobial action. The OIV lists purposes including limiting unwanted yeasts and bacteria, inhibiting oxidative activity and use around bottling. [@source.oiv-sulphiting-wine]

The addition does not replace clean cellar work, sound grapes or careful transfers. It forms one part of a protection strategy in which temperature, oxygen exposure, clarity and microbiological condition also matter.
:::

:::section{#vrij-en-gebonden depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.awri-sulfur-dioxide"}
## Free and bound sulfur dioxide

SO₂ occurs in wine in free and bound forms. The free fraction supplies most of the desired antioxidant and antimicrobial protection. Part of an addition binds to other wine components and is no longer active in the same way. [@source.awri-sulfur-dioxide]

The balance depends in part on the wine's composition and history. Winemakers therefore assess not only how much SO₂ was once added, but measure free and total SO₂ at relevant stages.
:::

:::section{#ph-en-effectiviteit depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.awri-sulfur-dioxide"}
## Why pH changes effectiveness

The antimicrobial effectiveness of free SO₂ is closely related to wine [[concept.acidity|pH]]. At higher pH, a smaller share is present in the particularly active molecular form. The same measured free concentration therefore does not provide identical protection in every wine. [@source.awri-sulfur-dioxide]

That makes a fixed dose without wine context misleading. Intended protection, pH, wine style, residual sugar, microbiological condition, expected storage and local regulation must be considered together.
:::

:::section{#momenten-en-botteling depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-sulphiting-wine source.awri-sulfur-dioxide"}
## Adding and checking again

SO₂ may be used on grapes or must, after fermentation, during maturation and around bottling. The immediate purpose differs at each stage: controlling wild microbial activity, protecting fruit during a vulnerable operation or carrying a finished wine safely into bottle.

Transfers and bottling can introduce additional oxygen. Checks before and after such operations show whether the intended free protection actually remains. [@source.awri-sulfur-dioxide]
:::

:::in-the-glass{#waarom-in-het-glas depth="foundation"}
Well-judged protection helps preserve fresh aromas and colour and reduces the risk of microbial spoilage. Too little can leave a wine vulnerable; careless or excessive use may itself become noticeable. The appropriate approach therefore belongs to the particular wine.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Sulfur dioxide works as part of a system. Effective protection requires the right free concentration for the wine, combined with hygiene, oxygen management and checks throughout the winery route.
:::
