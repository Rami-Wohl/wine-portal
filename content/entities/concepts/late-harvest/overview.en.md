:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.dwi-praedikate source.vins-alsace-vendanges-tardives"}
In a **late harvest**, grapes remain on the vine longer than usual for the place, variety and intended wine style. That extra wait may bring further ripening, water loss or [[concept.botrytis|noble rot]], but none of those outcomes is automatic.

“Late harvest” is therefore not a worldwide recipe. On one label it is a legally defined category; on another it is chiefly a style description. Sweetness cannot be inferred from the words alone either.
:::

:::section{#later-is-relatief depth="foundation" source_refs="source.dwi-praedikate source.vins-alsace-vendanges-tardives"}
## Overview — later only exists relative to a normal harvest

A calendar date says little by itself. [[grape.riesling|Riesling]] in a cool valley, an early-ripening variety in a warm region and grapes for sparkling wine all have different normal harvest times. Late harvest means that a producer deliberately waits beyond the customary or desired ripeness point for a particular style.

That decision may concern one parcel, selected bunches or successive picking passes. The aim may be greater ripeness or concentration, but nature helps decide whether waiting actually delivers the desired fruit.
:::

:::section{#wat-langer-hangen-verandert depth="foundation" source_refs="source.sanmartin-postharvest-water-loss-2021 source.penn-state-grape-sour-rot"}
## The grape — ripening further, drying or becoming infected

As long as vine and weather allow, flavour and phenolic maturity may continue to develop. Meanwhile, acidity often falls and pH can rise. Beyond a certain point, a higher sugar reading may chiefly reflect berries losing water rather than the plant supplying sugar indefinitely.

Longer hang time opens other routes too. Dry air can produce [[concept.passerillage|on-vine passerillage]]; suitable alternating humid and dry weather may allow noble rot; persistent moisture may instead cause harmful grey or sour rot. Late harvest names the timing, not the route taken.
:::

:::detail{#rijpheid-loopt-niet-gelijk parent="wat-langer-hangen-verandert" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.sanmartin-postharvest-water-loss-2021 source.penn-state-grape-sour-rot"}
### Sugar, acid, aroma and skins do not share one clock

Sugar concentration, acidity, aroma precursors, [[concept.tannin|tannin maturity]] and berry health do not change at the same rate. Heat may raise sugar quickly as freshness recedes; cooler weather can slow development; rain may dilute berries, split them or encourage rot.

The decision therefore requires tasting, measuring and looking. Following sugar alone can leave a producer waiting while chiefly pH and health risks rise. “Longer” is only better when the whole grape moves closer to the intended wine.
:::

:::detail{#concentratie-zonder-verdere-rijping parent="wat-langer-hangen-verandert" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.sanmartin-postharvest-water-loss-2021"}
### Greater concentration need not mean further physiological ripening

When water evaporates, sugar concentration per litre of juice rises without proportional new sugar production. Aromas, acids and phenolics may meanwhile concentrate, decline or change through stress responses. The word *overripe* can therefore blur plant ripening and physical dehydration.

Precise comparison requires berry weight, acid, pH, health and sensory assessment as well as must weight. One high number does not explain how it arose.
:::

## Viticulture — every extra day is still spent outdoors

A ripe bunch remains exposed to rain, hail, birds, insects, frost and fungi. Berries soften and may split after rainfall. Penn State warns that sour rot can increase when harvest is delayed in pursuit of higher sugar while chiefly acidity falls and pH rises. [@source.penn-state-grape-sour-rot]

Producers can remove damaged fruit or make several passes to pick only suitable bunches and berries. That raises labour costs and often lowers final yield. A late harvest is not simply free extra ripeness.
:::

:::detail{#jaar-en-perceel-beslissen-mee parent="wachten-is-een-risico" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.penn-state-grape-sour-rot"}
### [[concept.vintage|Vintage]] and parcel help make the decision

A dry autumn may reward patience; an approaching rain front may make earlier picking prudent. Bunch density, skin strength, canopy, wind and local humidity alter the risk even within one estate.

Late harvest is therefore an annual selection rather than a fixed quality ladder. Abandoning the style in a difficult year can be the better viticultural decision.
:::

:::section{#van-rijpe-druif-naar-wijn depth="foundation" source_refs="source.sanmartin-postharvest-water-loss-2021"}
## Winemaking — harvest time shapes the raw material, not all residual sugar

Richer grapes give yeast more sugar to convert into alcohol. If fermentation continues until almost all sugar has disappeared, a late harvest may finish dry. If the producer stops fermentation or yeast stalls in highly concentrated must, a sweet wine remains.

The final balance also depends on acidity, alcohol, any botrytis, [[concept.extraction|extraction]] and [[concept.elevage|maturation]]. “Late harvested” therefore describes an important starting point, not a complete tasting note.
:::

:::detail{#vergisting-onder-druk parent="van-rijpe-druif-naar-wijn" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.sanmartin-postharvest-water-loss-2021"}
### Rich must can ferment slowly or incompletely

High sugar places yeast under osmotic pressure; later, rising alcohol inhibits it too. Sound fruit, sufficient nutrients, temperature control and a suitable yeast population become especially important.

A stopped fermentation may be intended, but an unexpectedly stuck fermentation is not a style choice. Wine containing residual sugar must also be stabilised so that yeast does not restart later in cask or bottle.
:::

:::section{#etiket-en-herkomst depth="foundation" source_refs="source.dwi-praedikate source.dwi-wine-law-seminar-handbook source.vins-alsace-vendanges-tardives source.wosa-wine-styles"}
## Label — the same translation, different rules

In Germany, [[concept.spatlese|Spätlese]] is a Prädikat tied to grape must weight and ripe fruit harvested somewhat later. It does not by itself represent one sweetness level. [@source.dwi-praedikate] [@source.dwi-wine-law-seminar-handbook] In [[region.alsace|Alsace]], *Vendanges Tardives* is a regulated mention for overripe grapes of Riesling, [[grape.muscat-blanc-a-petits-grains|Muscat]], [[grape.pinot-gris|Pinot Gris]] or [[grape.gewurztraminer|Gewürztraminer]], with its own requirements. [@source.vins-alsace-vendanges-tardives]

South Africa uses *Late Harvest* within its own legal system of wine styles and residual sugar. [@source.wosa-wine-styles] The ordinary translation “late harvest” does not make these categories legally or stylistically identical.
:::

:::detail{#geen-internationaal-predikaat parent="etiket-en-herkomst" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.dwi-praedikate source.vins-alsace-vendanges-tardives source.wosa-wine-styles"}
### Origin rules come before dictionary meaning

German Spätlese is classified through grape ripeness; Alsace Vendanges Tardives constrains appellation, varieties, sugar at harvest and maturation before release, among other things; the South African term belongs to yet another labelling framework. Even when all three labels are translated as “late harvest”, they do not measure precisely the same thing.

Read country and origin first, the protected term second, and only then clues to sweetness. A historic bottle does not automatically show today's rules either.
:::

:::section{#in-het-glas depth="foundation" source_refs="source.dwi-praedikate source.vins-alsace-vendanges-tardives"}
## In the glass — ripeness is a spectrum, not a fixed profile

Late-harvest wine may combine citrus and flowers with ripe peach, tropical fruit, honey or dried fruit. It may be light and precise or opulent and sweet; botrytis can add another aromatic layer, but need not be present.

Acidity is often key to tension, especially with substantial residual sugar. Yet no aroma proves harvest timing: variety, site, fermentation and bottle age can create similar impressions.
:::

:::caveat{#foto-is-geen-definitie depth="advanced" variant="uncertainty"}
Shrivelled berries are one possible result of extended hang time, not the definition of late harvest. A healthy, plump bunch may be picked late; an early-damaged bunch may already be wrinkled. Only harvest context and production rules make the term verifiable.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Late harvest is a decision to leave ripe grapes in the vineyard longer, exchanging possible extra ripeness or concentration for additional risk. What appears in the bottle is then determined by grape, weather, selection, fermentation and the rules behind the label.
:::
