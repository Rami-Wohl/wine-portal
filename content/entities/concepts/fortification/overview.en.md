:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-fortification"}
Fortification means adding alcohol to must or wine. Timing matters: during fermentation it can retain grape sugar, whereas fortifying after complete fermentation can also produce a dry wine. Fortified and sweet therefore do not mean the same thing.
:::

:::section{#overzicht depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-fortification source.oiv-liqueur-wine"}
## Overview

The [[concept.fortified-wines|fortified wine family page]] connects this operation with sweetness, ageing and particular wine families.

In fortification, some alcohol comes from an added spirit or another permitted alcohol source. This distinguishes the process from high alcohol content obtained solely through [[concept.fermentation|fermentation]]. The permitted source depends on product rules.

This page focuses on addition timing.
:::

:::section{#werking depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-liqueur-wine source.ivdp-port-vinification source.sherry-fortification"}
## How it works

Addition increases the mixture’s alcohol content. During active fermentation, this change can stop yeast activity, leaving remaining grape sugar unfermented. Port provides a concrete example. This explanation supplies no universal alcohol threshold or dosing recipe.

| Timing | Starting material | Significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before fermentation | Unfermented must | Sugar has not yet been converted by fermentation |
| During fermentation | Partially fermented must | Interruption can retain grape sugar |
| After fermentation | Fully fermented wine | Fortification need not produce sweetness |

The OIV definition of liqueur wine includes these three starting materials. For dry Sherry, fully fermented base wine is fortified. The table organizes possible timings; it does not grant permission for every product type.
:::

:::section{#omstandigheden-en-variatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.ivdp-port-vinification source.sherry-ageing"}
## Conditions and variation

Sugar still present at alcohol addition helps explain Port’s sweetness. Subsequent [[concept.elevage|ageing]] changes the character further. Addition timing and ageing environment deserve separate attention.
:::

:::detail{#rijping-na-versterken parent="omstandigheden-en-variatie" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.sherry-fortification source.sherry-ageing"}
### Biological and oxidative ageing

In Sherry, the chosen fortification supports ageing under flor or without this yeast layer. The [[concept.fortified-wines|family page]] explains biological and [[concept.oxidation|oxidative ageing]] and the separate solera system.
:::

:::section{#toepassing-en-keuzes depth="foundation" source_refs="source.ivdp-port-vinification source.sherry-fortification source.champagne-dosage"}
## Application and decisions

First ask whether the aim is retaining sugar during fermentation or fortifying fully fermented base wine. Port and dry Sherry make this choice visible. Product rules and intended ageing path determine implementation; one product’s process is not a general recipe for another.

[[concept.dosage|Dosage]] is a different operation: adjustment after disgorgement in the traditional sparkling route. It does not name the purpose of fortification.
:::

:::section{#bewijs-en-grenzen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-fortification source.oiv-liqueur-wine"}
## Evidence and limits

The OIV describes fortification for special wine categories and distinguishes it from simply increasing the alcohol content of ordinary wine. That technical recommendation does not replace local law or an appellation specification. Without further information, 'fortified' does not say which alcohol source, quantity or ageing period is permitted.
:::

:::section{#betekenis-voor-wijn depth="foundation" source_refs="source.ivdp-port-vinification source.sherry-fortification"}
## Significance for wine

Read added alcohol, residual sugar and ageing as separate pieces of information. This explains why sweet Port and dry Fino can both be fortified. Fortification can also overlap with dried grapes, as the [[concept.passito|passito]] page shows through an Italian example.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Fortification describes alcohol addition. Timing helps explain sugar retention; the ageing route explains other changes.
:::
