**Grand vin** is the principal wine through which an estate presents itself. In [[region.bordeaux|Bordeaux]], it is often the wine bearing the château name without an added qualifier, assembled by the producer from the parcels and lots selected for its primary [[concept.assemblage|blend]]. Which lots qualify may change by estate and [[concept.vintage|vintage]].

The words sound like an official distinction, but do not by themselves say that the wine is a *grand cru* or *cru classé*. Above all, they describe its place within the producer's range. [@source.chateau-latour-grand-vin]
:::

## Meaning and use — the estate's principal wine

A grand vin is generally the wine an estate regards as its foremost expression. It sits at the top of the producer's own selection, which is not the same as occupying the top of a legal or regional ranking. An unclassified estate may speak of its grand vin, while a classified château may make several other wines.

Not every producer displays the term prominently on the label. Sometimes the château name alone identifies the main wine. Read *grand vin* as a portfolio designation in context, not as a freestanding seal of quality.
:::

:::detail{#geen-synoniem-voor-grand-cru parent="betekenis-en-gebruik" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.gcc1855-classification-overview"}
### Grand vin, grand cru and cru classé answer different questions

*Grand vin* identifies the wine at the centre of a producer's portfolio. Depending on the origin, *grand cru* may form part of a protected appellation name or classification term. *Cru classé* refers to a particular classification, such as [[classification.bordeaux-1855|Bordeaux 1855]]. [@source.gcc1855-classification-overview]

The shared prestige vocabulary makes these expressions visually confusing. Always check the full wording and the appellation; isolated words do not replace a wine's formal identity.
:::

:::section{#selectie-en-assemblage depth="foundation" source_refs="source.chateau-latour-grand-vin source.chateau-latour-les-forts source.chateau-mouton-wines"}
## Selection and blending — the name takes shape at the tasting table

Parcels, grape varieties and vinification lots are often followed separately. After harvest, the technical team tastes which barrels can combine to form the intended principal wine. Origin, ripeness, structure, aromatic quality and house style may all matter. The eventual [[concept.cuvee|cuvée]] is therefore both a selection and a blend.

Wine not included in the grand vin is not automatically a failure. A lot may better suit a [[concept.second-wine|second wine]], another cuvée, or may not be bottled under the estate name at all. The boundary can shift every year.
:::

:::detail{#twee-chateaux-twee-systemen parent="selectie-en-assemblage" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.chateau-latour-grand-vin source.chateau-latour-les-forts source.chateau-mouton-wines"}
### Two châteaux show why there is no single recipe

[[producer.chateau-latour|Château Latour]] describes its grand vin as a selection from old vines in the Enclos. Some lots initially eligible for it may still be assigned to Les Forts de Latour after blending tastings. [[producer.chateau-mouton-rothschild|Château Mouton Rothschild]] emphasises selected parcels, older vines and its most convincing vats for the principal wine. [@source.chateau-latour-les-forts] [@source.chateau-mouton-wines]

These examples show how strict selection may be, but do not establish a universal definition. Another estate may apply different parcel, vine-age or tasting criteria while using the same term.
:::

:::detail{#selectie-is-geen-vaste-formule parent="selectie-en-assemblage" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.chateau-latour-grand-vin source.chateau-latour-les-forts source.chateau-mouton-wines"}
### The hierarchy is made anew each year

A celebrated parcel does not automatically earn a place in the grand vin. Frost, drought, ripeness, disease and the character of individual barrels can alter selection. Conversely, a second wine is not necessarily made solely from young vines: at Latour, lots from potential grand-vin parcels may be assigned elsewhere after tasting. [@source.chateau-latour-les-forts]

It is therefore risky to draw an estate range as a fixed disposal ladder. The names remain recognisable, but the route from grape to bottle is a fresh editorial and technical decision by the estate each year.
:::

## Labels and quality — read beyond the largest words

Start with the producer, exact appellation, vintage and bottler. These details tell you which wine you are holding and which origin rules govern it. *Grand vin* may identify the principal wine within a range, but does not guarantee flavour, ageing potential or value for money. [@source.dgccrf-wine-labels-2025]

Compare the complete wine name when buying. A second wine and grand vin from one château may carry the same appellation while coming from different selections. A dry white companion wine may instead fall under another appellation and need not belong to the same hierarchy.
:::

:::detail{#grand-vin-de-bordeaux parent="etiket-en-kwaliteit" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.boagri-bordeaux-specification-2025 source.dgccrf-wine-labels-2025"}
### “Grand Vin de Bordeaux” can do a different job from “the grand vin”

The specification of the broad Bordeaux AOC permits certain appellation wines to display the larger geographical unit “Vin de Bordeaux” or “Grand Vin de Bordeaux”. In that complete expression, the words function as geographical presentation, not as proof that the bottle is the estate's principal wine. [@source.boagri-bordeaux-specification-2025]

That small distinction makes close reading important. Check whether the words belong to a broader origin statement, a château's presentation or a formal classification term. Typography may create an impression; the complete context supplies the meaning.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Grand vin usually means the principal selection of this producer in this vintage. It does not independently prove a classification, nor does it guarantee that every estate follows the same selection recipe.
:::
