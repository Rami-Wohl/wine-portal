Tannin gives wine grip. You notice it as a drying mouthfeel, sometimes fine-grained and sometimes firm. In red wine it helps determine shape and ageing potential; quality comes from the balance between that grip, fruit, acidity and everything else.
:::

:::section{#mondgevoel depth="foundation" source_refs="source.wset-mouthfeel-2026 source.wset-tasting-guide-2018"}
## Recognising tannin

Tannins are a group of plant compounds belonging to the **phenolic** family. In wine they contribute to *astringency*: the rough, drying sensation on the gums and inside the mouth. Astringency is primarily a tactile perception.

Bitterness can occur at the same time, but you perceive it differently. [[concept.acidity|Acidity]], which makes the mouth water, and alcoholic warmth are separate sensations too. A tannic wine may be completely dry or contain sweetness; *dry* in a wine description refers to sugar, while *drying* describes mouthfeel.

Describe both amount and texture: little or considerable grip, fine or coarse, supple or hard. A wine may have abundant fine tannin, or relatively little yet hard grip. [@source.wset-tasting-guide-2018]
:::

:::detail{#speeksel-en-waarneming parent="mondgevoel" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.wset-mouthfeel-2026"}
### Why the mouth feels rougher

Tannins interact with proteins in saliva, changing lubrication in the mouth. The mouth consequently feels drier even though moisture remains present. This helps explain why mouthfeel differs from an aroma you smell. Perceived texture arises where wine meets the mouth. [@source.wset-mouthfeel-2026]
:::

:::section{#waar-tannine-vandaan-komt depth="foundation" source_refs="source.wset-wine-composition source.oiv-oak-maturation source.awri-skin-contact"}
## Where tannin comes from

Grapes supply tannins from skins and seeds; stems, if included, can contribute too. How much enters the wine depends partly on contact and handling in the cellar. Red wine is usually fermented on skins and consequently tends to have more noticeable tannin than whites made without prolonged skin contact.

[[concept.maceration|Skin contact]] can also give white wine distinct grip. Wood may release tannins during [[concept.elevage|élevage]], the preparation of young wine for bottling. Grape and wood are therefore different tannin sources; firm structure alone reveals little about which source was used. [@source.awri-skin-contact] [@source.oiv-oak-maturation]

## From grape to wine

Tannin composition changes between grape and finished wine. [[concept.extraction|Extraction]] transfers compounds from solids into liquid, while some bind to grape material or disappear during further treatment. Variety, maturity and cellar choices together determine what remains.

Contact time, temperature, developing alcohol and movement of the skins influence that transfer. Longer maceration changes both amount and composition. The winemaker therefore steers towards the structure that suits the batch. [@source.awri-extended-maceration-2019]

Choices remain after extraction. A winemaker may assess batches separately and blend them, or use a fining agent to remove some compounds contributing unwanted bitterness or astringency. Such treatment may also affect colour and the rest of the mouthfeel. Small trials help establish which intervention improves the balance. [@source.awri-fining-agents]
:::

:::detail{#kleur-en-tannine-uit-elkaar parent="druif-en-kelder" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.awri-tannin-value-chain-2014"}
### Keeping colour and tannin distinct

Grape-derived red pigments, anthocyanins, belong to a different group from tannins. During winemaking and ageing they can react together to form coloured compounds. Colour and tannin structure therefore develop in concert, yet a dark wine may show fine or coarse, abundant or modest grip. [@source.awri-tannin-value-chain-2014]
:::

:::section{#ontwikkeling depth="foundation" source_refs="source.wset-tasting-guide-2018 source.awri-tannin-value-chain-2014"}
## Ageing and development

As a red wine develops, its grip may become more supple. Beautiful ageing also requires sufficient fruit and flavour to keep supporting the structure. Assess firm tannin with the complete wine before putting a bottle away for years.

Tannin belongs in assessing ageing potential, alongside acidity and flavour intensity. An immediately enjoyable red with little grip can fulfil its purpose perfectly. A wine with abundant fine grip may feel rich and harmonious. Assess balance, rather than scoring a single property. [@source.wset-tasting-guide-2018]
:::

:::detail{#zachter-is-niet-alleen-minder parent="ontwikkeling" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.awri-tannin-value-chain-2014"}
### How tannin can feel softer

Research on old red wines shows that a softer mouthfeel involves more than tannin precipitation. Older wines may still contain comparable amounts while composition and interactions with salivary proteins have changed. Molecular development and precipitation therefore operate alongside each other. [@source.awri-tannin-value-chain-2014]

A tannin measurement consequently does not predict an exact drinking window. Molecular changes, the rest of the wine and storage conditions must be considered together.
:::

:::section{#proeven-in-context depth="foundation" source_refs="source.wset-mouthfeel-2026"}
## Tasting and pairing in context

Taste grip alongside fruit, acidity and alcohol. Ask how much tannin you feel, what texture it has and how cohesive the wine seems. Words such as velvety, chalky or grainy describe that perception, while its precise origin requires evidence about grape and cellar.

Food changes the experience. A savoury, protein-rich dish may make the grip of a firm red feel less prominent. Serving temperature matters too: substantial chilling may make tannins seem harder. Use those effects as starting points, then match the complete wine to the complete dish. [@source.wset-mouthfeel-2026]
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Tannin means grip and texture. Its value lies in wine's balance and development, not in maximising roughness.
:::
