:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.civb-bordeaux-wine-trade source.bordeaux-negoce-profession"}
The **Place de Bordeaux** is the trading network in which wine producers from [[region.bordeaux|Bordeaux]], [[concept.courtier|courtiers]] and [[concept.negociant|négociants]] offer, broker and distribute wine across international markets. Here, *place* means marketplace: a system of relationships, customs and transactions extending far beyond one building or company. [@source.civb-bordeaux-wine-trade]
:::

:::section{#drie-kernrollen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.civb-bordeaux-wine-trade source.bordeaux-negoce-profession"}
## The three core roles

The château makes the wine and decides what to release. The courtier moves between producer and merchant as an independent broker, follows supply and prices, and helps record terms. The négociant buys wine or receives an allocation, then sells onwards to importers, distributors, hospitality businesses and other professional customers. [@source.civb-bordeaux-wine-trade]

This division of labour gives a château access to many markets without building its own sales organisation everywhere. At the same time, one négociant gives a buyer access to wines from several producers. The Place is therefore principally a network connecting fragmented supply with dispersed demand.
:::

:::section{#handelsstroom depth="foundation"}
## Flow of trade

A simple route reads: **château → courtier → négociant → professional buyer → drinker**. The wine itself, information about it and the money may move at different times. In [[concept.en-primeur|en primeur]], for example, the commercial commitment may occur while the young wine is still maturing in barrel and the physical bottles follow much later.

The route has several variations. A courtier brokers and normally does not own the wine; a producer may use several négociants; some sales are direct or outside the Place. The model makes the functions clear, while each transaction may follow its own route.
:::

:::detail{#releases-en-toewijzingen parent="handelsstroom" depth="intermediate"}
### Releases and allocations

When a producer releases a parcel, it may divide volumes among several négociants. An allocation is a commercial allotment, often influenced by established relationships, sales strength and the markets a merchant serves. The négociant then offers that wine onwards within its network.

A release is therefore not a central auction with one universal counter price. Timing, volume, terms and later margins can vary at each link. Scarcity may reflect genuinely small supply, but it can also result from staged releases or limited allocation.
:::

:::section{#geschiedenis depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.msha-bordeaux-wine-history"}
## History

The abstract Place has very tangible roots. Bordeaux stood at a river port where wine from a broad hinterland was collected, stored and shipped to foreign markets. Trading houses and warehouses settled in the Chartrons, along the quays north of the old centre, to finance and organise this flow. [@source.msha-bordeaux-wine-history]

The historic port landscape explains the network's name and culture, but the modern Place does not inhabit one quay. Telephones, digital communication, specialist storage and global logistics have transformed its physical setting; the accumulated relationships and division of roles remain recognisable.
:::

## Reach and limits

The Place is most closely associated with Bordeaux's sought-after wines and en-primeur campaigns, but its trading apparatus also serves widely priced Bordeaux wines. Some négociants also distribute wines from beyond Bordeaux. This makes their portfolios more international without turning every traded bottle into Bordeaux wine.

Conversely, not every Bordeaux wine necessarily travels through this network. Co-operatives, direct sales, proprietary distribution and other commercial routes coexist with it. “Sold through the Place” describes a route to market, not an origin or a quality class.
:::

:::detail{#geen-centrale-regisseur parent="reikwijdte-en-grenzen" depth="advanced"}
### A decentralised market

The Place operates without a central order book or authority setting every price. Information travels through bilateral relationships; reputation, credit, allocations and access to customers give participants differing positions.

That is precisely why the network can be both efficient and hard to see through. It distributes wine around the world and concentrates considerable market knowledge, but outsiders do not automatically see every volume, margin or agreement between its links.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
The Place de Bordeaux is a trading network: producers supply the wine, courtiers connect parties, and négociants finance and distribute stock. It forms an important decentralised route alongside direct sales and other distribution models.
:::
