With **millerandage**, one bunch carries berries that develop conspicuously differently. Normal berries may sit beside small, often seed-poor berries and hard green ovaries. This differs from [[concept.coulure|coulure]], in which flowers or young fruitlets are shed. Millerandage concerns heterogeneity that remains present in the bunch. [@source.muntean-millerandage-review-2025] [@source.dry-reproductive-performance-2010]
:::

:::section{#overzicht depth="foundation" source_refs="source.muntean-millerandage-review-2025"}
## Overview

An ordinarily set bunch already contains some variation, but with millerandage the contrast is conspicuous: large and small berries occur together and do not proceed evenly through ripening. Some small berries colour and ripen, often with few or no fully developed seeds. Other ovaries remain green, hard and very small.

The term is not used with equal precision everywhere. It is therefore better to name the observed category than to call every size difference millerandage. [@source.muntean-millerandage-review-2025]
:::

:::section{#werking depth="foundation" source_refs="source.muntean-millerandage-review-2025 source.vasconcelos-flowering-review-2009"}
## How it works

Seed development provides signals that support growth of the surrounding fruit tissue. Where pollination or fertilisation is incomplete, or embryo and seed subsequently fail to develop normally, an ovary may remain attached but grow far less. Small seed-poor or seedless berries then occur beside normally developed berries.

Not every arrested ovary follows the same route. Some may soften and colour; others remain as green, living ovaries. The route from flower to berry is placed in context under [[concept.flowering-fruit-set-yield|flowering, fruit set and yield formation]].
:::

:::detail{#twee-vormen-van-achterblijven parent="werking" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.dry-reproductive-performance-2010 source.muntean-millerandage-review-2025"}
### Small and ripening is not the same as green and arrested

A small ripening berry may accumulate sugar, soften and, in dark-skinned cultivars, colour despite limited seed formation. A green living ovary instead remains hard and scarcely follows normal berry development. Both make the bunch uneven, but they have different implications for sampling, sorting and eventual composition.
:::

:::section{#omstandigheden-en-variatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.muntean-millerandage-review-2025"}
## Conditions and variation

Cultivars differ in susceptibility, flower anatomy, seed formation and natural variation in berry size. Cool or unstable weather around flowering may disrupt pollen germination and fertilisation; vine nutrition, water status and strong vegetative growth may likewise alter conditions for flower and early berry development.

No one factor explains every case. The same visible mixture can arise through different combinations of genetic predisposition, flowering weather and vine condition. Some size variation is also normal, and outcomes within one bunch may form a continuum.
:::

:::section{#toepassing-en-keuzes depth="foundation" source_refs="source.muntean-millerandage-review-2025 source.dry-reproductive-performance-2010"}
## Application and decisions

Millerandage becomes visible soon after fruit set, but a second assessment around veraison sharpens the distinction: which small berries follow ripening and which remain green? Representative bunches are assessed by berry category, not only by average bunch mass.

Measures before and around flowering can be chosen sensibly only when cultivar, vine balance and likely cause are known. Later in the season, decisions chiefly concern sampling, harvest selection and sorting. Making every bunch completely uniform is neither a universal nor a necessary objective.
:::

:::detail{#bemonsteren-van-een-ongelijke-tros parent="toepassing-en-keuzes" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.muntean-millerandage-review-2025"}
### An average can conceal the bunch

If only the largest berries are tasted or analysed, a heterogeneous bunch appears riper than it is as a whole. A representative sample includes the different sizes and developmental stages in their actual proportions. Towards harvest, selective picking or sorting may matter where hard green ovaries remain materially present.
:::

:::section{#bewijs-en-grenzen depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.dry-reproductive-performance-2010 source.muntean-millerandage-review-2025"}
## Evidence and limits

Scientific papers and practical language do not always use *millerandage*, *shot berries*, small seedless berries and green ovaries in exactly the same way. A clear study or field note therefore states which berries were counted, which size or ripeness threshold was used, and when measurement occurred. [@source.muntean-millerandage-review-2025]
:::

:::detail{#categorieen-in-plaats-van-een-label parent="bewijs-en-grenzen" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.dry-reproductive-performance-2010"}
### Categories say more than one label

A millerandage index can count small ripening berries separately from green living ovaries. Normal berries, shed flowers and total flower number are also needed to distinguish the phenomenon from coulure and a naturally low flower count. A vague bunch appearance thus becomes a series of testable observations.
:::

:::section{#betekenis-voor-wijn depth="foundation" source_refs="source.tello-bunch-compactness-review-2018 source.muntean-millerandage-review-2025"}
## Significance for wine

Millerandage may reduce bunch mass and yield and makes the raw material less uniform. Small ripening berries have a different skin-to-juice ratio from large berries; green arrested organs may differ in composition from the ripe share. How much this matters depends on their proportion and on harvest and sorting.

At the same time, less compact bunch architecture may provide air and space between berries. That practical benefit and heterogeneity can coexist. The label alone therefore does not determine quality; it tells growers and winemakers to treat averages and rapid visual assessment with extra care.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Millerandage is not a synonym for a loose bunch. It is the continued coexistence of different berry developments—a difference that remains visible during ripening, sampling and selection.
:::
