:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.awri-lees-contact-2020"}
**Autolysis** is the gradual breakdown of dead yeast cells. Components from their cell walls and interiors can thereby enter the wine. The process plays a part during [[concept.lees-ageing|lees ageing]], but develops slowly and is only one element of what occurs there. [@source.awri-lees-contact-2020]
:::

:::section{#betekenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.awri-lees-contact-2020"}
## Meaning — what yeast leaves behind after fermentation

After [[concept.fermentation|fermentation]], much of the yeast population dies and settles. The cells do not disappear immediately. Their own enzymes help break down their structure over time, allowing various components to pass into the surrounding wine.
:::

:::detail{#tijd-en-omstandigheden parent="betekenis" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.awri-lees-contact-2020"}
### A slow process without a sharp starting point

Autolysis unfolds over time and is influenced by factors including temperature, pH, yeast strain and the duration of lees contact. Living yeast, bacteria, oxygen and other particles may be active at the same time. More than autolysis alone is therefore taking place in a vessel. [@source.awri-lees-contact-2020]
:::

:::section{#betekenis-voor-wijn depth="foundation" source_refs="source.awri-lees-contact-2020"}
## Significance for wine — released compounds can participate

Released cell components can contribute to changes in mouthfeel, aroma and stability. Whether those changes are perceptible or beneficial depends on the wine and its wider [[concept.elevage|ageing process]]. Autolysis is consequently a mechanism, not a stylistic guarantee.
:::

:::detail{#mannoproteinen-en-grenzen parent="betekenis-voor-wijn" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.awri-lees-contact-2020"}
### Mannoproteins belong to a larger mixture

Mannoproteins from yeast cell walls receive considerable attention for their possible effects on texture and stability. Autolysis also releases other polysaccharides, amino acids, fatty acids and compounds. Their influence depends on concentration and interaction with the rest of the wine. [@source.awri-lees-contact-2020]

A fuller mouthfeel does not prove that autolysis was the sole cause. Alcohol, acids, oak, [[concept.malolactic-fermentation|malolactic fermentation]] and [[concept.batonnage|bâtonnage]] may all make a difference at the same time.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Autolysis is the slow breakdown of dead yeast cells. The compounds released can influence wine, but their effect always arises within a wider context of time, material and cellar choices.
:::
