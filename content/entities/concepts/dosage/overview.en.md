:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.champagne-dosage"}
**Dosage** is the addition used after [[concept.disgorgement|disgorgement]] to top up a finished sparkling wine and set its final sweetness balance. The added liquid is called *liqueur de dosage* or *liqueur d'expédition*. Dosage is distinct from the [[concept.liqueur-de-tirage|liqueur de tirage]] that started the [[concept.second-fermentation|second fermentation]]. [@source.champagne-dosage]
:::

:::section{#moment-en-functie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.champagne-dosage"}
## Timing and purpose — the final adjustment

Removing the yeast sediment leaves a little space in the bottle. The winemaker tops it up with a wine-based mixture that may contain sugar. The amount of sugar in the finished wine helps determine a label term such as *brut*. Wine with no sugar added at this stage may still contain a small amount of natural residual sugar. [@source.champagne-dosage]
:::

:::detail{#smaakbalans parent="moment-en-functie" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.champagne-dosage"}
### Dry on paper can taste different in the glass

The same sugar level can seem different alongside different acidity, mousse or aromatic intensity. Dosage is therefore a stylistic choice: it can soften an edge or preserve a taut profile. The label term describes a sugar range, not the complete flavour. [@source.champagne-dosage]
:::

:::section{#onderscheid depth="foundation" source_refs="source.champagne-bottling-second-fermentation source.champagne-dosage"}
## Distinction — not the sugar that makes the bubbles

Sugar in the liqueur de tirage is added before the second fermentation to feed the yeast. Dosage comes only after the yeast has been removed and serves to **finish** the wine. Both steps may use sugar, but answer different questions: ‘how do we make the mousse?’ and ‘how should the finished wine taste?’. [@source.champagne-bottling-second-fermentation] [@source.champagne-dosage]
:::

:::detail{#etiket-en-grenzen parent="onderscheid" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.champagne-dosage"}
### A label term is not a quality score

*Brut nature*, *extra brut* and *brut* name different sweetness categories. Their exact legal thresholds should be read within the applicable rules. Less sugar does not automatically mean greater quality: the base wine, acidity and intended style matter too. [@source.champagne-dosage]
:::

:::key-idea{#kern depth="foundation"}
Tirage starts the bubbles; dosage adjusts the finished wine's balance after disgorgement.
:::
