**Oxidation** is change in wine caused by reactions involving oxygen. A little oxygen may be deliberately allowed during production or ageing, while too much exposure at the wrong time can erase fruit aromas and leave a wine flat. ‘Oxidative’ can describe an intended style; ‘oxidised’ often describes a fault. [@source.awri-wine-faults] [@source.wine-australia-oxygen-style]
:::

:::section{#herkennen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.awri-wine-faults"}
## Recognition — when freshness fades

Unintentionally oxidised wine may show less fresh fruit and develop aromas reminiscent of bruised apple, nuts or cardboard. White wine may darken; red wine changes in colour and aroma too. No single aroma proves oxidation on its own: age, variety, oak and intended style provide essential context. [@source.awri-wine-faults]
:::

:::detail{#kwetsbaarheid parent="herkennen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.awri-wine-faults"}
### The wine determines how quickly it shows

Wines differ in sensitivity. Phenolic compounds can buffer oxygen reactions; their amounts differ, for instance, between white and red wines. Oxygen entering the bottle or closure at packaging and subsequent storage also matter. The same exposure therefore does not always produce the same flavour. [@source.awri-wine-faults]
:::

:::section{#keuze-of-fout depth="foundation" source_refs="source.awri-wine-faults source.wine-australia-oxygen-style"}
## Choice or fault — oxygen is not simply the enemy

Some wine styles deliberately seek nutty, savoury or dried-fruit character through controlled oxygen contact. In a young, fruit-led wine, the same loss of freshness would be unwelcome. Judge oxidation against the **intended style** and the wine's balance, not merely the presence of air. [@source.awri-wine-faults] [@source.wine-australia-oxygen-style]
:::

:::detail{#chemie parent="keuze-of-fout" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.awri-wine-faults source.wine-australia-oxygen-style"}
### Time, dose and protection interact

Oxygen reacts through a network of wine compounds; aldehydes can form and aromas can be lost. Winemakers manage the process through vessel choice, racking, measured oxygen supply, sulfur dioxide, bottling and closures. ‘No oxygen’ is therefore not a useful universal ideal: control suited to the wine is what matters. [@source.awri-wine-faults] [@source.wine-australia-oxygen-style]
:::

:::key-idea{#kern depth="foundation"}
Oxidation is a process. Whether its result is appealing or intrusive depends on oxygen level, timing and the style the winemaker seeks.
:::
