A wine has a composition; a taster experiences aroma, taste and mouthfeel in a particular setting. The wine itself also changes during storage. These three perspectives help explain why an analysis is not a tasting note, why balance means more than equal intensities, and why older does not automatically mean better.

:::section{#overzicht depth="foundation" source_refs="source.spence-wine-psychology-2020 source.echave-bottle-ageing-storage-2021"}
## Overview — wine, taster and time

**Perception** arises when wine meets a taster. Its [[concept.wine-composition|composition]] supplies stimuli, while attention, experience and expectations also influence what someone experiences and names. **Wine development** is the change in wine over time. A difference between two tasting notes can therefore come from the wine, the taster or the conditions.

This page connects those perspectives. [[concept.acidity|Acidity]], [[concept.tannin|tannin]] and [[concept.oxidation|oxidation]] have their own detail pages. [[concept.elevage|Élevage]] covers cellar work before bottling; [[concept.bottling|bottling]] covers stability and packaging. The focus here is how to interpret their effects in the glass.
:::

## Structure and relationships — more than taste alone

What everyday language calls ‘taste’ combines several contributions:

| Contribution | What you experience | What to observe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smell before a sip | Volatile compounds reach the nose from the glass: orthonasal smell | Aroma character and intensity |
| Smell during tasting | Volatile compounds reach the nose from the mouth through the throat: retronasal smell | Aroma experienced during and after a sip |
| Taste | Including sweetness, sourness and bitterness | Describe these separately from aroma words |
| Mouthfeel | Texture, drying, warmth and tingling | Separate astringency, alcohol warmth and CO₂ prickle |
| Sight and context | Colour, label, surroundings and expectations | Record what information was available beforehand |

Both smell routes belong to the same combined experience. Astringency is a tactile sensation rather than a basic taste; ‘drying’ and ‘bitter’ describe different things. [@source.awri-wine-sensory-assessment-2022; §5] Alcohol warmth and CO₂ prickle belong to the chemical stimulation of the sense of touch, often called **trigeminal perception**. **Body** is the impression of weight and fullness, to which several components contribute. [@source.wset-mouthfeel-2026]
:::

## How it works — relationships and changes

**The mixture matters.** Aroma compounds can enhance or mask one another's recognisability. Analysing individual compounds therefore does not produce a simple sum of separate smells. [@source.ilc-wine-aroma-meta-analysis-2016; odor thresholds and mixture interactions] Taste contributions also interact: sweetness can suppress perceived sourness. [@source.awri-wine-sensory-assessment-2022; §5]

**Balance describes a relationship.** Acidity, sweetness, tannin, alcohol, aroma and texture need not be equally intense. The question is how the components work together within the intended wine style. First describe what stands out, then explain your judgement. ‘High acidity’ is an observation; ‘too much acidity’ is a judgement requiring a frame of reference.

**The wine keeps reacting.** Bottle storage changes aroma compounds, pigments and tannins, among other components. Phenolic compounds can react with one another and with other wine constituents; precipitation can also occur. The outcome is not a simple rule that larger tannin molecules always taste softer. [@source.echave-bottle-ageing-storage-2021; §§4.1, 4.3] Bottle development is also more than oxidation: TDN, known for petrol-like Riesling aromas, can form from precursors through acid-catalysed transformations during storage. [@source.tarasov-riesling-tdn-thresholds-2020; §1] New aromas and the loss of existing aromas can occur together; assessing their value requires tasting again.
:::

:::detail{#drempels-en-mengsels parent="werking" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.tarasov-riesling-tdn-thresholds-2020 source.ilc-wine-aroma-meta-analysis-2016"}
### Detecting, recognising and rejecting ask different questions

A **detection threshold** concerns noticing a difference or stimulus. A **recognition threshold** also asks what the smell is. A **rejection threshold** examines the concentration at which a panel rejects a sample. The last also relates to preference and familiarity; it is not a biological safety limit. [@source.tarasov-riesling-tdn-thresholds-2020; §1]

A threshold belongs to a compound, matrix, method and studied group. People differ. Compounds individually below a threshold can still contribute perceptibly together; a compound above a reported threshold can be masked in a mixture. [@source.ilc-wine-aroma-meta-analysis-2016; odor thresholds] Treat such a number as research context rather than a guarantee for every bottle or taster.
:::

**Three different questions about the same glass**

1. **Smelling the glass:** aroma reaches the nose from outside. Notice which aromas you detect before a sip.
2. **Tasting:** aroma from the mouth, taste and mouthfeel combine in the experience. What you ‘taste’ therefore extends beyond basic tastes.
3. **Storage:** the wine itself changes over time, depending on the starting wine, packaging and conditions. The hourglass represents time, not an improvement in quality.
:::

## Conditions and variation — the moment matters

**During tasting**, temperature, order and repetition influence the experience. Continued smelling can bring adaptation; successive tannic samples can instead increase the drying sensation. Breaks and a comparable tasting setup help. [@source.awri-wine-sensory-assessment-2022; §5] Colour and information given beforehand can shape expectations. Blind tasting limits some of that information, but does not free a taster from all context. [@source.spence-wine-psychology-2020; Conclusions]

**During storage**, temperature, light, closure and the wine's condition at bottling act on the wine. Prolonged heat can accelerate development and damage wine; light can cause unwanted sulfur aromas. Store in cool, stable conditions, limit light and record storage history. Visible leakage or a displaced cork warrants investigation, but alone does not prove what sensory damage has occurred. [@source.awri-wine-transport-storage; Effects of temperature, humidity and light]
:::

## Decisions and trade-offs — describe before judging

Keep three questions distinct: **what do I perceive**, **how do I judge it within this style**, and **do I enjoy it**? A preference for youthful fruit aromas can lead to a different storage decision from an interest in developed aromas. A shared assessment framework makes judgements easier to discuss; it does not replace personal preference.

For a focused comparison, keep temperature, glass and available information as consistent as possible and record tasting order. Discuss impressions only after everyone has recorded them independently. [@source.awri-wine-sensory-assessment-2022; Palate]

**Ageing potential** is an informed expectation. Consider the specific wine, previous experience with comparable bottles and known storage conditions. High acidity or tannin alone does not yield an exact drinking window. A series of comparably stored bottles offers more insight into development than a single young sample; continued observation is more useful than presenting a year as certain.
:::

:::section{#wereldwijde-context depth="foundation" source_refs="source.tarasov-riesling-tdn-thresholds-2020 source.awri-wine-transport-storage"}
## Global context — examples with a defined question

**Riesling from the Rheingau** provided the matrix for research on TDN. Detection, recognition and rejection were investigated using different tasting tasks. A petrol note becoming recognisable therefore answers a different question from whether participants appreciate it. Wine matrix, panel and method belong with the result. [@source.tarasov-riesling-tdn-thresholds-2020; §§1–3]

**Australian storage trials with Chardonnay and Riesling**, discussed by AWRI, found little effect of horizontal versus upright storage over five years on the chemical and sensory properties investigated. Other trials with different closures produced different findings. A storage rule without specifying closure, wine and study duration is therefore too broad. [@source.awri-wine-transport-storage; Bottle orientation]

In [[concept.sweet-wines|sweet wines]], [[concept.sparkling-wines|sparkling wines]] and [[concept.fortified-wines|fortified wines]], sugar, CO₂ and alcohol each occupy a different place in the whole. Use those differences to discuss balance precisely. The family or origin alone does not tell you how an individual bottle tastes now or how it will be appreciated later.
:::

:::section{#bewijs-en-grenzen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.ilc-wine-aroma-meta-analysis-2016 source.awri-wine-sensory-assessment-2022 source.tarasov-riesling-tdn-thresholds-2020 source.spence-wine-psychology-2020"}
## Evidence and limits — a result belongs to a research question

| Information | What it helps answer | What it does not establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chemical analysis | Which measured compounds are present and how much | The full perceived aroma, balance or quality |
| Descriptive tasting | Which properties a panel describes under those conditions | Every drinker's preference |
| Preference research | What the studied group prefers in this setup | A universal quality ranking |
| Storage trial | How studied bottles change under recorded conditions | An exact drinking window for every other bottle |

Concentration and perception complement one another. Threshold research makes that relationship testable, but a different mixture or group can yield a different result. [@source.ilc-wine-aroma-meta-analysis-2016] When making a strong claim about aroma or quality, state the study design and its scope too.
:::

:::detail{#minerality-als-proefwoord parent="bewijs-en-grenzen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.parr-maltman-minerality-2018"}
### Minerality calls for a more precise description

**Minerality** is a tasting word that people use differently. It can evoke associations with stone, freshness or other impressions; it is not an agreed measurement of a single component. Research finds varied definitions and sensory judgements, with associations involving multiple wine constituents. [@source.parr-maltman-minerality-2018]

Make the word useful by naming what you perceive: is it aroma, taste or texture, and how would you describe it concretely? This makes a comparison discussable without assuming a cause in the soil. [[concept.vineyard-soils|Vineyard soils]] covers the separate chain of rock, dissolved nutrients and plant uptake. A tasting word is not evidence that you taste the vineyard's rock.
:::

:::detail{#vergelijkbaar-onderzoek parent="bewijs-en-grenzen" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.awri-wine-sensory-assessment-2022 source.spence-wine-psychology-2020 source.tarasov-riesling-tdn-thresholds-2020"}
### A comparison needs to limit alternative explanations

Hiding a label removes information; random order limits order effects; repeated samples help investigate response consistency. These are different measures. Preference tasks, recognition tasks and descriptive analysis also provide different kinds of evidence. [@source.awri-wine-sensory-assessment-2022; §5] [@source.tarasov-riesling-tdn-thresholds-2020; §2]

Older bottles add bottle variation. Different closures and unknown storage histories can complicate a comparison of vintages. Record what is known and limit the conclusion to the bottles investigated. A blind comparison is therefore not automatically a controlled experiment into age alone. [@source.spence-wine-psychology-2020; Conclusions]
:::

## Significance for wine — turn an impression into a question

Start with a concrete description: sour, sweet, drying, warm, prickling or aromatic. Then ask which components together might create that impression. Consult the detail page for the mechanism and [[concept.wine-composition|wine composition]] for the place of compounds in the whole.

A storage decision adds time: what do you want to preserve or see change, what experience supports that expectation, and what do you know about the bottle's history? [[concept.bottling|Packaging]] and [[concept.sulfur-dioxide|protection]] belong alongside the wine itself. Record observation, judgement and uncertainty separately, so that the next bottle becomes a useful comparison.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Connect what a wine contains with what someone experiences and what changes over time. Detecting, judging and enjoying remain different questions.
:::
