An **own-rooted or franc-de-pied vine** grows on the roots of its own grape variety. It therefore has no graft union with a separate [[concept.rootstock|rootstock]]. This became less common for European *Vitis vinifera* after the [[concept.phylloxera|phylloxera crisis]], but own-rooted vineyards still exist — as old survivors, in areas where the insect is absent and as new, deliberate plantings. [@source.osu-grape-phylloxera]
:::

:::section{#betekenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.osu-grape-phylloxera source.uc-ipm-grape-phylloxera"}
## Meaning — one plant from root to bunch

On a grafted vine, the root system and fruit-bearing upper plant come from different vine material. A franc-de-pied vine is one continuous plant: a cutting or layer makes its own roots and later bears grapes of that same variety.

This says nothing yet about age. A vine predating the nineteenth-century crisis may be own-rooted, but a grower can also plant a young vine without rootstock today. **Own-rooted**, **old vine** and **pre-phylloxera** are therefore three separate claims. [@source.uc-ipm-grape-phylloxera]
:::

## Where own roots can persist

For susceptible *Vitis vinifera*, the absence or strong suppression of root phylloxera is decisive. Very sandy soils strongly inhibit the insect. Islands and other isolated areas may prevent introduction, while quarantine restricts movements of plant material, soil and machinery. [@source.uc-ipm-grape-phylloxera]

The scale differs in each example. Santorini is described as a never-affected, own-rooted island vineyard; Chile lists phylloxera officially as a nationally absent quarantine pest. Australia instead shows that one country can contain both infested areas and protected exclusion zones. A current local status is therefore more informative than a world map coloured with simple yes-or-no categories. [@source.wines-greece-pdo-santorini] [@source.sag-chile-quarantine-pests-2025] [@source.awri-phylloxera]
:::

:::detail{#risico-en-schaal parent="waar-het-kan" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.awri-phylloxera source.uc-ipm-grape-phylloxera"}
### Risk belongs to a place and a moment

An own-rooted parcel within an affected region may depend on sand, isolation, particular plant material or careful management. This does not make the surrounding region free of phylloxera. Conversely, an officially free zone remains dependent on biosecurity: absence is a condition to protect, not biological immunity in the vine. [@source.awri-phylloxera]
:::

### Heritage and the contemporary movement

Producers may preserve own-rooted vines for their old plant material, local varieties, growing knowledge or the history of a place. The Association Francs de Pied seeks to connect these practices internationally and is developing its own certification mark and heritage programme. The association also advances strong ideas about flavour and authenticity; these ambitions represent the organisation's position rather than an independent measure of quality. [@source.francs-de-pied-association]

[[producer.liber-pater|Liber Pater]] in [[appellation.graves|Graves]] is a modern [[region.bordeaux|Bordeaux]] example. The estate works with own-rooted plantings and replanted historic Bordeaux varieties; owner Loïc Pasquet also leads the association. [@source.francs-de-pied-association] [@source.rvf-liber-pater-profile]
:::

:::detail{#etiket-en-bewijs parent="waar-het-kan" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.francs-de-pied-association"}
### The precise claim behind the term

*Franc de pied* may describe an entire estate, one parcel or only the material used for a particular [[concept.cuvee|cuvée]]. For a specific wine, ask about its precise scope, planting date and origin of the material. The term alone proves neither great age, a rare variety, a phylloxera-free region nor superior wine. An association mark may acquire its own controlled meaning in future, but it does not replace a general legal definition. [@source.francs-de-pied-association]
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Franc de pied describes the construction of the vine: the grape variety grows on its own roots. Age, origin, phytosanitary status and quality each require separate evidence.
:::
