Ugni Blanc is a grape whose greatest strength is not always obvious in a glass of white wine. Its fresh acidity, modest sugar and relatively restrained aroma make it important to Cognac. In Italy it is also known as Trebbiano Toscano: it is more than a French raw material for brandy.
:::

:::section{#identiteit depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-ugni-blanc"}
## Identity and origins — a Tuscan name, an international life

Ugni Blanc is a white wine grape originating in Tuscany. Plantgrape lists Trebbiano Toscano as a synonym. That needs precision: the broad name “Trebbiano” also appears with other Italian varieties; it is not automatic proof that two vines are genetically identical. [@source.plantgrape-ugni-blanc]

The grape can make ordinary white wine, but in France it is especially central to wine subsequently made into a spirit. That is not a second-class use. The desirable traits of a distillation base differ from those of a powerfully aromatic drinking wine.
:::

## Regions and appellations — Italy, Cognac and Armagnac

In Tuscany, Ugni Blanc belongs under the name Trebbiano Toscano. In the Charentes it is the dominant Cognac grape. The Cognac trade body points to the combination of high acidity and relatively little sugar: it yields a dry, modest-alcohol base wine suited to distillation. [@source.plantgrape-ugni-blanc] [@source.bnic-cognac-grapes]

Ugni Blanc is also important in Armagnac, alongside varieties such as [[grape.colombard|Colombard]]. Outside those spirits it can be a white wine grape. In [[region.bordeaux|Bordeaux]] it is only one possible supporting white variety for [[appellation.cremant-de-bordeaux|Crémant de Bordeaux]]; that local use should not define its wider role. [@source.bnia-armagnac-grapes] [@source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026]

:::section{#smaak depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-ugni-blanc source.bnic-cognac-grapes"}
## Wine styles and sensory profile — restrained aroma, useful tension

As a still white wine, Ugni Blanc is often relatively neutral and fresh. That need not be a flaw: the restrained aroma leaves room for place, other grapes or a chosen cellar style. Plantgrape describes its wines as relatively neutral, depending on conditions. [@source.plantgrape-ugni-blanc]

For Cognac, the equation changes. Acidity helps keep the base wine sound, while modest sugar leads to relatively low alcohol after fermentation. Distillation then concentrates and transforms aromas; the flavour of Cognac after time in barrel is not simply twice the aroma of the grape. [@source.bnic-cognac-grapes]
:::

:::section{#klimaat depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-ugni-blanc"}
## Climate, site and soils — a long road to ripeness

Ugni Blanc ripens relatively late, making the length and course of the season important. The grapes need sufficient maturity for sound, usable juice, but maximum sugar is not the goal for a distillation base. Soil and water influence growth; the grape's name does not reveal the ground beneath the vine.
:::

:::section{#groeicyclus depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.plantgrape-ugni-blanc"}
## Vine and growing cycle — vigour and a later harvest

Plantgrape describes Ugni Blanc as a vigorous vine with large, sometimes elongated bunches. In its comparative collection, ripeness falls about three and a half to four weeks after Chasselas. This is a relative reference, not a date transferable from Tuscany to the Charentes. [@source.plantgrape-ugni-blanc]

Strong growth requires suitable training. Support matters where wind puts pressure on the shoots. An excess of foliage or fruit can also work against the harvest goal; useful yield and healthy ripening fruit must come together.
:::

:::section{#wijnbouw depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.plantgrape-ugni-blanc"}
## Viticulture and vulnerabilities — sound fruit for a clean base

According to Plantgrape, Ugni Blanc is susceptible to downy mildew and eutypa dieback, a disease of perennial wood. Its bunches are less markedly prone to grey rot. “Less prone” is not immunity: vine and fruit health still require attention. [@source.plantgrape-ugni-blanc]

A substantial but sound harvest is valuable for distillation. Growers must consider growth, disease pressure and picking date together. A potentially productive variety does not automatically make good base wine when the fruit is unhealthy.
:::

:::section{#wijnmaken depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.bnic-cognac-grapes source.plantgrape-ugni-blanc"}
## Winemaking and ageing — first wine, then perhaps spirit

Ugni Blanc is first pressed and fermented into white wine. It can be drunk as wine or used as a base for distillation. In Cognac the second path is characteristic: the wine is distilled and the spirit may then spend time in oak. The grape alone does not explain the finished aroma; fermentation, distillation and time in oak play their parts. [@source.bnic-cognac-grapes]

Still white wine follows another emphasis. A maker can retain the fresh, restrained profile, shape the texture or blend it with more aromatic fruit. Not every bottle of Ugni Blanc is therefore an unfinished Cognac base.
:::

:::section{#verwantschap depth="advanced" source_refs="source.plantgrape-ugni-blanc"}
## Synonyms, clones and relationships — Trebbiano is not one grape

Trebbiano Toscano refers to Ugni Blanc; “Trebbiano” without a qualifier can refer to several varieties. Names alone are not genetic evidence. Plantgrape also documents selected French vine material: a clone is a selection **within** the variety, not a synonym or a new variety. [@source.plantgrape-ugni-blanc]
:::

:::section{#etiket depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-ugni-blanc source.bnic-cognac-grapes"}
## Labels and recognition — the name may change

Trebbiano Toscano is a useful name to look for in Italy; Ugni Blanc appears in French contexts. On Cognac, origin and spirit take centre stage, not necessarily the variety. None of those labels alone tells us how the base wine tasted or how long the spirit matured.
:::

:::section{#heden depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.plantgrape-ugni-blanc source.bnic-cognac-grapes"}
## Contemporary developments — utility is a form of quality

Ugni Blanc shows why a grape should not be judged solely against the ideal of an aromatic still wine. Acidity, reliable production and a suitable starting point for distillation are central in the Charentes. At the same time, in Italy and elsewhere it remains a wine grape with local uses of its own. Context determines what kind of quality matters.
:::
