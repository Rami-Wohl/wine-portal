Cabernet Sauvignon is a grape with backbone. Small dark berries can give wine deep colour, freshness and abundant tannin: the drying grip that lends young red wine its frame. Blackcurrant is its most famous aroma association, but the variety speaks in more than one voice. A cooler year may bring leaf and bell pepper to the fore; warmer conditions can shift the fruit towards ripe blackberry or plum.

Its home lies in [[region.bordeaux|Bordeaux]], yet Cabernet Sauvignon now grows in almost every major wine-producing country. It may carry a wine on its own or form part of a [[concept.assemblage|blend]], a composition of different grapes or lots. What reaches the glass is therefore never just “the taste of the grape”: climate, soil, ripeness, yield, cellar work and time all contribute. [@source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon] [@source.wine-grapes-cabernet-sauvignon]

## Identity and origins — younger than its reputation suggests

Cabernet Sauvignon is a black-berried variety of the European grapevine *Vitis vinifera*. Its name sounds ancient, but it emerged in Bordeaux's history later than some of its relatives. By the eighteenth century it was firmly associated with the gravelly Left Bank, especially the [[region.medoc|Médoc]] and [[appellation.graves|Graves]]. Its long season suited sunny, freely draining sites and wines that could mature before being drunk. [@source.uc-davis-bordeaux-grape-varieties]

Cabernet's strength is not one aroma but a useful combination: colour, [[concept.acidity|acidity]], [[concept.tannin|tannin]] and aromatic intensity can coexist. That made it attractive for long-lived wines and for blends in which other varieties contribute roundness, perfume or earlier accessibility.
:::

:::detail{#van-bordeaux-naar-de-wereld parent="identiteit-en-oorsprong" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.wine-grapes-cabernet-sauvignon source.uc-davis-bordeaux-grape-varieties"}
### A traveller with a recognisable accent

During the nineteenth century, Cabernet Sauvignon travelled with cuttings, nurseries and growers to Italy, California, Chile, South Africa and Australia, among other places. Its spread accelerated in the twentieth century as putting a grape name on the label became an increasingly direct promise to buyers outside Europe.

The variety did not become a world language by sounding identical everywhere. It became popular because its tannin and fruit offer recognition while place and producer can remain audible. The same grape can be slender and fragrant, firm and classical, or broad and sun-ripened. That range makes Cabernet both international and local.
:::

:::detail{#genetische-ouders parent="identiteit-en-oorsprong" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.bowers-meredith-cabernet-parentage-1997"}
### One red and one white parent

DNA research by John Bowers and Carole Meredith showed in 1997 that Cabernet Sauvignon is the offspring of [[grape.cabernet-franc|Cabernet Franc]] and [[grape.sauvignon-blanc|Sauvignon Blanc]]. They compared microsatellite markers: small inherited segments of DNA that test relationships much more reliably than similar names or leaf shapes. [@source.bowers-meredith-cabernet-parentage-1997]

This does not tell us who made the cross or on what date it occurred. It probably happened spontaneously within Bordeaux's vine population. Genetics can establish parentage; the precise historical event remains out of sight.
:::

:::section{#wijnstok-en-groeicyclus depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon"}
## Vine and growing cycle — late to wake, late to finish

Cabernet Sauvignon buds relatively late and ripens late as well. Late budbreak can spare young shoots some spring-frost risk, but the vine then needs a sufficiently long season to bring sugar, acidity, aroma and tannin into balance. A cool or wet end to summer can make those final steps difficult.

The vine can grow vigorously. It generally forms small bunches and small, round berries. This creates a relatively high proportion of skin to juice; many colour and tannin compounds sit in and just beneath that skin. Berry size and bunch compactness still vary with clone, season and cultivation. [@source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon]
:::

:::detail{#lezen-aan-de-stok parent="wijnstok-en-groeicyclus" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon"}
### Reading leaf, bunch and berry

[[concept.ampelography|Ampelography]] describes and compares grape varieties through features of the shoot tip, leaf, bunch and berry, among others. For Cabernet Sauvignon, Plantgrape mentions young reddish leaves with bronze patches, round mature leaves with deep lobes and round berries. [@source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon]

One feature is rarely decisive. Leaves change with growing conditions and damage, and related varieties may look remarkably similar. Sound field assessment combines several traits; certified planting material or DNA can resolve doubt.
:::

:::detail{#variatie-binnen-het-ras parent="wijnstok-en-groeicyclus" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon source.uc-davis-bordeaux-grape-varieties"}
### One Cabernet vine is not quite another

A clone is a vegetatively propagated line within the same variety. [[concept.clonal-selection|Clonal selection]] may emphasise traits such as yield, bunch architecture, ripening time or sanitary status; it does not turn Cabernet Sauvignon into a new grape variety. France and other wine countries maintain their own selections and numbers, which are not interchangeable without context.

Virus status, [[concept.rootstock|rootstock]], vine age and nursery history also affect performance. A celebrated “heritage clone” is therefore no independent guarantee of quality. The useful question is what combination of plant material, site and management a grower is trying to achieve. [@source.uc-davis-bordeaux-grape-varieties]
:::

## Climate, site and soils — enough time, not limitless heat

A late-ripening grape needs warmth, but “the warmer the better” is a poor rule. Cabernet needs a season in which skins and seeds can mature while acidity and freshness do not disappear. Sunlight, night temperatures, wind, rain, elevation and proximity to sea or river jointly determine the pace.

Soil acts chiefly through water, heat, nutrients and rooting. Freely draining gravel became famous in Bordeaux; Coonawarra is associated instead with red soil over limestone. Neither pours cassis or cedar directly into a berry. They shape how the vine finds water, grows and ripens. [@source.wine-australia-cabernet-sauvignon]
:::

:::detail{#smal-rijpheidsvenster parent="klimaat-ligging-en-bodem" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon source.wine-australia-cabernet-sauvignon"}
### Between green and overripe

With insufficient ripeness, vegetal notes, sharp acidity and angular tannins may dominate. More time on the vine can deepen fruit and texture, but excessive heat or delay can bring stewed fruit, higher alcohol and lost freshness. The desired harvest date is not a pursuit of maximum sugar.

That window moves with site and [[concept.vintage|vintage]]. Cooling coastal influence may lengthen the season in a warm region; a warm gravel rise can help the late grape in a temperate one. Irrigation, crop load and canopy alter the outcome again.
:::

:::detail{#bodem-is-geen-aroma parent="klimaat-ligging-en-bodem" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.wine-grapes-cabernet-sauvignon"}
### Terroir is a system, not the taste of stones

Cabernet is often linked to gravel, yet it also grows on clay, limestone, weathered rock and alluvial soils. [[concept.terroir|Terroir]] is useful only when soil is considered alongside topography, climate, water, plant material and human management.

“Graphite” or “pencil shavings” in a tasting note is an association, not evidence that gravel minerals entered the wine as aroma. Such language can evoke a wine; it cannot replace an explanation of how a vineyard works.
:::

:::section{#regios-en-appellations depth="foundation" source_refs="source.civb-bordeaux-grape-varieties source.wine-grapes-cabernet-sauvignon"}
## Regions and appellations — from Left Bank to world citizen

In Bordeaux, Cabernet Sauvignon is most closely tied to the Left Bank. In [[appellation.pauillac|Pauillac]] and other parts of the Médoc it may be the largest component in a red blend; on the cooler or more clay-rich Right Bank, [[grape.merlot|Merlot]] often carries more weight. Bordeaux labels generally foreground origin and château rather than grape. [@source.civb-bordeaux-grape-varieties]

Beyond Bordeaux, Cabernet frequently appears under its own name. It may be bottled almost alone or blended with Merlot, Cabernet Franc or local partners. Its global reach is vast, but a list of countries teaches less than a handful of clear regional comparisons.
:::

:::detail{#zes-wereldankers parent="regios-en-appellations" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.wine-grapes-cabernet-sauvignon source.wine-australia-cabernet-sauvignon"}
### Six anchors for reading the world

[[appellation.napa-valley|Napa Valley]] made ripe, concentrated Cabernet a Californian calling card. In Australia, [[appellation.coonawarra|Coonawarra]] and [[appellation.margaret-river|Margaret River]] show how moderate conditions, different soils and distinct cellar traditions can produce divergent versions of firm yet fresh Cabernet. [@source.wine-australia-cabernet-sauvignon]

[[appellation.maipo-valley|Maipo Valley]] is a classic Chilean anchor, [[appellation.stellenbosch|Stellenbosch]] a South African one, and [[appellation.bolgheri|Bolgheri]] showed how Cabernet could become part of the Tuscan coast's identity beyond the traditional Sangiovese axis. These names are starting points, not a complete honour roll.
:::

:::detail{#geen-wereldranglijst parent="regios-en-appellations" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.wine-grapes-cabernet-sauvignon"}
### A region is not a style switch

Within one appellation, elevation, exposure, soil, water, clone, yield, picking date and ambition differ. “Napa is ripe” or “Bordeaux is austere” can sketch an initial contrast, but erases warm Bordeaux vintages, cool Napa sites and producer decisions.

Acreage rankings also age. Plantings shift with demand, drought, regulation and replanting. Current hectare comparisons need a dated dataset; this page's durable task is explaining why Cabernet can behave differently across all these places.
:::

:::section{#wijnstijlen-en-smaakprofiel depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon source.wine-grapes-cabernet-sauvignon"}
## Wine styles and sensory profile — dark fruit in a firm frame

Cabernet Sauvignon often makes deeply coloured dry red wine with evident tannin and enough acidity to give the fruit tension. Blackcurrant, blackberry, dark cherry, plum, violet, mint, bay and dried herbs are useful signposts. Not every bottle contains them all.

Structure matters at least as much as aroma. Tannin may feel grainy or drying when young and can make wine suited to food and maturation; acidity keeps it lively. Ripeness, yield and [[concept.extraction|extraction]] determine whether that frame feels refined, juicy, massive or hard. [@source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon]
:::

:::detail{#groen-is-een-spectrum parent="wijnstijlen-en-smaakprofiel" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.awri-green-flavour-cabernet-sauvignon"}
### Bell pepper is not a simple ripeness light

[[concept.methoxypyrazines|Methoxypyrazines]] are highly aromatic compounds that can suggest bell pepper, green bean or other green plants. They belong to Cabernet's aromatic landscape, but “green” may also mean mint, herbs, fresh grass, unripe tannin or other compounds. [@source.awri-green-flavour-cabernet-sauvignon]

Fresh herbal character may add complexity; an overwhelming raw quality can indicate insufficient ripeness or unsuitable handling. Climate, light around the bunch, harvest date and contact with green stems jointly affect the drinker's experience.
:::

:::detail{#tijd-verandert-de-taal parent="wijnstijlen-en-smaakprofiel" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.wine-grapes-cabernet-sauvignon"}
### From cassis to cedar — sometimes

With bottle age, primary fruit may give way to dried berries, tobacco, cedar, leather, earth and savoury notes. Tannins can rearrange and feel softer. Oak maturation may already have contributed spice, toast or vanilla; not everything that smells of cedar comes from the grape.

Ageing capacity is not a fixed expiry date. Concentration, acidity, tannin, cork or other closure, bottle size and storage all matter. Some Cabernets gain nuance for decades; simple or deliberately ripe styles may give most pleasure when young.
:::

:::section{#wijnbouw-en-gevoeligheden depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon"}
## Viticulture and vulnerabilities — the calendar sets the stakes

Because Cabernet ripens late, a grower first asks whether a site can reliably complete its cycle. Late budbreak sometimes helps with spring frost, but flowering, fruit set, summer water and a sufficiently dry harvest window remain decisive. Excess growth can shade bunches; excessive restriction can hamper photosynthesis or ripening.

Plantgrape describes the variety as not very susceptible to grey rot, but susceptible to powdery mildew and trunk diseases such as esca and eutypa dieback. These are tendencies, not immunity or destiny: pressure changes with climate, pruning wounds, hygiene and vineyard management. [@source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon]
:::

:::detail{#bladerdek-en-oogstmoment parent="wijnbouw-en-gevoeligheden" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon source.awri-green-flavour-cabernet-sauvignon"}
### Admit light without roasting the fruit

A vigorous canopy needs guidance: positioning shoots, perhaps removing leaves around the bunch and matching crop load to the vine. More light and air can help ripening and health. In a hot climate, the same leaf removal may worsen sunburn and dehydration.

Harvest is a multiple decision. Sugar, acidity, aromatic maturity, skin and seed need not reach their ideal on the same day. The producer chooses a compromise suited to site and desired style, not one universal Cabernet number.
:::

:::detail{#warmte-water-en-hout parent="wijnbouw-en-gevoeligheden" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon source.wine-australia-cabernet-sauvignon"}
### New risks do not all move in the same direction

Additional warmth may make late ripening more reliable in a marginal climate. Heatwaves, drought and rapid sugar accumulation can elsewhere pull aroma, colour, acidity and tannin maturity apart. Freely draining soil is useful in a wet year and may offer little buffer during prolonged drought.

Rootstock, pruning date, ground cover, canopy, yield and picking date are tools, not recipes. Trunk diseases also make replacement slow and expensive. Adaptation therefore requires decisions across years, not only intervention shortly before harvest.
:::

:::section{#wijnmaken-en-rijping depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon source.wine-australia-cabernet-sauvignon"}
## Winemaking and ageing — gaining structure without walling the wine in

For red wine, juice ferments with skins. Temperature, duration and movement determine how much colour, aroma and tannin is extracted. Cabernet can carry considerable structure, but more [[concept.extraction|extraction]] is not automatically better: ripe skins may give depth while hard seed or stem tannin can dry the wine out.

After fermentation, lots may be assembled before [[concept.elevage|maturation]] in tank, barrel or another vessel. Oak and gradual oxygen exposure can alter texture and aroma. New versus used wood, vessel size and duration are house decisions, not properties Cabernet requires.
:::

:::detail{#solo-of-samen parent="wijnmaken-en-rijping" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.civb-bordeaux-grape-varieties source.wine-australia-cabernet-sauvignon"}
### Solo or together is not a quality ranking

A bottle naming Cabernet Sauvignon may centre the variety. In Bordeaux, Merlot often fills the mid-palate and Cabernet Franc may add perfume and suppleness. In Australia, Cabernet–Shiraz is also a historically important partnership. [@source.wine-australia-cabernet-sauvignon]

Blending does not inherently hide something, and a single-variety wine is not automatically purer. [[concept.assemblage|Blending]] is composition: lots are brought together to achieve balance, continuity or a chosen expression.
:::

:::detail{#extractie-hout-en-hele-trossen parent="wijnmaken-en-rijping" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.awri-green-flavour-cabernet-sauvignon"}
### Every powerful control has a trade-off

Longer [[concept.maceration|skin contact]], higher temperatures and more forceful pump-overs may increase concentration but can also accumulate bitterness or astringency. New oak can add tannin and aroma to a wine that already carries substantial structure. The proportion must be tasted, not derived from a prestige formula.

Much Cabernet is destemmed. Whole bunches or stems can add aroma and texture, but also vegetal character and harder tannin. What works depends on stem ripeness, proportion, extraction and intended style.
:::

:::section{#synoniemen-klonen-en-verwantschap depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon source.bowers-meredith-cabernet-parentage-1997"}
## Synonyms, clones and relationships — family is not identity

Cabernet Sauvignon, Cabernet Franc and Sauvignon Blanc are separate varieties even though the latter two are its genetic parents. “Cabernet” alone may mean Cabernet Sauvignon in conversation, but is less precise and can evoke other Cabernet varieties elsewhere.

A clone remains Cabernet Sauvignon. It has been selected and propagated vegetatively for particular traits or health. A synonym is another name for the same variety; a related grape shares genetic history but is not identical. [@source.bowers-meredith-cabernet-parentage-1997]
:::

:::detail{#selectie-is-geen-nieuw-ras parent="synoniemen-klonen-en-verwantschap" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon source.uc-davis-bordeaux-grape-varieties"}
### Numbers only speak within their system

The current Plantgrape sheet lists no officially recognised synonym for Cabernet Sauvignon in France or elsewhere in the European Union. Historical and local names exist in reference works; that does not make them legally interchangeable on labels. [@source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon]

Clone numbers demand the same care. A French, Californian or other selection belongs to a specific registration, origin and sanitary history. A number alone predicts no wine quality, and similar-looking numbers from different systems need not identify the same material.
:::

:::section{#etiket-en-herkenning depth="foundation" source_refs="source.civb-bordeaux-grape-varieties source.eu-wine-variety-labelling-2019"}
## Labels and recognition — sometimes the grape leads, sometimes the place

Many wines from California, Chile, South Africa or Australia put Cabernet Sauvignon prominently on the label. In classic European origins, place may carry the grape information: a red Pauillac often names château, appellation and vintage while leaving Cabernet in the blend unstated.

A grape name does not everywhere mean “only this grape”. Labelling rules differ by country and wine category. Read origin, producer and any blend information as well.
:::

:::detail{#vijfentachtig-procent-is-niet-honderd parent="etiket-en-herkenning" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.eu-wine-variety-labelling-2019"}
### Eighty-five per cent is not one hundred

Within the European Union, wine naming one grape variety must in principle contain at least 85 per cent of that variety, after the exceptions set out in the regulation. Different conditions apply when two or more varieties are named, and their order is not independent of proportion. [@source.eu-wine-variety-labelling-2019]

This is a legal example, not a worldwide rule. An American, Chilean or Australian bottle falls under another system. “Cabernet Sauvignon” on the front is an important clue, but its precise meaning comes from the applicable law.
:::

:::detail{#kijken-is-niet-bewijzen parent="etiket-en-herkenning" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon"}
### A beautiful leaf is not proof

The historical plate above brings together features a field observer can compare. It is instructive, but one bunch photograph or leaf cannot establish identity. Growth, disease, season and kinship can make appearance misleading.

For a vineyard, planting-material certificates, multiple ampelographic traits and genetic analysis where needed are more reliable. For a bottle, the situation differs: the label is a regulated claim, not a botanical test the drinker can perform.
:::

:::section{#hedendaagse-ontwikkelingen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.wine-australia-cabernet-sauvignon source.plantgrape-cabernet-sauvignon"}
## Contemporary developments — a late grape in a warmer age

Cabernet Sauvignon shows why climate change is not a simple line towards “better ripeness”. Warmer summers may help cool sites, while extreme heat, drought and early sugar accumulation threaten freshness and even phenolic ripening elsewhere. The same long cycle that once posed a risk can become both advantage and vulnerability.

The response begins with suitable sites and healthy planting material, then extends through soil and canopy management, water strategy, [[concept.rootstock|rootstock]], harvest timing, extraction and blending. No single control solves everything.
:::

:::detail{#aanpassen-zonder-een-recept parent="hedendaagse-ontwikkelingen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.wine-australia-cabernet-sauvignon"}
### Adapting without fixing one identity

In a hot, dry region, shading bunches may become more important; in a cool, damp one, admitting air and light remains useful. A later-ripening clone may spread risk in one place and increase harvest risk in another. Retaining and draining water can even be necessary within the same vineyard.

There is therefore no single “future of Cabernet” style. The variety will also change as growers choose different sites, plant material and picking dates, and as consumers and rules move with them.
:::

:::detail{#cijfers-horen-een-datum parent="hedendaagse-ontwikkelingen" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.wine-australia-cabernet-sauvignon"}
### Global fame is not a fixed acreage figure

Cabernet Sauvignon ranks among the world's most planted wine grapes, but a position or hectare total is a snapshot. New planting, removal, drought, disease and changing demand can shift the order. [@source.wine-australia-cabernet-sauvignon]

This entity therefore keeps the durable explanation. A current world ranking belongs in a dated dataset with year, geographic coverage and method. That keeps a changing figure visibly changeable without repeatedly rewriting the story of the grape.
:::
