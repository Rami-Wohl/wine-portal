Sauvignon Blanc can run through a glass of white wine like a crisp bright line: citrus, herbs, juicy fruit and acidity that keeps everything awake. Yet behind that immediate recognition sits a remarkably versatile variety. It can appear taut and stony in the Loire, exuberantly tropical in Marlborough, as a partner to Sémillon in Bordeaux, and as anything from crystalline to creamy and oak-matured in the cellar. [@source.plantgrape-sauvignon] [@source.civb-sauvignon-blanc]

Those differences are not deviations from one correct Sauvignon. They arise because growth, ripeness, light, water, harvest date, fermentation and oxygen each turn a different dial. Understanding the variety therefore means learning not one smell, but how freshness and aroma are made.

## Identity and origins — French by origin, global in importance

Sauvignon Blanc is a white *Vitis vinifera* variety and one of the defining aromatic grapes of the modern wine world. France is its historical core. Plantgrape keeps two possible areas of origin open: central France and the south-west. That fits its deep roots in both the [[region.loire-valley|Loire Valley]] and [[region.bordeaux|Bordeaux]], but does not prove that either can claim its birthplace exclusively. [@source.plantgrape-sauvignon]

The variety matters because it can combine dependable freshness with pronounced aroma in many places. It appears as a single-variety wine, but also plays a substantial role in [[concept.assemblage|blends]]: compositions in which different varieties or lots complement one another.
:::

:::detail{#twee-franse-thuishavens parent="identiteit-en-oorsprong" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.plantgrape-sauvignon source.inao-sancerre-overview source.civb-sauvignon-blanc"}
### Two French homes, two public faces

In the Loire, Sauvignon Blanc became closely tied to place names such as [[appellation.sancerre|Sancerre]] and [[appellation.pouilly-fume|Pouilly-Fumé]]. It generally stands alone in the white wine there, even when its name is absent from the front label. Bordeaux presents another face: dry and sweet white wines in which Sauvignon may sit beside [[grape.semillon|Sémillon]], [[grape.muscadelle|Muscadelle]] or [[grape.sauvignon-gris|Sauvignon Gris]]. [@source.inao-sancerre-overview] [@source.civb-sauvignon-blanc]

Marlborough's international breakthrough added a third powerful image in the late twentieth century: intensely aromatic, instantly recognisable wine with bright fruit and herbaceousness. For many drinkers the variety consequently became not just an ingredient in a wine of place, but a destination in its own right.
:::

:::detail{#familie-zonder-geboorteakte parent="identiteit-en-oorsprong" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.plantgrape-sauvignon source.bowers-meredith-cabernet-parentage-1997"}
### Family helps, but provides no birth certificate

Genetic analyses place Sauvignon close to [[grape.savagnin|Savagnin]]. A precise parent combination has not thereby been demonstrated; “closely related” is deliberately more cautious than “offspring of”. What is established is that Sauvignon Blanc and [[grape.cabernet-franc|Cabernet Franc]] are the parents of [[grape.cabernet-sauvignon|Cabernet Sauvignon]]. [@source.plantgrape-sauvignon] [@source.bowers-meredith-cabernet-parentage-1997]

That unexpected parentage connects an aromatic white grape to a famous dark-skinned descendant. It explains kinship, not flavour in a straight line. Cabernet Sauvignon is not simply Sauvignon Blanc with colour: genes recombine, and vineyard and cellar transform the outcome further.
:::

:::section{#wijnstok-en-groeicyclus depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-sauvignon"}
## Vine and growing cycle — abundant leaves around small bunches

Sauvignon grows vigorously and can produce abundant vegetation, particularly in young vineyards. Its bunches and berries are small; compact bunches demand air and attention in humid conditions. Plantgrape places both budbreak and ripening around the middle of its French reference scale. Sauvignon is therefore neither exceptionally early nor late, but remains highly dependent on a well-managed season. [@source.plantgrape-sauvignon]

An exuberant vine can shade its own fruit. Too much foliage holds moisture, slows berry development and shifts aromatic balance. Managing growth does not mean stripping the plant bare, but balancing light, shelter and air.
:::

:::detail{#een-kalender-met-een-vergelijkingspunt parent="wijnstok-en-groeicyclus" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.plantgrape-sauvignon"}
### A calendar with a reference point

Plantgrape records budbreak seven days after Chasselas and ripeness about two and a half weeks after that reference variety. These figures are relative measurements from a fixed collection, not a universal harvest calendar. Latitude, elevation, soil water, pruning, crop load and [[concept.vintage|vintage]] move the actual date. [@source.plantgrape-sauvignon]

Wine also demands more than sugar. Acidity, aroma precursors, skin condition and health need not reach their ideal points together. Harvest date is therefore a choice between different forms of ripeness, not the reading of a single number.
:::

:::detail{#blad-tros-en-bes-als-patroon parent="wijnstok-en-groeicyclus" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.plantgrape-sauvignon"}
### Leaf, bunch and berry form a pattern

[[concept.ampelography|Ampelographers]] identify Sauvignon partly by a densely hairy young shoot tip, yellow young leaves sometimes marked with bronze, small to medium five-lobed adult leaves with a blistered surface, and ellipsoid berries. No single feature decides the matter. [@source.plantgrape-sauvignon]

Leaves change through the season and disease or vigour may blur their form. Ampelography therefore compares several parts of the vine; dependable propagation also relies on plant-material records and, where necessary, genetic verification.
:::

## Climate, site and soils — freshness needs balance, not cold alone

Sauvignon can ripen in cool to warm wine regions, but its stylistic window moves markedly. Cooler conditions and earlier picking may emphasise acidity, citrus and green notes. Greater warmth and ripeness may make the fruit rounder and more tropical, while extreme heat or a long wait can cost freshness and aromatic tension. [@source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-aroma-2012]

Soil does not write an aroma directly into the grape. It affects how much water, heat and nutrition the roots find, and therefore how vigorously the vine grows and how evenly its fruit ripens. A suitable site keeps growth manageable and supplies enough water without prolonged saturation.
:::

:::detail{#licht-rond-de-tros parent="klimaat-ligging-en-bodem" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.plantgrape-sauvignon source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-aroma-2012"}
### Light around the bunch changes the aromatic field

A bunch's microclimate may differ from what a weather station reports. Shade, leaf temperature and airflow determine together how quickly berries dry and ripen. Sauvignon aroma research shows that light, temperature and ripeness influence compounds later perceived as green or fruity smells. [@source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-aroma-2012]

More sun is not automatically better. Careful leaf removal can reduce moisture and promote ripening; excessive exposure may overheat or scorch berries. The goal is a healthy, even bunch zone adapted to place and season.
:::

:::detail{#de-val-van-mineraliteit parent="klimaat-ligging-en-bodem" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-sancerre-overview source.inao-pouilly-fume-overview"}
### Soil names are a beginning, not tasting proof

Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé include limestone, marl and flint-bearing soils; site and soil vary again within each appellation. Producers and drinkers use words such as chalky, stony or mineral to describe impressions of aroma, taste and texture. That does not mean limestone or flint literally becomes a flavouring in the wine. [@source.inao-sancerre-overview] [@source.inao-pouilly-fume-overview]

A careful comparison therefore follows the physical chain first: soil structure affects water, rooting, heat and growth; these affect the fruit; fermentation and [[concept.elevage|maturation]] then shape the wine. “Mineral” can remain useful provided it does not skip those steps.
:::

:::section{#regios-en-appellations depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-sancerre-overview source.inao-pouilly-fume-overview source.civb-sauvignon-blanc source.nzwine-marlborough"}
## Regions and appellations — Loire, Bordeaux and Marlborough as a compass

Three areas quickly reveal Sauvignon's range. Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé show the central Loire, where white wine rests on Sauvignon and place takes priority on the label. Bordeaux shows the variety building both dry freshness and sweet wine. [[region.marlborough|Marlborough]] made a powerful, fruity and herbaceous style into a worldwide reference. [@source.inao-sancerre-overview] [@source.inao-pouilly-fume-overview] [@source.civb-sauvignon-blanc] [@source.nzwine-marlborough]

These three are not a world ranking. Sauvignon is also important in Chile, South Africa, California, Australia, Austria and north-eastern Italy, among others. Within every country, ocean influence, elevation, wind, water and producer matter more than the flag alone.
:::

:::detail{#loire-en-bordeaux parent="regios-en-appellations" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-sancerre-overview source.inao-pouilly-fume-overview source.inao-pessac-leognan-blanc source.civb-sauvignon-blanc"}
### From Loire soloist to Bordeaux conversationalist

Sancerre occupies hills on the Loire's left bank; Pouilly-Fumé sits farther upstream on the right. Both connect Sauvignon with dry white wine, but contain varied soils and producer choices. At Pouilly, the grape is also called *Blanc Fumé* locally; according to INAO, the name refers to the smoky bloom on ripe berries as well as local aromatic language. [@source.inao-sancerre-overview] [@source.inao-pouilly-fume-overview]

In Bordeaux, Sauvignon provides tension and aroma in dry whites from places including [[appellation.pessac-leognan|Pessac-Léognan]], [[appellation.graves|Graves]] and [[appellation.entre-deux-mers|Entre-deux-Mers]]. Barrel fermentation or maturation can give Pessac-Léognan a richer face. In [[appellation.sauternes|Sauternes]], Sauvignon may form a smaller share beside Sémillon, contributing freshness and aromatic tension to sweet wine. [@source.inao-pessac-leognan-blanc] [@source.civb-sauvignon-blanc]
:::

:::detail{#marlborough-en-de-wereld parent="regios-en-appellations" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.nzwine-marlborough source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-aroma-2012"}
### Marlborough proved that intensity can also have precision

Marlborough carried New Zealand emphatically onto the international wine stage in the 1980s. Much of its Sauvignon combines pronounced citrus and tropical fruit with herbaceousness and firm acidity. Even within the region, the Wairau and Awatere valleys differ in wind, temperature, soil and ripening pattern; “Marlborough style” is therefore a family, not a formula. [@source.nzwine-marlborough]

Worldwide spread also made techniques mobile. Protected juice handling, selected yeast, cool fermentation and rapid bottling can support a fruit-led aromatic style. Other producers seek site expression, spontaneous fermentation, lees, barrel or longer maturation. International Sauvignon is not a copying machine but a debate about clarity, texture and place.
:::

:::section{#wijnstijlen-en-smaakprofiel depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-sauvignon source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-aroma-2012"}
## Wine styles and sensory profile — green and tropical are not opposites

Sauvignon Blanc generally makes dry white wine with clear [[concept.acidity|acidity]] and pronounced aroma. Citrus, gooseberry, blackcurrant, boxwood, fresh grass, green herbs, passionfruit, guava and stone fruit can all occur within its spectrum. They are recognition points, not a checklist or guarantee. [@source.plantgrape-sauvignon] [@source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-aroma-2012]

A wine can smell green and tropical at the same time. Ripeness, grape health, oxygen and fermentation determine which signals lead. Texture varies too: from light and taut to broad, creamy or sweet.
:::

:::detail{#twee-aromafamilies parent="wijnstijlen-en-smaakprofiel" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-aroma-2012"}
### Two aroma families, many shades between

[[concept.methoxypyrazines|Methoxypyrazines]] are highly aromatic compounds that may recall capsicum, leaf and green plants. Their presence begins in the grape and changes with ripeness and bunch microclimate. [[concept.volatile-thiols|Volatile thiols]] may instead suggest grapefruit, passionfruit, guava or boxwood. Some are released from odourless precursors when yeast works during fermentation. [@source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-aroma-2012]

Sauvignon aroma is consequently not a simple print of a ripe berry. The vineyard supplies the material, fermentation makes some smells perceptible, and other compounds such as esters add further fruit. What smells “typical” is an interaction.
:::

:::detail{#fris-jong-en-toch-houdbaar parent="wijnstijlen-en-smaakprofiel" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-ageing-2015 source.inao-pessac-leognan-blanc"}
### Fresh and youthful does not mean short-lived

Much Sauvignon is drunk for its young citrus and green or tropical aroma. Those aromas are sensitive to oxygen and change during bottle maturation. Fruit may retreat; with suitable concentration, acidity, closure and storage, wax, honey, dried herbs, smoky or savoury nuances may emerge. [@source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-ageing-2015]

A taut stainless-steel example and a barrel-matured white Pessac-Léognan do not follow the same curve. Ageing potential comes from the whole wine, not the variety name. Some bottles gain complexity; others mainly lose brightness.
:::

:::section{#wijnbouw-en-gevoeligheden depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-sauvignon"}
## Viticulture and vulnerabilities — vigour demands openness

Sauvignon's natural strength is both reserve and risk. Fertile soil may encourage abundant foliage, leaving bunches shaded and humid. Plantgrape therefore recommends moderately fertile sites, restrained plant material, suitable pruning and training that helps create a favourable microclimate around the bunches. [@source.plantgrape-sauvignon]

That is not a universal pruning prescription. Shade may be essential in a hot, dry area; openness may take precedence in a humid one. The same intervention also changes yield, ripening and sunburn risk.
:::

:::detail{#rot-meeldauw-en-houtziekten parent="wijnbouw-en-gevoeligheden" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.plantgrape-sauvignon"}
### Compact fruit makes timing important

Plantgrape describes Sauvignon as highly susceptible to grey rot, partly because of its compact bunches, and to trunk diseases; powdery mildew is another clear vulnerability. The same source considers it less susceptible to downy mildew. These are varietal tendencies, not forecasts for every vineyard. [@source.plantgrape-sauvignon]

Rain around ripening makes healthy airflow and harvest selection especially important. In dry years attention turns instead to water stress and sun protection. Disease management, canopy and harvest date cannot therefore be designed separately.
:::

:::section{#wijnmaken-en-rijping depth="foundation" source_refs="source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-aroma-2012 source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-ageing-2015"}
## Winemaking and ageing — aroma also begins in the cellar

After pressing, Sauvignon generally ferments without skins into white wine. How rapidly and protectively the grapes are handled, how clear the juice is, which yeast ferments it and at what temperature all steer aroma and texture. Stainless steel and cool fermentation are often used to retain fresh fruit, but represent only one stylistic route. [@source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-aroma-2012]

Some producers ferment or mature in wood, keep wine on yeast cells or deliberately allow more oxygen contact. Aromatic brightness may then yield some space to breadth, savour and longer development.
:::

:::detail{#van-voorloper-naar-geur parent="wijnmaken-en-rijping" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-aroma-2012"}
### Yeast opens aromatic doors

Some fruity Sauvignon compounds exist in must as odourless precursors. During fermentation, yeast can release volatile thiols from them. Yeast strain, temperature, juice treatment, nutrition and oxygen influence how much is formed and retained. [@source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-aroma-2012]

This explains why two lots from the same vineyard can smell different after distinct fermentations. It does not mean a cellar can manufacture any desired aroma at will: the grape must supply suitable precursors and all compounds interact.
:::

:::detail{#lies-hout-en-zuurstof parent="wijnmaken-en-rijping" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-ageing-2015 source.inao-pessac-leognan-blanc"}
### Lees, wood and oxygen exchange brightness for shape

During [[concept.lees-ageing|lees ageing]], wine remains in contact with spent yeast cells. This can change mouthfeel and stability; stirring increases that contact. Wood can admit oxygen gradually and contribute aroma or [[concept.tannin|tannin]]. In white Pessac-Léognan, toasted or nutty notes belong to the possible style alongside citrus and exotic fruit. [@source.inao-pessac-leognan-blanc]

Oxygen is both tool and threat here. Controlled contact may open and integrate a wine; excess can break down vulnerable fruit aromas and accelerate browning. Barrel, tank, lees, closure and storage time make one continuous oxygen history. [@source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-ageing-2015]
:::

:::section{#synoniemen-klonen-en-verwantschap depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-sauvignon source.inao-pouilly-fume-overview"}
## Synonyms, clones and relationships — Sauvignon is a name with side paths

The variety is officially called Sauvignon in France; Sauvignon Blanc is its best-known international name. Plantgrape also lists *Zeleni sauvignon* within officially used European Union synonymy. *Blanc Fumé* persists as a local name in Pouilly. The legal status of such a name varies by country and appellation. [@source.plantgrape-sauvignon] [@source.inao-pouilly-fume-overview]

[[grape.sauvignon-gris|Sauvignon Gris]] is a separately registered variant, not another spelling for the same white berry. A clone is different again: selected material within the variety, propagated to preserve traits reliably.
:::

:::detail{#klonen-zijn-geen-stijlknoppen parent="synoniemen-klonen-en-verwantschap" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.plantgrape-sauvignon"}
### Clones preserve difference; they do not dictate wine

Plantgrape records eighteen certified Sauvignon clones in France and reports conservatories in Bordeaux and the Loire holding hundreds of selections. [[concept.clonal-selection|Clonal selection]] can preserve differences in yield, bunch architecture, aroma and health. [@source.plantgrape-sauvignon]

A clone number is neither a flavour guarantee nor a quality ranking. [[concept.rootstock|Rootstock]], soil, virus status, season, yield and management continue to matter. Conservation is valuable precisely because future vineyards may require traits different from yesterday's popular selections.
:::

:::section{#etiket-en-herkenning depth="foundation" source_refs="source.eu-wine-variety-labelling-2019 source.inao-sancerre-overview source.inao-pouilly-fume-overview"}
## Labels and recognition — Sauvignon may be prominent or absent

In Marlborough and many other international regions, Sauvignon Blanc often appears clearly on the front label. A white Sancerre or Pouilly-Fumé leads with place instead; the drinker must know that Sauvignon underpins the white wine. Bordeaux may foreground appellation and château while the wine blends several white varieties. [@source.inao-sancerre-overview] [@source.inao-pouilly-fume-overview]

Read a label as a stack of claims: origin, producer, vintage, possible variety and sometimes a technique such as barrel maturation. No single line tells you how the wine will taste.
:::

:::detail{#de-vijfentachtig-procent-regel parent="etiket-en-herkenning" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.eu-wine-variety-labelling-2019"}
### One variety name need not mean one hundred per cent

Within the European Union, naming one grape variety generally requires at least 85 per cent of the wine to come from that variety, subject to the applicable conditions and exceptions. “Sauvignon Blanc” on a label may therefore permit a smaller share of other authorised grapes. [@source.eu-wine-variety-labelling-2019]

An appellation may impose stricter or differently structured rules. White Sancerre, Pouilly-Fumé and Pessac-Léognan consequently make different promises even when Sauvignon plays a role in all three.
:::

:::detail{#proeven-is-geen-identificatietest parent="etiket-en-herkenning" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-aroma-2012 source.plantgrape-sauvignon"}
### A recognisable smell remains a clue

Capsicum, boxwood, grapefruit or passionfruit may suggest Sauvignon, but none belongs exclusively to this variety. Fermentation can amplify aromas that occur in other grapes too; ripeness and oxygen can obscure typical signals. [@source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-aroma-2012]

Nor can leaf and bunch provide conclusive proof from one photograph. Tasting and looking build a hypothesis; provenance records, certified material and genetic analysis establish botanical identity.
:::

:::section{#hedendaagse-ontwikkelingen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-sauvignon source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-aroma-2012"}
## Contemporary developments — preserving freshness without freezing style

In warmer seasons Sauvignon may accumulate sugar earlier and lose acidity while drought and sunlight place the fruit under further pressure. In cool or humid areas, vigour and rot remain important. Climate change makes one fixed response impossible: the extra shade that protects fruit in heat may hinder ripening and drying elsewhere.

The central question is therefore not how every region can copy a famous style, but how Sauvignon remains healthy, ripe and lively in its own place. That may mean different sites, plant material, canopies, crop loads or harvest dates, and a reconsideration of oxygen and texture in the cellar.
:::

:::detail{#van-aromawedloop-naar-herkomst parent="hedendaagse-ontwikkelingen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.nzwine-marlborough source.coetzee-du-toit-sauvignon-ageing-2015"}
### Not every Sauvignon needs to shout loudest

The international popularity of an intense young style made aromatic power easy to recognise. At the same time, producers in Marlborough and elsewhere are exploring subregional differences, older vines, wild or mixed fermentation, lees contact and barrel. The question then shifts from maximum aroma to the relationship between aroma, texture, place and time. [@source.nzwine-marlborough]

This is not a farewell to freshness. It broadens the language: Sauvignon can be immediate and exuberant, but also restrained, savoury and suitable for bottle maturation. Stylistic diversity is more credible when it grows from clearly described choices than from vague words such as “premium”.
:::

:::detail{#cijfers-horen-bij-een-meetjaar parent="hedendaagse-ontwikkelingen" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.plantgrape-sauvignon"}
### Global fame is not a timeless hectare figure

Plantgrape shows strong growth in France's Sauvignon area across the second half of the twentieth and beginning of the twenty-first centuries. Global rankings likewise change through new planting, removal, disease, drought and market demand. [@source.plantgrape-sauvignon]

This overview therefore preserves the durable explanation of why the variety spread. Exact areas and positions belong in a dated dataset or narrative, with measurement year and definition visible.
:::

:::in-the-glass{#waarom-in-het-glas depth="foundation"}
Do not taste Sauvignon as an aroma quiz. Find acidity and texture first: taut or round, light or concentrated. Then place green, citrus and tropical impressions beside one another and ask what fermentation, lees, wood or bottle age may have added. A far more interesting construction emerges behind the recognisable scent.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Sauvignon Blanc is not a fixed choice between “green” and “tropical”. Through growth, light and ripeness, the vine supplies aromatic potential; yeast, oxygen and maturation turn it into different wines. Place defines the field, and the producer chooses how loudly each voice speaks.
:::

:::section{#verder-ontdekken depth="foundation"}
## Explore further — follow place, aroma or blend

Follow the variety to Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé for its Loire solo role, to Pessac-Léognan and Sauternes for its dry and sweet Bordeaux roles, and to Marlborough for its modern international breakthrough. Compare it with blending partner Sémillon, colour variant Sauvignon Gris and descendant Cabernet Sauvignon.

To unravel aroma, continue to methoxypyrazines and volatile thiols. For texture, follow lees ageing, wood and oxygen; to read the plant, follow ampelography and clonal selection. Sauvignon then remains not a handful of smells, but a network of vine, place and cellar.
:::
