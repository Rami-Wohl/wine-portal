Sémillon is a grape with more than one life. In dry white Bordeaux it contributes breadth and texture; in Sauternes and Barsac noble rot can transform it into concentrated sweet wine; and in Hunter Valley it starts out light, lemony and unoaked before developing almost improbable depth in bottle. [@source.civb-semillon] [@source.wine-australia-semillon]

These styles can seem to come from different varieties. They are connected by Sémillon's rounded berries, relatively thin skins, moderate to low perception of acidity when ripe, and remarkable response to picking time, fungus, vessel and age. Remembering only “honeyed” misses its taut young face; seeing only Bordeaux misses one of Australia's classic white wines.

## Identity and origins — one variety, three classic forms

Sémillon is a white *Vitis vinifera* variety. Plantgrape places its origin in the vineyards of [[region.bordeaux|Bordeaux]], where it has long played a leading role in both dry and sweet white wine. From France it travelled to other wine countries; Australia gave it a second historic centre and a distinctive dry style. [@source.plantgrape-semillon] [@source.wine-australia-semillon]

Its importance does not rest on one forceful aroma. Sémillon is valued for what it can add to a wine's construction: volume, waxy or silken texture, capacity to age and, depending on harvest and cellar, room for citrus, ripe fruit, herbs, toast and honeyed notes.
:::

:::detail{#oorsprong-zonder-mythe parent="identiteit-en-geschiedenis" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.plantgrape-semillon"}
### A Bordeaux origin without a precise birth certificate

The current French variety register names the Bordeaux vineyards as the origin. That is firmer than a loose tradition, but provides no year, first village or known parental combination. Such precision cannot be inferred retrospectively from the variety's long presence. [@source.plantgrape-semillon]

Genetic relationships also require care. Sémillon is often placed alongside [[grape.sauvignon-blanc|Sauvignon Blanc]] because they form famous wines together; partnership in an [[concept.assemblage|assemblage]] is not evidence of parentage. This page deliberately separates documented origin, historical movement and winemaking partners.
:::

:::section{#wijnstok-en-groeicyclus depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-semillon"}
## Vine and growing cycle — productive, adaptable and responsive to site

Sémillon has moderate vigour and can be fairly productive. Plantgrape describes medium-sized bunches with large, rounded berries. The vine responds strongly to the fertility of its surroundings: the same variety can carry much more fruit in vigorous conditions than on a leaner or drier site. [@source.plantgrape-semillon]

Crop management is therefore part of the style story. A generous crop need not make poor wine, but an overloaded or dense vine can ripen less evenly and remain damp for longer. The desired balance depends on climate, soil water, vine age and intended wine.
:::

:::detail{#tros-blad-en-fenologie parent="wijnstok-en-groeicyclus" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.plantgrape-semillon"}
### Phenology is a comparison, not a calendar

In the French reference collection, Sémillon buds several days after Chasselas and reaches the second ripening period, around two and a half weeks after that reference variety. Such data help compare varieties but do not predict a local harvest date. Site, water, pruning, crop and [[concept.vintage|vintage]] shift the reality. [@source.plantgrape-semillon]

[[concept.ampelography|Ampelographers]] examine features including the hairy young shoot tip, young green leaves with bronze patches, light- to medium-green five-lobed leaves with a regularly blistered surface, and rounded berries. The pattern matters; no photograph or detached leaf is conclusive proof.
:::

## Climate, site and soils — water and growth set the field of play

Sémillon can ripen in diverse climates, from maritime south-west France to warm Australian regions. The central question is not whether a site is simply warm or cool, but how quickly sugar rises, acidity falls, skins remain sound and the vine finds water. Early picking can retain freshness; longer hanging can bring greater maturity or, under very particular conditions, botrytis concentration.

Plantgrape identifies gravel and clay-limestone as suitable French soil environments. Sandy alluvial flats are important for Sémillon in Hunter Valley. These terms are not flavour recipes: soil works through water, rooting, temperature, nutrition and vigour. [@source.plantgrape-semillon] [@source.wine-australia-hunter-valley]
:::

:::detail{#vruchtbaarheid-water-en-opbrengst parent="klimaat-bodem-en-wijngaard" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.plantgrape-semillon"}
### Fertility can move yields substantially

According to Plantgrape, Sémillon responds particularly strongly to agronomic fertility, so yields may vary greatly by site. Growth may become more expansive in fertile or water-rich ground, requiring deliberate management of canopy, bunch zone and crop. On a drier site, adequate water becomes the limiting factor instead. [@source.plantgrape-semillon]

The aim is not universally low yield, but a healthy relationship between leaves, fruit and available water. That relationship partly determines whether grapes suit a light early-picked wine, a riper dry wine or an extended wait for selective noble rot.
:::

:::section{#regios-en-appellations depth="foundation" source_refs="source.civb-semillon source.wine-australia-semillon source.wine-australia-hunter-valley source.inao-sauternes-overview source.inao-pessac-leognan-blanc"}
## Regions and appellations — Bordeaux and Australia as two main axes

Bordeaux and Australia provide the quickest compass. In Bordeaux, Sémillon may form part of fresh dry blends, richer barrel-aged whites and noble sweet wines. In Australia it appears both as a varietal wine and alongside Sauvignon Blanc; [[region.hunter-valley|Hunter Valley]] has the most distinctive and internationally benchmarked dry tradition. [@source.civb-semillon] [@source.wine-australia-semillon]

Sémillon also grows in South Africa, Chile, Argentina, New Zealand and the United States, among other places. That worldwide presence produces no fourth fixed style recipe. Old vines, local climate, picking date and producer choice are more informative than the national flag alone.
:::

:::detail{#bordeaux-drie-rollen parent="regios-en-appellations" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.civb-semillon source.inao-sauternes-overview source.inao-pessac-leognan-blanc"}
### Bordeaux holds three roles side by side

In straightforward and ambitious dry white Bordeaux, Sémillon can provide volume and texture alongside Sauvignon Blanc's aroma and tension. In [[appellation.pessac-leognan|Pessac-Léognan]] and [[appellation.graves|Graves]], riper fruit, barrel fermentation and ageing can shape a more layered, age-worthy white wine. Proportions and cellar routes vary by producer. [@source.inao-pessac-leognan-blanc]

In [[appellation.sauternes|Sauternes]] and [[appellation.barsac|Barsac]], another possibility takes centre stage: fruit concentrated by noble rot and harvested in successive selections. The Sauternes appellation comprises five communes, including Barsac; wine from Barsac may under the rules carry its own name or Sauternes. Local rules and geography belong on the appellation pages, not to the grape alone. [@source.inao-sauternes-overview]
:::

:::detail{#hunter-valley-en-de-wereld parent="regios-en-appellations" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.wine-australia-semillon source.wine-australia-hunter-valley"}
### Hunter Valley shows that warmth need not mean weight

Hunter Valley is warm and humid, yet its classic Sémillon is picked early, fermented dry, generally without overt oak and bottled young. The young wine can be light, taut and lemony, with modest alcohol. Bottle age then brings greater breadth and notes reminiscent of toast, wax, honey or nuts. [@source.wine-australia-hunter-valley]

Elsewhere in Australia there are richer, sometimes barrel-fermented styles and Sauvignon-Sémillon blends, notably associated with [[appellation.margaret-river|Margaret River]]. Beyond Australia, too, the grape can appear as a dry varietal, blending partner or sweet wine. Hunter is not a universal norm, but compelling evidence that mature complexity need not come from late picking or new oak. [@source.wine-australia-semillon]
:::

:::section{#wijnstijlen-en-smaakprofiel depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-semillon source.civb-semillon source.wine-australia-semillon"}
## Wine styles and sensory profile — from a line of lemon to golden richness

Young dry Sémillon may smell discreetly of citrus, apple, herbs or light wax. Picked riper and aged more broadly, it can become fuller, with pear, quince, stone fruit and softer texture. Botrytis wine adds concentrated fruit, honey, spice and a pronounced interplay of sweetness and acidity. These are tendencies, not aromatic guarantees. [@source.civb-semillon] [@source.wine-australia-semillon]

Structurally, the variety is often more important than its aromatic volume suggests. It can add weight and length while Sauvignon Blanc supplies fragrance and [[concept.acidity|acid tension]]. In an early-picked Hunter style, Sémillon can instead appear slender and vivid. Origin and harvest decide which side emerges.
:::

:::detail{#drie-stijlfamilies parent="wijnstijlen-en-smaakprofiel" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.civb-semillon source.wine-australia-semillon source.wine-australia-hunter-valley"}
### Three style families, not three sealed boxes

A practical memory aid distinguishes early-picked, dry and often unoaked wine; riper dry wine with greater texture and possible barrel use; and sweet wine from shrivelled or noble-rotted fruit. Each family contains lighter, richer, younger and longer-aged examples.

The boundaries also overlap. A dry wine may be partly barrel-fermented, a blend may combine several picking dates, and not every sweet Sémillon is a botrytis wine. The category provides orientation, but production method and origin supply the real explanation.
:::

:::detail{#flesrijping-zonder-hout parent="wijnstijlen-en-smaakprofiel" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.wine-australia-hunter-valley"}
### Toast on the nose does not prove barrel ageing

Mature Hunter Valley Sémillon can develop aromas associated with toast, honey or vanilla even when it has not been raised in new oak. Bottle ageing changes existing aroma compounds and their relationships; tasting words do not automatically reveal a cellar action. [@source.wine-australia-hunter-valley]

Sémillon is therefore a useful warning against reasoning backwards. A toasty note may fit oak, but does not prove a barrel. Check the producer, method and age before turning an aroma into production history.
:::

:::section{#botrytis-en-zoete-wijn depth="foundation" source_refs="source.civb-semillon source.inao-sauternes-overview"}
## Botrytis and sweet wine — when infection becomes concentration

Sémillon's relatively thin skin leaves it susceptible to fungal infection. Under a particular alternation of damp and drying conditions, *Botrytis cinerea* can make the skin porous and allow water to evaporate from the berry. Sugar, acidity and flavour compounds become concentrated: this is [[concept.botrytis|noble rot]]. [@source.civb-semillon]

Harvest does not simply occur when the entire parcel is “ready”. Pickers may pass through the same rows several times, taking only suitable berries or bunches. That produces little, concentrated juice and makes the wine labour-intensive. Sauvignon Blanc and [[grape.muscadelle|Muscadelle]] can add further freshness or fragrance to the blend.
:::

:::detail{#edel-of-grijs parent="botrytis-en-zoete-wijn" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.plantgrape-semillon source.inao-sauternes-overview"}
### Noble and grey rot are not two tidy fungal species

The same fungus can be desirable or destructive. Sustained wetness, damaged berries or insufficient drying may encourage grey rot and loss of sound fruit. Morning moisture followed by drying conditions can instead support the gradual concentration sought in noble-sweet viticulture. The result depends on weather, bunch condition, timing and selection.

Sémillon's susceptibility is therefore not a general virtue. For dry wine, growers usually seek sound berries; for noble-sweet wine, only a particular form and stage of infection is wanted. Plantgrape explicitly identifies the variety as susceptible to grey rot. [@source.plantgrape-semillon]
:::

:::section{#wijnbouw-en-risicos depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-semillon"}
## Viticulture and vulnerabilities — guarding health without reducing the variety to disease

Alongside rot susceptibility, Plantgrape identifies black rot on young leaves, mites and leafhoppers as concerns; according to the same source, the variety is less susceptible to powdery mildew and eutypa dieback. Such relative susceptibilities are not predictions for every parcel. Climate, infection pressure, planting material and management determine actual risk. [@source.plantgrape-semillon]

The vineyard task is integrated: manage vigour and crop, retain air and protection around the bunches, limit water stress and keep the harvest aim clear. A parcel intended for an early dry pick follows a different calendar from one held in hope of botrytis selection.
:::

:::detail{#dunne-schil-en-ziektedruk parent="wijnbouw-en-risicos" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.plantgrape-semillon source.wine-australia-semillon"}
### Thin skin increases both opportunity and vulnerability

A relatively thin skin facilitates drying by botrytis under suitable conditions, but also increases the need to monitor rain, damage and rot. According to Wine Australia, botrytis wine is not the normal route in many Australian conditions; there, healthy grapes are often picked early for dry wine. [@source.wine-australia-semillon]

That contrast shows why a varietal trait should never be judged outside its environment. Susceptibility can be an opportunity in one production model and a direct threat in another.
:::

:::section{#wijnmaken-en-rijping depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-semillon source.wine-australia-semillon source.civb-semillon"}
## Winemaking and ageing — picking time writes the first sentence

The cellar can keep Sémillon fresh and restrained or make it broad and layered. Early picking, cool fermentation and neutral vessels preserve a light line. Riper fruit, barrel fermentation, [[concept.lees-ageing|lees ageing]] and careful oxygen contact can add texture. Botrytised grapes require the handling of highly concentrated, sugar-rich juice.

No technique stands alone. Fruit health and composition determine what pressing, yeast, temperature, vessel and time can achieve. Winemaking cannot create an arbitrary style from identical grapes; it guides the potential delivered by the vineyard.
:::

:::detail{#oogstmoment-als-stijlkeuze parent="wijnmaken-en-rijping" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.wine-australia-semillon source.wine-australia-hunter-valley"}
### Early picking can mean ripe as well as light

Hunter Valley breaks the simple rule that warm regions always make heavy wine. Sémillon there can have adequate flavour maturity at relatively low sugar. Early picking retains acidity and limits potential alcohol; dry fermentation and early bottling then produce a slender young wine. [@source.wine-australia-hunter-valley]

For other dry styles, producers may wait for more weight or combine parcels and picking dates. For noble-sweet wine, not only ripeness but the condition of each affected berry becomes decisive.
:::

:::detail{#vat-lies-en-assemblage parent="wijnmaken-en-rijping" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.plantgrape-semillon source.civb-semillon source.wine-australia-semillon"}
### Barrel, lees and blending form one ageing story

Sémillon can ferment and mature in barrel, but oak need not be loud. Vessel size, age, toast, duration, oxygen and [[concept.batonnage|bâtonnage]] together determine the effect. Lees contact can support texture; [[concept.autolysis|autolysis]] is one of the processes involved.

In blends, Sauvignon Blanc may add tension and fragrance while Sémillon brings volume and length. This is a useful pattern, not a fixed division of labour. Ripeness, proportions and ageing can reverse or overlap the roles. [@source.civb-semillon]
:::

:::section{#synoniemen-klonen-en-verwantschap depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-semillon"}
## Synonyms, clones and relationships — names are not a family tree

The accented spelling is internationally familiar, while the official French variety name appears in Plantgrape as *Semillon B*. Writing it without the accent does not indicate a different variety. Names such as Groendruif and Hunter River Riesling belong to particular historical contexts and do not prove genetic relationship.
:::

:::detail{#officiele-naam-en-historische-bijnamen parent="synoniemen-klonen-en-verwantschap" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.plantgrape-semillon"}
### Historical nicknames are not the same as official synonyms

Old books and different countries contain names such as Groendruif or Hunter River Riesling. Such historical terms can be culturally important, but Plantgrape currently records no officially recognised synonym for Semillon in France or the other EU registers. “Riesling” in an old Australian name never made Sémillon genetically Riesling. [@source.plantgrape-semillon]

France has several approved [[concept.clonal-selection|clones]], while Bordeaux conservatories preserve broader variation. A clone number identifies selected planting material within the variety; it is not a flavour style or quality rank in itself.
:::

:::section{#etiket-en-herkenning depth="foundation" source_refs="source.eu-wine-variety-labelling-2019 source.inao-sauternes-overview"}
## Labels and recognition — sometimes the grape leads, sometimes the place

Semillon often appears on Australian and other varietal labels. In Bordeaux, origin usually leads: Sauternes, Barsac, Pessac-Léognan or Bordeaux tells where and under which rules the wine was made, not automatically what percentage of Sémillon it contains.

A variety name and an appellation name therefore answer different questions. To learn which grapes were actually used, combine the legal origin context with producer information; the front label does not always disclose the complete blend.
:::

:::detail{#herkennen-zonder-raden parent="etiket-en-herkenning" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.plantgrape-semillon"}
### Recognition begins with context, not a guess

In the glass, lemon, wax, herbs, ripe fruit, honey or toast may offer clues, but none belongs exclusively to Sémillon. Label, origin, producer and age provide more reliable context. The same applies in the vineyard: leaf, shoot, bunch and berry are assessed together.

The two images on this page teach observation, not identification. A botanical plate idealises traits; a photograph shows one vine at one moment. Reliable varietal identity also rests on planting-material provenance and, where needed, genetic analysis.
:::

:::section{#hedendaagse-context depth="foundation" source_refs="source.plantgrape-semillon source.wine-australia-semillon"}
## Contemporary developments — an old variety for new decisions

Warmer seasons sharpen the relationship between sugar, acidity, water and fruit health. Early picking may retain freshness but requires enough flavour development; waiting longer can increase ripeness but also drought pressure, acidity loss or unwanted rot. Sémillon's response to fertility and water makes site choice and canopy management important.

At the same time, the variety offers several routes: light unoaked wine, gastronomic dry blends, barrel-aged wine and sweet botrytis wine. That versatility is not a fashionable slogan, but a historically demonstrated range requiring precise choices.
:::

:::detail{#veelzijdigheid-zonder-modewoord parent="hedendaagse-context" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.plantgrape-semillon source.wine-australia-semillon"}
### Planting figures always have a measurement year

French and Australian plantings have changed over time. Such figures are useful for following scale and development, but age quickly and differ by register. This overview therefore avoids a free-floating “world total” as a permanent fact; Plantgrape and Wine Australia retain their own measurement years and definitions. [@source.plantgrape-semillon] [@source.wine-australia-semillon]

For the reader, the more stable knowledge matters most: where Sémillon has a leading role, which styles emerge there and which vineyard and cellar choices explain them.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Sémillon is not a grape with one signature aroma, but a variety that makes time and treatment visible: picked early it can be light and taut, riper and more broadly aged it can be layered, and under noble rot intensely sweet. Place, health, harvest timing and ageing together write the form that appears.
:::
