:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-overview source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
Crémant de Bordeaux is the sparkling side of [[region.bordeaux|Bordeaux]]: white or rosé, with the second fermentation in the same bottle eventually sold. The appellation uses both white and black Bordeaux grapes and connects fresh base wine with time on lees.

This is not Champagne from Bordeaux. *Crémant* has its own protected meaning and Bordeaux its own grape palette and landscape. Understand it as a local expression of the [[concept.traditional-method|traditional method]], not a copy of a more famous region.
:::

## Location and boundaries — bubbles from a broad region

The geographical area covers most of the Gironde department. Crémant can consequently come from limestone slopes, clay, sand or gravel. It is not a small cool corner on the map but a method and origin spread across Bordeaux.

That breadth makes grape choice, picking time and producer important. Base wine usually calls for freshness and modest sugar rather than the full ripeness sought for powerful still red.
:::

:::detail{#geen-enkel-terroirprofiel parent="ligging-en-afbakening" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
### No single terroir profile

A limestone slope can offer tension and water regulation, while sand or clay gives a different growth rhythm. Yet there is no fixed flavour recipe: pressing, blending, second fermentation and lees contact carry great weight in sparkling wine.

“Bordeaux” remains meaningful as origin but identifies vineyard location less precisely than a village appellation. A producer may offer further place information when labelling rules permit.
:::

:::section{#geschiedenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026 source.msha-bordeaux-wine-history"}
## History — underground cellars and a young name

Bordeaux already made sparkling wine in the nineteenth century. Galleries cut into limestone slopes offered cool, even spaces for second fermentation and bottle ageing. Bordeaux mousseux gained protection in 1943; the Crémant de Bordeaux AOC followed on 3 April 1990. [@source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026]

The current appellation is younger than the practice behind it. It brought hand harvest, gentle pressing, bottle fermentation and minimum ageing into one recognisable framework.
:::

:::detail{#cremant-als-beschermde-familie parent="geschiedenis" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
### Crémant is a shared word with local rules

Other French regions also produce Crémant. The shared word denotes a protected family of sparkling wines, but each origin has its own specification, grapes and boundaries. Crémant de Bordeaux therefore remains Bordeaux wine.

Historic cellar galleries suit maturation but are not required for every bottle. Modern temperature control and specialist equipment can coexist with old caves.
:::

:::section{#landschap-klimaat-en-bodem depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
## Landscape, climate and soils — seeking freshness in a mild climate

Bordeaux is mild and maritime, with rain that feeds growth and raises disease pressure. Acidity matters for sparkling wine, so grapes are often picked earlier than fruit destined for powerful still red. Cooler position, soil water and canopy help preserve balance.

Picking too early can give green, thin base wine; too late can cost acidity and finesse. The art is not simply “picking unripe”, but joining ripe flavour and healthy fruit to lively tension.
:::

:::section{#druivenrassen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
## Grape varieties — the broad palette turns sparkling

White may use [[grape.semillon|Sémillon]], [[grape.sauvignon-blanc|Sauvignon Blanc]], [[grape.sauvignon-gris|Sauvignon Gris]], [[grape.muscadelle|Muscadelle]], [[grape.merlot|Merlot]], [[grape.cabernet-franc|Cabernet Franc]] and [[grape.cabernet-sauvignon|Cabernet Sauvignon]], among others. Black grapes yield white juice when skin contact is restricted.

Rosé takes colour from black grapes, often Merlot and Cabernet Franc. The exact blend can express citrus and flowers, red fruit, spice or texture; no single grape must play the leading role.
:::

:::detail{#aanvullende-witte-rassen parent="druivenrassen" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
### Accessory white varieties are limited

[[grape.colombard|Colombard]], [[grape.merlot-blanc|Merlot Blanc]] and [[grape.ugni-blanc|Ugni Blanc]] are permitted accessory white varieties. Planting and blending rules limit their share.

Their presence does not make Crémant less Bordelais; it broadens the toolkit for freshness and yield. Labels do not always state the blend, making producer information the best route to detail.
:::

:::section{#wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
## Viticulture and winemaking — the bubbles form in bottle

Grapes are hand harvested and gently pressed as whole bunches. First fermentation produces a still, acidic base wine. A [[concept.liqueur-de-tirage|liqueur de tirage]] containing yeast and sugar then starts the [[concept.second-fermentation|second fermentation]] inside the sealed bottle; the carbon dioxide cannot escape.

The wine then rests on spent yeast. [[concept.autolysis|Autolysis]] can add bread, brioche and creamy nuances. [[concept.remuage|Riddling]] moves sediment to the neck, [[concept.disgorgement|disgorgement]] removes it, and [[concept.dosage|dosage]] helps determine the final sweetness balance.
:::

:::detail{#persing-en-sapfractie parent="wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
### Gentle pressing protects finesse

Whole bunches may not be crushed or destemmed before pressing and juice is collected in fractions. This limits coarse skin components, oxidation and excess colour. Fast, careful handling matters especially for white wine from black grapes.

The specification caps extraction at 100 litres of must per 150 kilograms of grapes. The technical number supports a stylistic aim: clean juice with concentration for two fermentations.
:::

:::section{#appellationregels depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
## Appellation rules — time and pressure are not optional

Crémant de Bordeaux must be white or rosé and sparkling, with bottle fermentation by the traditional method. Hand picking, whole-bunch pressing, a juice limit and minimum maturation form part of its identity. Pressure after disgorgement must be at least 3.5 bar.

The rules guarantee a controlled route, not a set amount of brioche or bubble finesse. Grape quality, base wine, time on lees and bottling care still distinguish producers.
:::

:::detail{#actuele-termijnen parent="appellationregels" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
### Current timings

Tirage may not occur before 1 December after harvest and only once three months have elapsed. Bottles remain continuously on lees for at least nine months. Release follows no earlier than twelve months after tirage and one month after disgorgement. [@source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026]

Base yield is 72 hectolitres per hectare and the ceiling 78; density is in principle 4,000 vines per hectare and may fall to 3,300 under conditions. These are current legal values, not a lasting quality rank.
:::

:::section{#classificatie-en-producenten depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-overview"}
## Classification and producers — method before rank

There is no official château classification for Crémant de Bordeaux. Producers range from sparkling-wine specialists to châteaux and cooperatives that make Crémant alongside still wine.

When comparing, note disgorgement date if stated, grapes, maturation time and sweetness category. A prestigious château name does not guarantee sparkling expertise; a lesser-known specialist may work with exceptional precision.
:::

:::section{#wijnstijl-en-flesontwikkeling depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-overview source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
## Wine style and bottle development — fruit, mousse and yeast

White Crémant can combine apple, citrus and white flowers with occasional brioche or nut. Rosé tends toward strawberry, raspberry and light spice. Mousse should be lively, while acidity and dosage together determine whether the wine feels taut or rounder.

Many bottles are made to drink young and fresh. Longer lees ageing or greater concentration can carry further development, but appellation alone supplies no cellar duration.
:::

:::detail{#serveren-en-combineren parent="wijnstijl-en-flesontwikkeling" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-overview"}
### From aperitif to table

Start around 7–10 °C in a tulip-shaped glass, which holds bubble and aroma together better than a broad coupe. White suits oysters, fried snacks or soft cheese; rosé can also accompany charcuterie and red fruit.

Brut describes dosage, not whether wine is “better”. Extra brut or brut nature may taste drier, but ripe fruit, acidity and time on lees shape the impression just as strongly.
:::

:::section{#etiket-en-aankoop depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
## Labels and buying — read colour and sweetness

Look for “Crémant de Bordeaux”, colour, producer and sweetness term. *Brut*, *extra brut* and *brut nature* refer to regulated sugar bands after dosage; they are not a quality ladder. Vintage or disgorgement date can add context but need not appear on every bottle.

Store sparkling wine cool and dark. After opening, a sound pressure stopper helps briefly, although the mousse is liveliest on day one.
:::

:::section{#hedendaagse-ontwikkelingen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-cremant-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
## Contemporary developments — another face of Bordeaux

Crémant gives Bordeaux a fresh, accessible style beyond classic red wine. Warming meanwhile makes acidity more urgent. Earlier picking helps but still requires flavour-ripe fruit; site and canopy grow more important.

Producers can distinguish themselves through longer lees ageing, drier dosage or more transparent origin information. The challenge is making difference perceptible without losing the festive ease of good sparkling wine.
:::

:::key-idea{#kernidee depth="foundation"}
Crémant de Bordeaux is white or rosé sparkling Bordeaux, hand harvested and second-fermented in bottle. Method supplies the structure; grapes, base wine and time supply the character.
:::
