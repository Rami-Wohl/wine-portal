:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026 source.civb-pauillac-overview"}
Pauillac is small on the map and enormous in the wine world. Low gravel rises, famous châteaux and a port that connected the vineyards to distant markets line the broad Gironde. Cabernet Sauvignon often leads red wines whose power comes not simply from volume but from tension: dark fruit, freshness, firm tannin and the capacity to unfold slowly.

Yet Pauillac is neither a fixed flavour recipe nor a synonym for expensive bottles. It is a protected origin within the [[region.medoc|Médoc]], with its own land, history and rules; it is also a community of large estates, smaller growers and a cooperative. Keeping those layers separate reveals far more than three famous labels. [@source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026] [@source.civb-pauillac-overview]

## Location and boundaries — a narrow strip with a great name

Pauillac lies on the left bank of the Gironde estuary, roughly halfway up the Médoc and north of [[region.bordeaux|Bordeaux]]. The commune sits between [[appellation.saint-julien|Saint-Julien]] to the south and [[appellation.saint-estephe|Saint-Estèphe]] to the north. From the quay, the water is at once a climatic buffer, an old trade route and the vineyards’ eastern horizon.

The name on a bottle means more than “made near the village”. [[concept.aop|The protected designation of origin]] is reserved exclusively for still red wine. White or rosé from a local estate may belong to the same business world, but cannot be sold as Pauillac PDO. [@source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026]
:::

:::detail{#wettelijke-grens parent="plaats-en-naam" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026 source.legifrance-pauillac-order-2026"}
### The wine map does not exactly follow the municipal map

The current specification includes Pauillac and historically designated parcels in Saint-Estèphe, Saint-Julien-Beychevelle and Saint-Sauveur. Within that zone, only officially delimited vineyard land qualifies. An address in the commune is not enough; both parcel and wine must comply. [@source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026]

The specification re-approved in April 2026 has applied since 1 May. [@source.legifrance-pauillac-order-2026] Appellations are living legal agreements. For exact boundaries, the applicable dossier must lead—not a tourist sketch map or an old surface figure.
:::

## History — the port came before global fame

Pauillac tells its history from the water. The site served as a trading place early on, but recognisable centres of viticulture developed mainly from the mid-thirteenth century according to the appellation dossier. The port gave barrels a route to Bordeaux and beyond; shipping brought money, contacts and expectations back to the vineyard.

In the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries the quay became the face of a prosperous wine town. Warehouses, landing places and château ownership belonged to one system. A château was not merely a handsome building, but an agricultural business, capital asset and trading name. The quays show how closely town and estuary commerce were intertwined. [@source.pauillac-quays-2017]
:::

:::detail{#landgoederen-en-handel parent="haven-en-wijngaard" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026 source.medoc-wine-council-history"}
### From wet land to great estates

From the seventeenth century, former lordships increasingly passed to Bordeaux officials, merchants and investors. Dutch knowledge and enterprise contributed to drainage in parts of the Médoc. This was no single heroic act: ditches, dykes and watercourses required continuing local labour.

Planting, construction and cellar techniques accelerated in the eighteenth century. Cabernet Sauvignon and other small-berried varieties gained importance while the port became a gateway for Médoc wine. Négociants and courtiers later invested directly in estates. Pauillac’s [[concept.terroir|terroir]] is therefore nature shaped by centuries of water management, ownership, labour and trade. [@source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026] [@source.medoc-wine-council-history]
:::

:::detail{#van-reputatie-naar-aop parent="haven-en-wijngaard" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026 source.gcc1855-classification-overview"}
### Reputation first, legal boundaries later

When the trade assembled a hierarchy for the 1855 Paris exposition, Pauillac’s leading estates were already established market names. [[classification.bordeaux-1855|The 1855 classification]] recorded that reputation; it did not create the vineyard. [@source.gcc1855-classification-overview]

The origin acquired a legal outline later. A Lesparre court ruling in 1926 recognised that certain parcels beyond the commune historically belonged to Pauillac growths. The AOC was recognised on 14 November 1936 and incorporated that history of use. [@source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026] Classification ranks a historical estate group; appellation protects origin and production conditions.
:::

:::section{#grindkoppen-aan-het-water depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026"}
## Landscape, climate and soils — an undulating landscape of gravel

Calling Pauillac flat means looking too quickly. The vines roll across low *croupes*: rounded gravel rises running parallel to the Gironde and divided by stream valleys. Their crests, flanks and proximity to the estuary create small differences in drainage, exposure and temperature.

The temperate oceanic climate is moderated by the Atlantic and Gironde, but remains changeable. Rain during flowering or harvest, a cool summer or a warm, dry late season alters ripening. [[concept.vintage|Vintage]] is no decorative number here: it records the conditions in which vine and grower sought balance. [@source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026]
:::

:::detail{#bodem-en-afwatering parent="grindkoppen-aan-het-water" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026 source.brgm-bordeaux-geology-2022"}
### Gravel works through water, warmth and roots

The Quaternary terraces stand roughly 3 to 30 metres above sea level and average about ten metres thick. Older marls and limestones lie beneath. Sandy gravel dominates the croupes; hollows may contain sand over clay, while younger alluvial *palus* and marsh soils occur beside the estuary. [@source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026]

Gravel matters because permeable profiles shed water and warm readily, not because pebbles put “pencil shavings” directly into wine. Clay, sand, subsoil, roots and management act together. Official delimitation favours well-drained gravel and sandy-gravel soils and excludes, among other land, poorly drained parcels. [@source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026] Geology creates possibilities; vines and people determine how they are used. [@source.brgm-bordeaux-geology-2022]
:::

:::detail{#drie-landschappen parent="grindkoppen-aan-het-water" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026"}
### Three groups, cut through by water

The dossier distinguishes three systems: the largest in the south around Saint-Lambert and Bages, a smaller central group around Artigues, and the northern group around Pouyalet up to the Lafite channel and marsh. The town and port sit lower on the bank. [@source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026]

This helps read the landscape but is no internal quality ranking. Estates may own parcels on several croupes. A reliable parcel map requires verified Atlas geometry; château stories or soil colours must never serve as boundary data.
:::

## Grape varieties — Cabernet Sauvignon leads, but never plays alone

[[grape.cabernet-sauvignon|Cabernet Sauvignon]] ripens relatively late and can provide colour, [[concept.acidity|fresh acidity]], blackcurrant and a firm spine of [[concept.tannin|tannin]]. It is often the largest component on free-draining gravel rises, but “largest” means neither “everything” nor the same proportion every year.

[[grape.merlot|Merlot]] generally ripens earlier and can add fruit, breadth and suppleness. [[grape.cabernet-franc|Cabernet Franc]] and [[grape.petit-verdot|Petit Verdot]] may contribute perfume, tension, colour or spice. [[grape.malbec|Malbec]]—called *cot* in the dossier—and [[grape.carmenere|Carménère]] are also permitted. These six are the legal toolkit; each château uses what is planted and ripens. [@source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026]
:::

:::detail{#druif-plaats-en-jaar parent="cabernet-en-zijn-gezelschap" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026 source.civb-pauillac-overview"}
### The blend begins outdoors

The [[concept.assemblage|blend]]—combining separate lots into the finished wine—effectively begins with deciding which variety grows on which parcel. A warm gravel rise may help late-ripening Cabernet; a cooler or more clay-rich section responds differently to drought. Vine age, yield, picking date and selection shift the result further.

Variety descriptions therefore indicate functions that *may* emerge; they are not buttons with which a cellar master guarantees cassis, roundness or spice.
:::

:::section{#van-rank-tot-vat depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026 source.civb-pauillac-overview"}
## Viticulture and winemaking — style emerges from a sequence of decisions

Planting density, pruning, canopy, soil management, disease control and harvest date shape which grapes reach the cellar. Lots are selected, fermented separately, and colour and tannin drawn from the skins.

After fermentation, the maker tastes barrels and tanks to assemble the [[concept.grand-vin|grand vin]]. Blending is not a way to hide faults, but to balance parcels, varieties and vessels. The result may be stern and deep or approachable early; appellation and tradition set the field without writing the composition. [@source.civb-pauillac-overview]
:::

:::detail{#extractie-en-elevage parent="van-rank-tot-vat" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026"}
### Building structure without piling on hardness

During [[concept.maceration|skin contact]], temperature, duration and movement determine how much colour, aroma and tannin is drawn out. [[concept.extraction|Extraction]] is no contest for “more”: unripe seeds or excessive force can make wine lean and coarse.

Next comes [[concept.elevage|maturation]], development in barrel, tank or another vessel before bottling. Many ambitious Pauillacs mature partly in oak, but new wood, vessel size, duration and oxygen exposure differ. Oak may add texture and aroma without automatically drowning out fruit and place.
:::

:::section{#regels-als-ondergrens depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-aop-overview source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026"}
## Appellation rules — a floor, not a house style

The specification determines where grapes may grow, which varieties are allowed and which vineyard and cellar conditions apply. That makes Pauillac a controlled origin, not a free marketing term. It does not promise every bottle will be great, ageworthy or taste of blackcurrant.

The PDO says wine was made within a shared geographical and technical framework; producer, parcel and year explain choices within it. [[concept.chateau|“Château” as a wine term]] points to an estate or brand, not a higher appellation category.
:::

:::detail{#wat-de-regels-niet-bepalen parent="regels-als-ondergrens" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026 source.legifrance-pauillac-order-2026"}
### A few figures, treated with due modesty

Current rules require at least 7,000 vines per hectare, set the base yield at 57 hectolitres per hectare and require maturation until at least 1 June after harvest. Consumer release may begin on 1 September. [@source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026] This version has applied since May 2026. [@source.legifrance-pauillac-order-2026]

None of those figures reveals the Cabernet share, new oak or grand-vin selection. Rules are essential to authenticity but inadequate as a tasting note or quality ranking.
:::

:::section{#achttien-namen-een-geschiedenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.gcc1855-classification-overview"}
## Classification and producers — eighteen names cast a long shadow

No other communal appellation is so closely entwined with 1855. The current list contains eighteen Pauillac growths, including three premiers crus. [@source.gcc1855-classification-overview] That helps explain global visibility, capital and the collector market.

A [[concept.cru|cru]] here is a classified estate within a historical system. Its rank does not automatically belong to every bottle carrying a family name or “Pauillac”. Nor does 1855 compare all current producers afresh: continuity is precisely its hallmark.
:::

:::detail{#drie-premiers parent="achttien-namen-een-geschiedenis" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.gcc1855-classification-overview"}
### Three first growths as the first bearings

The most famous anchors are [[producer.chateau-lafite-rothschild|Château Lafite Rothschild]], [[producer.chateau-latour|Château Latour]] and [[producer.chateau-mouton-rothschild|Château Mouton Rothschild]]. All now appear as premiers crus on the official list. Their names made Pauillac recognisable, but they do not express one uniform style.

They are followed by second growths [[producer.chateau-pichon-baron|Château Pichon Baron]] and [[producer.chateau-pichon-longueville-comtesse-de-lalande|Château Pichon Longueville Comtesse de Lalande]]. Their near-neighbouring buildings offer a fine lesson: proximity and equal rank do not erase an estate’s signature. [@source.gcc1855-classification-overview]
:::

:::detail{#alle-achttien parent="achttien-namen-een-geschiedenis" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.gcc1855-classification-overview"}
### The complete 1855 Pauillac group

- **Premiers crus:** Château Lafite Rothschild, Château Latour and Château Mouton Rothschild.
- **Deuxièmes crus:** Château Pichon Baron and Château Pichon Longueville Comtesse de Lalande.
- **Quatrième cru:** [[producer.chateau-duhart-milon|Château Duhart-Milon]].
- **Cinquièmes crus:** [[producer.chateau-pontet-canet|Château Pontet-Canet]], [[producer.chateau-batailley|Château Batailley]], [[producer.chateau-haut-batailley|Château Haut-Batailley]], [[producer.chateau-grand-puy-lacoste|Château Grand-Puy-Lacoste]], [[producer.chateau-grand-puy-ducasse|Château Grand-Puy Ducasse]], [[producer.chateau-lynch-bages|Château Lynch-Bages]], [[producer.chateau-lynch-moussas|Château Lynch-Moussas]], [[producer.chateau-darmailhac|Château d’Armailhac]], [[producer.chateau-haut-bages-liberal|Château Haut-Bages Libéral]], [[producer.chateau-pedesclaux|Château Pédesclaux]], [[producer.chateau-clerc-milon|Château Clerc Milon]] and [[producer.chateau-croizet-bages|Château Croizet-Bages]].

This is navigation, not buying advice. Style, reputation, holdings and price vary within one rank; judge a bottle by producer, cuvée and year. [@source.gcc1855-classification-overview]
:::

:::detail{#meer-dan-1855 parent="achttien-namen-een-geschiedenis" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.gcc1855-classification-overview source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026"}
### Pauillac does not end with the list

Not every producer is a grand cru classé. The dossier also describes smaller holdings and a cooperative tradition bringing small growers together since 1933. [@source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026] This keeps “Pauillac” from being understood solely as a luxury brand.

Classification shapes attention, price and market access, but does not spare the drinker from tasting or impose an automatic ceiling on unclassified wine. Rank cannot rescue a weak year or badly stored bottle either.
:::

:::section{#pauillac-in-het-glas depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026 source.civb-pauillac-overview"}
## Wine style and bottle development — power with edges and rhythm

Young Pauillac can be dark and compact: blackcurrant, blackberry or plum, alongside cedar, graphite-like associations, herbs and sometimes mint or violets. Fresh acidity and noticeable tannin provide shape—as though fruit were held in a firm frame.

The best balance is not heavy. Cabernet gives length, Merlot may fill the middle and oak can round contours. Some wines are open young; others close down. These are recognition points, not a guaranteed aroma list. [@source.civb-pauillac-overview]
:::

:::detail{#tijd-in-de-fles parent="pauillac-in-het-glas" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026 source.civb-pauillac-overview"}
### What bottle age can change

Fresh fruit may shift towards dried berries, tobacco, cedar, leather, earth and savoury notes. Tannins can feel less angular. Such development depends on concentration and structure, but also winemaking, closure and stable storage.

“Pauillac can age” does not mean every bottle should wait decades. An accessible cuvée may delight young; a concentrated grand vin may be decanted or require patience. Producer, vintage, bottle size and storage are more useful than one universal window.
:::

:::detail{#stijl-is-geen-uniform parent="pauillac-in-het-glas" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.civb-pauillac-overview source.brgm-bordeaux-geology-2022"}
### The classic description has limits

“Cabernet, cassis, cedar and power” is an entry point but soon a caricature. More Merlot, different croupes, gentler extraction or less new oak shift the balance. Climate and harvest date alter ripeness; bottle age changes the language.

Soil is no aroma machine either. Gravel chiefly affects water and heat; “graphite” is a tasting association, not dissolved rock. [@source.brgm-bordeaux-geology-2022] A profile shapes expectations, but the actual wine remains the test.
:::

:::section{#etiket-lezen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.dgccrf-wine-labels-2025 source.gcc1855-classification-overview"}
## Labels and buying — read what the bottle actually promises first

Look first for Pauillac, then producer, vintage and bottler. Check whether a classification term truly belongs to the château. “Grand vin”, “vieilles vignes” or an imposing building cannot replace an official rank. Mandatory details and protected origin give bearings; design chiefly tells how the wine presents itself. [@source.dgccrf-wine-labels-2025]

Ask whether this is the grand vin, another [[concept.cuvee|cuvée]] or an older bottle with known storage. Current price should not become a permanent fact here; [[concept.en-primeur|en primeur]] also adds market and delivery risk to unbottled wine.
:::

:::detail{#tweede-wijn-en-rang parent="etiket-lezen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.gcc1855-classification-overview"}
### A second wine is not a second growth

A [[concept.second-wine|second wine]] is a separate cuvée, often from different parcels, younger vines or lots excluded from the grand vin. It may still carry Pauillac when it meets every rule. “Second” concerns estate selection, not the five ranks of 1855.

Classification does not automatically leap onto every bottle in the same ownership group. Read the exact wine name. A second wine may be accessible sooner or cost less, but that is a producer choice rather than a legal promise.
:::

:::section{#een-appellation-in-beweging depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inrae-bordeaux-climate-adaptation source.inao-pauillac-specification-2026"}
## Contemporary developments — no museum behind a château gate

Old façades and classification can make Pauillac appear unchanging. In fact vines are replaced, parcels replanted, cellars renewed, and ideas about soil life, crop protection and selection revised. The rules themselves change periodically.

Large, well-capitalised estates can invest differently from small businesses or cooperative members. A complete account therefore needs labour, technical access and continuity alongside auction bottles.
:::

:::detail{#klimaat-en-keuzes parent="een-appellation-in-beweging" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inrae-bordeaux-climate-adaptation"}
### Warmth makes ripeness easier—and balance harder

Warmer seasons can help Cabernet ripen reliably, but heat, drought and faster sugar accumulation create tension. Growers respond through pruning date, canopy, ground cover, [[concept.rootstock|rootstock]], harvest timing and varietal distribution. The question is not maximum ripeness, but how aroma, acidity, tannin and alcohol keep moving together. [@source.inrae-bordeaux-climate-adaptation]

Adaptation is local and long-term. A choice helping in a dry summer may harm in a wet one. Gravel drains readily—a historic strength—yet sharpens water-availability questions in prolonged drought. Pauillac’s future will again emerge from place, rules and human decisions.
:::
