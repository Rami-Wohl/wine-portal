:::summary{#overzicht depth="foundation" source_refs="source.junta-sherry-specification source.sherry-types"}
**Sherry is the protected wine name Jerez-Xérès-Sherry from south-western Spain.** It encompasses dry and sweet wines. Their major differences arise through ageing beneath a yeast layer, flor, or ageing with oxygen without that layer. Fortification with wine alcohol is traditionally important; current rules also allow dry expressions without added alcohol. [@source.junta-sherry-specification; B.1 and C.2] [@source.sherry-types]
:::

:::section{#ligging-en-afbakening depth="foundation" source_refs="source.junta-sherry-specification"}
## Location and boundaries

The wine region lies around Jerez de la Frontera, near Andalusia's Atlantic coast. The protected zone extends beyond the familiar triangle of Jerez, El Puerto de Santa María and Sanlúcar de Barrameda. Trebujena, Chipiona and Lebrija are among the other municipalities within the demarcated area. [@source.junta-sherry-specification; D.1]

**Manzanilla-Sanlúcar de Barrameda has its own protected designation of origin.** It shares the region and biological ageing with Sherry, but has its own appellation conditions. “Sherry” in a broad trade explanation and Jerez-Xérès-Sherry as a legal name therefore require careful boundaries. [@source.junta-sherry-specification; C.3]
:::

:::section{#geschiedenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.sherry-history"}
## History

Trade with northern Europe helped shape today's Sherry styles. Growing demand for stronger, more mature wines conflicted in the eighteenth century with rules favouring young wine sales. As those restrictions disappeared, ageing and stockholding gained more room. Soleras and their associated cellar architecture developed further from the late eighteenth century. [@source.sherry-history]
:::

:::section{#landschap-klimaat-en-bodem depth="foundation" source_refs="source.sherry-viticulture"}
## Landscape, climate and soils

Low, rolling hills, hot dry summers and Atlantic influence characterise the region. The westerly poniente brings cooler, moist air; the levante is warmer and drier. Pale, limestone-rich albariza soil can retain winter rainfall. This water helps sustain vines through the dry growing period. Distance from the coast and local soils introduce differences between vineyards. [@source.sherry-viticulture]
:::

:::section{#druivenrassen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.sherry-viticulture source.junta-sherry-specification"}
## Grape varieties

**Palomino** is the main starting point for dry Sherry. Its base wine acquires much of its final character during cellar ageing. **Pedro Ximénez** and **Moscatel** play important roles in sweet wines. Grapes for Pedro Ximénez are dried, concentrating their sugar. The permitted variety list extends beyond these familiar three; grape name and wine type are separate pieces of information. [@source.sherry-viticulture] [@source.junta-sherry-specification; B.1 and F]
:::

:::section{#wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken depth="foundation" source_refs="source.sherry-ageing source.sherry-types source.junta-sherry-specification"}
## Viticulture and winemaking

For dry types, grape juice first ferments into dry base wine. The ageing route is then chosen. **Flor** is a living yeast layer on the wine's surface. During biological ageing, it changes the wine and limits direct oxygen contact. During oxidative ageing, the wine develops without this protective layer. [@source.sherry-ageing]

A **solera** is a system in which some older wine is repeatedly drawn off and replenished from younger stocks. This enables a house to maintain its style across successive bottlings. The blending system can serve both biological and oxidative ageing. Ageing by a separate harvest year, añada, also exists. [@source.sherry-ageing] [@source.junta-sherry-specification; C.3]

Sweet PX and Moscatel follow a different starting route: sugar-rich must from overripe or dried grapes is fortified with wine alcohol, retaining much of its sugar. Some other sweet types are made by blending dry wine with sweet components. [@source.sherry-types]

## Appellation rules

The appellation regulates origin, permitted varieties, wine types and ageing, among other matters. Its product specification distinguishes wine from liqueur wine: dry types can be made without fortification when they meet their conditions. For fortified expressions, added alcohol helps achieve the required strength and intended ageing route. “Sherry” therefore does not automatically mean “alcohol added”. [@source.junta-sherry-specification; B.1, C.2 and G] [@source.eu-sherry-amendment-2025]
:::

:::detail{#rijping-en-oogstjaar parent="appellationregels" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.junta-sherry-specification"}
### Average age and a separate harvest

Generoso types require at least two years' average ageing. In a solera, this concerns the system's stocks and turnover, rather than one shared birthday for all the wine. An añada is instead aged separately by harvest. VOS and VORS refer to controlled, particularly long ageing: more than twenty and more than thirty years respectively. [@source.junta-sherry-specification; B.1 and C.3]
:::

:::section{#classificatie-en-producenten depth="foundation" source_refs="source.sherry-labels source.sherry-types"}
## Classification and producers

The wine type's name tells you much about the bottle. The bodega name identifies the business; a brand may additionally identify a particular wine within its range. The Consejo Regulador's numbered guarantee seal on the label forms part of origin control. Type, bodega and brand are therefore three distinct points of reference. [@source.sherry-labels] [@source.sherry-types]
:::

:::section{#wijnstijl-en-flesontwikkeling depth="foundation" source_refs="source.sherry-ageing source.sherry-types source.junta-sherry-specification"}
## Wine style and bottle development

The dry types demonstrate different ageing routes:

- **Fino:** biological ageing beneath flor, with a dry character and impressions that can include yeast or almond.
- **Amontillado:** biological ageing followed by oxidative ageing.
- **Oloroso:** oxidative ageing, with a fuller, nutty profile.
- **Palo Cortado:** predominantly oxidative ageing after the initial flor has disappeared.

PX and Moscatel can be markedly sweet. Cream and related blended types provide further sweetness profiles. Dark colour therefore tells you more when read with the type's name: a dark Oloroso can be dry, while a dark PX is very sweet. [@source.sherry-types] [@source.junta-sherry-specification; B.1–3]

The characteristic biological or oxidative ageing takes place in the bodega. A sealed bottle of Fino does not continue the life of an active flor cask; the ageing story on the label primarily describes what happened before bottling. [@source.sherry-ageing]
:::

:::section{#etiket-en-aankoop depth="foundation" source_refs="source.sherry-labels source.junta-sherry-specification"}
## Labels and buying

Start with the protected name and wine type. **Oloroso** and **Cream**, for example, convey different sweetness information even when both are dark. Then read the producer, alcohol content and any age or harvest indication. “Solera” describes the ageing system; an old year within a brand name need not be the bottle's harvest year. [@source.sherry-labels] [@source.junta-sherry-specification; C.3]
:::

:::section{#hedendaagse-ontwikkelingen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.junta-sherry-document-history-2025 source.eu-sherry-amendment-2025 source.junta-sherry-specification"}
## Contemporary developments

The Union amendment approved in 2025 enables the protected name to encompass dry, unfortified wine too. The current product specification is therefore the starting point for explaining Sherry. The classic fortified production route continues alongside this possibility; the same origin name can encompass different routes. [@source.eu-sherry-amendment-2025] [@source.junta-sherry-document-history-2025] [@source.junta-sherry-specification; B.1 and C.2]
:::
