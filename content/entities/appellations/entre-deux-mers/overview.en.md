:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-entre-deux-mers-specification-2025"}
Entre-deux-Mers is a Bordeaux appellation for dry white and, since the recent expansion of its specification, red wine. White remains the familiar style: lively, citrusy and herbal, usually based on Sauvignon Blanc with Sémillon, Muscadelle or Sauvignon Gris. Red brings the area's traditional Bordeaux varieties under the same geographical name.

The appellation is smaller and more precise than the broad [[region.entre-deux-mers|Entre-deux-Mers region]] between Garonne and Dordogne. The two names do not automatically coincide.
:::

:::section{#ligging-en-afbakening depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-entre-deux-mers-specification-2025 source.gironde-landscape-atlas-entre-deux-mers"}
## Location and boundaries — an appellation within the between-land

The geographical zone lies between the Dordogne's left bank and the Garonne's right bank. It includes many communes, but only officially delimited parcels qualify. The landscape rolls over limestone plateaux and stream valleys; woods, fields and buildings interrupt the vines.

A wine geographically produced in Entre-deux-Mers may legally carry another Bordeaux appellation. Conversely, the label means that wine, grapes and production met the specification—not that the whole region has one uniform style.
:::

:::section{#geschiedenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-entre-deux-mers-specification-2025"}
## History — from medieval trade to a two-colour PDO

Monasteries, lordships and river trade helped develop viticulture in the Middle Ages. Wine travelled by river to Bordeaux and onward to markets including England. After major nineteenth-century expansion, fungal diseases and phylloxera brought destruction and replanting.

The name became an AOC in 1937. From 1953 it was reserved for dry white wine, although older legal history had linked both white and red to the regional name. The current specification permits both colours, giving regional red production a more specific origin alongside broader Bordeaux designations.
:::

:::detail{#rood-is-geen-voetnoot parent="geschiedenis" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-entre-deux-mers-specification-2025"}
### Red recognition changes the basic definition

Older books describing Entre-deux-Mers solely as a white appellation were correct in their time but are now incomplete. The new red category is neither rosé nor a loose geographical indication: it has its own varieties, yields and production standards within the same PDO specification.

Vintage consequently matters. A red wine from this country before introduction could not retrospectively become Entre-deux-Mers PDO.

## Landscape, climate and soils — slopes above stream valleys

Limestone forms an important base, often covered or mixed with clay, loam, sand and local gravel deposits. Streams cut plateaux into slopes of different aspects. Free-draining heights, cooler valleys and more clay-rich sites give growers different options for white and red varieties.

The climate is Atlantic: mild, with ample rain but risks from spring frost, mildew and wet harvests. Slopes and air circulation can dry grapes more quickly; in hot summers root depth and water reserves help determine how evenly they ripen.
:::

:::section{#druivenrassen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-entre-deux-mers-specification-2025"}
## Grape varieties — blending is obligatorily plural

For white, [[grape.sauvignon-blanc|Sauvignon Blanc]], Sauvignon Gris, [[grape.semillon|Sémillon]] and [[grape.muscadelle|Muscadelle]] are principal varieties; Colombard and Ugni Blanc are supplementary. For red, [[grape.merlot|Merlot]], [[grape.cabernet-sauvignon|Cabernet Sauvignon]], [[grape.cabernet-franc|Cabernet Franc]], Malbec and Petit Verdot are principal, with Carménère supplementary.

Both colours must contain at least two principal varieties in the blend. No principal variety may exceed eighty per cent; supplementary varieties are collectively limited. The appellation therefore regulates a blend rather than promoting varietal identity.
:::

:::detail{#rollen-in-de-blend parent="druivenrassen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.civb-bordeaux-grape-varieties source.inao-entre-deux-mers-specification-2025"}
### Each variety solves a different part of the puzzle

Sauvignon may offer citrus, green herbs and tension; Sémillon often contributes rounder fruit and texture, Muscadelle floral lift. In red, Merlot generally supplies supple fruit and volume, Cabernet Sauvignon structure and Cabernet Franc perfume and line. Malbec and Petit Verdot can add colour, spice and tannin.

These are roles, not fixed flavours. Soil, ripeness, yield and cellar work decide whether a blend feels taut, juicy, powerful or restrained.
:::

:::section{#wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-entre-deux-mers-specification-2025"}
## Viticulture and winemaking — fresh white, new red

White grapes are commonly harvested cool, pressed quickly and protected from excessive oxygen. Tank fermentation preserves clear fruit; barrel and fine-lees ageing can add texture. The wine must remain dry, placing freshness and aromatic definition at the centre.

Red grapes ferment with their skins. Producers adapt extraction to ripeness and desired structure; malolactic conversion, blending and possible barrel ageing follow. The new category need not imitate one model, but should remain recognisably dry, balanced and connected to origin.
:::

:::detail{#actuele-regels parent="wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-entre-deux-mers-specification-2025"}
### The figures behind two colours

Base yield is 65 hectolitres per hectare for white and 55 for red. Dry white generally contains no more than 4 grams of fermentable sugar per litre. The specification also sets planting density, pruning, ripeness, analytical limits and dates for maturation and release.

Exact values may change in later amendments. They are legal thresholds, not a flavour recipe or quality rank.
:::

:::section{#appellationregels depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-entre-deux-mers-specification-2025"}
## Appellation rules — two colours, one protected origin

The specification protects still dry white and still red wine. Both colours use delimited parcels, permitted grapes, a multi-variety blend and separate vineyard and wine standards. The rules create a verifiable baseline; they do not prescribe producer style, oak use or a quality rank.
:::

:::section{#classificatie-en-producenten depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-entre-deux-mers-specification-2025"}
## Classification and producers — no château hierarchy

Entre-deux-Mers has no official producer classification. Large family properties, small estates and cooperatives work under the same appellation rules. A château image gives a human scale, but is neither a model for every business nor a sign of rank.

Assess producers through current work, cuvée, vineyard information and consistency. Terms such as *grand vin* or an impressive building do not legally elevate a wine within the PDO.
:::

:::section{#wijnstijl-en-glas depth="foundation" source_refs="source.civb-bordeaux-wine-styles source.inao-entre-deux-mers-specification-2025"}
## Wine style and bottle development — bright white and fruit-led red

White may show grapefruit, lemon, green apple, flowers and fresh herbs in a light to medium body. Sémillon and lees ageing can broaden texture. Drink simple examples young and cool, around 8–11°C, with shellfish, goat's cheese, salads or green herbs.

Red is a younger and less settled category. Expect plum, red and black fruit, herbs and soft to evident tannin depending on Cabernet share and maturation. Serve lightly cool around 15–17°C. Producer and vintage say more than one universal style promise.
:::

:::section{#etiket-en-aankoop depth="foundation" source_refs="source.dgccrf-wine-labels-2025 source.inao-entre-deux-mers-specification-2025"}
## Labels and buying — colour and vintage tell a new story

Check “Entre-deux-Mers” as appellation name, then colour, producer, cuvée, vintage and bottler. For red, the vintage locates the wine in the recent history of the rules. “Entre-deux-Mers Haut-Benauge” is a complementary geographical designation for white under its own conditions, not a general quality rank.

Price and château imagery are not legal levels. Vintage and intended drinking style guide white purchases; for red, producer information and the actual blend are especially useful while the category develops its market identity.
:::

:::section{#hedendaagse-ontwikkelingen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inrae-bordeaux-climate-adaptation source.inao-entre-deux-mers-specification-2025"}
## Contemporary developments — more origin, more climate pressure

Recognition of red narrows the gap between what grows in the region and what can be sold under its name. It may strengthen regional identity, but success requires clear communication and convincing wine—a new rule does not create reputation by itself.

Heat, drought and intense rain also demand changed soil and canopy management. White depends on retaining acidity; red must unite ripe tannin with moderate alcohol. Diverse slopes and soils make tailored responses both possible and necessary.
:::

:::key-idea{#kernidee depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-entre-deux-mers-specification-2025"}
Entre-deux-Mers PDO is no longer dry white alone: red now belongs to its basic definition. The colours are united not by one flavour, but by a delimited origin and compulsory blending of several principal varieties.
:::
