:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-listrac-medoc-overview source.inao-listrac-medoc-specification-2025"}
Listrac-Médoc occupies the higher inland ridge of the central [[region.medoc|Médoc]]. It produces red wine only, generally from Merlot and Cabernet Sauvignon. Gravel terraces alternate with clay and limestone; distance from the Gironde means less direct estuary moderation than in Pauillac or Saint-Julien.

Classic Listrac has ripe dark fruit, a fresh line and palpable tannin. Its structure gives the nickname “roof of the Médoc” a meaning beyond elevation: wines may be compact when young and unfold with time.
:::

:::section{#ligging-en-afbakening depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-listrac-medoc-overview source.inao-listrac-medoc-specification-2025"}
## Location and boundaries — on the Médoc ridge

Listrac-Médoc lies west of the estuary road, between Moulis-en-Médoc and Saint-Julien. The production zone is tied to the commune of Listrac-Médoc and officially approved parcels within it. The large commune also contains forest, homes and other farmland; not every hectare is appellation vineyard.

With elevations reaching roughly forty metres, this is one of the higher parts of the apparently flat peninsula. That sounds modest but affects runoff, exposure and cool air. Legal boundary and landscape ridge do not coincide everywhere in a simple way.
:::

:::section{#geschiedenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-listrac-medoc-overview source.msha-bordeaux-wine-history"}
## History — trade, estates and a late independent AOC

As elsewhere in the Médoc, viticulture grew as drainage, estate formation and Bordeaux trade reinforced each other. Nineteenth-century vine diseases and phylloxera caused a rupture; replanting on American rootstocks remade the vineyard. No Listrac château entered the 1855 classification, but local properties later featured strongly among the Crus Bourgeois.

The Listrac-Médoc AOC was recognised in 1957. Its cooperative tradition matters too: it gave smaller growers access to vinification, maturation and sales under a recognisable origin. The appellation is not only a landscape of château ownership, but also of collective production.
:::

:::detail{#rang-en-markt parent="geschiedenis" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-listrac-medoc-overview"}
### Rank and market

The 1932 Crus Bourgeois classification named 28 Listrac properties, eight as Cru Bourgeois Supérieur. That precise historical figure shows that absence from 1855 did not mean commercial invisibility. Classifications later changed; never treat an old rank as an automatic statement of current status.
:::

## Landscape, climate and soils — gravel above clay and limestone

Garonne gravel terraces sit beside Pyrenean deposits, clay-limestone soils and sandier zones. Coarser, warmer, freer-draining ridges often suit Cabernet Sauvignon; cooler, moisture-retentive clay gives Merlot a strong position. Subsoil and slope determine how winter rain drains and how much water remains available in summer.

The climate is maritime, with mild winters, good sunshine and generally moderate summer drought. Distance from the Gironde makes local frost and ripening differences more relevant. No soil automatically makes “powerful” wine: [[concept.terroir|terroir]] emerges from its interaction with vine, season and producer.
:::

:::detail{#hoogte-en-water parent="landschap-klimaat-en-bodem" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-listrac-medoc-specification-2025"}
### Elevation works through water and air

The “roof of the Médoc” is not a mountain climate. A few dozen metres mainly alter runoff, exposure and cool-air movement. Gravel may suffer drought early; compact clay can remain wet after rain. Ground management, rootstock and harvest timing must respond differently.
:::

:::section{#druivenrassen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-listrac-medoc-specification-2025"}
## Grape varieties — Merlot beside Cabernet

Merlot is the most planted variety in many Listrac vineyards and gives plum fruit and mid-palate weight. Cabernet Sauvignon supplies blackcurrant, freshness and tannin; Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot may add fragrance, spice or structure. Malbec and Carménère also belong to the traditional legal palette.

The current specification also creates limited trial space for adaptation varieties including Arinarnoa, Castets, Marselan and Touriga Nacional. Permission does not mean widespread adoption: planting and use are restricted and subject to formal conditions.
:::

:::section{#wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-listrac-medoc-specification-2025"}
## Viticulture and winemaking — learning to dose structure

Harvest decisions balance fruit ripeness, acidity and mature skins. Red fermentation on skins builds colour and tannin; extraction must suit each vat. Malolactic conversion is followed by blending and maturation. Barriques are common but not a compulsory style template.

Over-extraction can turn natural firmness dry, while too little can hollow out the middle. The best wines retain both grip and juice: structure becomes support rather than an end in itself.
:::

:::section{#appellationregels depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-listrac-medoc-specification-2025"}
## Appellation rules — red wine on a current legal basis

Listrac-Médoc protects still red wine. Its specification regulates origin, grapes, vineyard management, ripeness, yields, analysis and maturation. The current French specification was homologated in 2025 and the consolidated European communication followed in 2026. Older wine was, of course, made under the rules of its own harvest.
:::

:::detail{#actuele-drempels parent="appellationregels" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-listrac-medoc-specification-2025"}
### Figures are minima, not tasting notes

The base yield is 57 hectolitres per hectare and the general ceiling 63. Such thresholds safeguard production and traceability, not quality. The official specification remains authoritative for exact planting, analytical and transitional rules.
:::

:::section{#classificatie-en-producenten depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-listrac-medoc-overview"}
## Classification and producers — Crus Bourgeois and cooperation

Listrac has no 1855 classified growth. Crus Bourgeois provide a more familiar producer framework, while the cooperative shows that ownership and winemaking can be organised collectively. Names help orientation, but an overview should not create a long alternative ranking: current performance, cuvée and vintage remain separate questions.
:::

:::section{#wijnstijl-en-flesontwikkeling depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-listrac-medoc-overview source.inao-listrac-medoc-specification-2025"}
## Wine style and bottle development — compact, fresh and patient

Listrac can combine blackcurrant, plum, spice and cedar with fresh acidity and distinct tannin. Merlot supplies breadth; Cabernet keeps line and length. A young wine may seem more angular than a Margaux of similar weight, but that is not a fixed law.

Bottle age may bring tobacco, forest floor and leather as structure softens. Lighter wines need not wait long; concentrated examples from strong years can develop considerably. Producer and storage matter too.
:::

:::section{#etiket-en-aankoop depth="foundation" source_refs="source.dgccrf-wine-labels-2025"}
## Labels and buying — status has a date

Read appellation, producer, cuvée, [[concept.vintage|vintage]] and bottler separately. When a classification is stated, check which system and edition it refers to. For older bottles, condition and provenance matter at least as much as a rank once printed on paper.
:::

:::section{#hedendaagse-ontwikkelingen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inrae-bordeaux-climate-adaptation source.inao-listrac-medoc-specification-2025"}
## Contemporary developments — ripening in a less predictable climate

More warmth can help Cabernet but brings drought, sunburn and rapid sugar accumulation. Wet periods and fungi have not disappeared. Adaptation varieties, ground cover, organic matter, altered pruning and precise picking are distinct tools; none applies identically to every gravel and clay parcel.
:::

Listrac-Médoc is the higher inland communal Médoc where Merlot and Cabernet make structured red wine, with Crus Bourgeois and cooperation more useful as references than 1855.
:::
