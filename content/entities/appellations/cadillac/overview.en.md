:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-cadillac-specification-2026"}
**Cadillac** is an appellation for sweet white wine on the Right Bank of the Garonne, southeast of Bordeaux. Sémillon usually provides the core; noble rot or shrivelling on the vine concentrates the grapes before pickers harvest them in several selective passes.

The short name demands attention: **Cadillac** is sweet white, whereas [[appellation.cadillac-cotes-de-bordeaux|Cadillac Côtes de Bordeaux]] is red. This is not a minor label detail, but two separate appellations with different wines and rules.
:::

:::section{#ligging-en-afbakening depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-cadillac-specification-2026"}
## Location and boundaries — a long ribbon with two gaps

Cadillac follows the Right Bank of the Garonne for roughly thirty kilometres. The official production area covers parts of 22 communes around the bastide of Cadillac-sur-Garonne, but not automatically every parcel within them. Only officially delimited vineyards qualify. [@source.boagri-cadillac-specification-2026]

The separate zones of [[appellation.loupiac|Loupiac]] and [[appellation.sainte-croix-du-mont|Sainte-Croix-du-Mont]] form gaps within this longer area. Cadillac is therefore not one compact village terroir, but a sequence of selected slopes, plateaus and lower slopes within [[region.entre-deux-mers|Entre-deux-Mers]].
:::

:::section{#geschiedenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-cadillac-specification-2026"}
## History — sweet wine before the separate name

Vineyards along this bank supplied white wine long before Cadillac was recognised as a separate AOC in 1973. Trade with northern Europe, especially Holland in the seventeenth century, encouraged demand across the wider district for sweet white wines suited to transport and keeping.

The modern name grew from the southern zone of Premières Côtes de Bordeaux. Earlier regional rules already permitted Cadillac as an addition; recognition in 1973 turned that selection into its own appellation. [@source.boagri-cadillac-specification-2026]
:::

:::detail{#handel-en-erkenning parent="geschiedenis" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.boagri-cadillac-specification-2026"}
### From trading practice to delimited origin

Development did not follow one straight line. After phylloxera, the Cadillac agricultural society helped with replanting, grafting and disease control from 1884. In 1919 a group of Right Bank communes was administratively distinguished as an origin for great white wines; rules from 1937 and later years prepared the separate Cadillac name.

This history explains why old labels and the present appellation structure do not align word for word. A historic Premières Côtes de Bordeaux label must be read in its own period; it is not automatically an old spelling for today's wine.
:::

:::section{#landschap-klimaat-en-bodem depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-cadillac-specification-2026"}
## Landscape, climate and soils — from river lowland to limestone plateau

The land rises from the Garonne through low terraces and lower slopes to plateaus. Streams have cut into the limestone base, creating distinct slopes in places and cool, wooded valleys. The highest points reach about 120 metres: marked relief for Bordeaux, but not high hill country. [@source.boagri-cadillac-specification-2026]

Limestone, molasse, clay, gravel, sand and silt occur in different combinations. This mosaic affects drainage, warmth and vine vigour; it does not prescribe a fixed aroma. A wine from the long northern sector need not share the same balance as one from the south.
:::

:::detail{#water-en-mist parent="landschap-klimaat-en-bodem" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.boagri-cadillac-specification-2026"}
### Mist opens a possibility; sun and wind must contain it

In autumn, moisture from the Garonne's meanders can reach the vineyards in the morning. Warm sunny periods and moving air then dry grapes and canopy again. That alternation can support [[concept.botrytis|noble rot]], but wet weather may interrupt concentration or bring unwanted rot instead.

Botrytis is therefore not an automatic consequence of river mist. Parcel, drainage, grape health and harvest weather determine whether a producer can select usefully concentrated fruit.

## Grape varieties — Sémillon as the carrying voice

The rules permit [[grape.semillon|Sémillon]], [[grape.sauvignon-blanc|Sauvignon Blanc]], [[grape.sauvignon-gris|Sauvignon Gris]] and [[grape.muscadelle|Muscadelle]]. Sémillon is the classic base: ripe fruit can carry concentration and a broad texture. Sauvignon may add acidity and aromatic tension; Muscadelle can widen the perfume.

These are roles, not a mandatory recipe. Proportions change with parcels, botrytis selection and vintage, and legal permission does not reveal how much of each variety is actually in a bottle.
:::

:::section{#wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-cadillac-specification-2026"}
## Viticulture and winemaking — picking what is ready today

Cadillac must be made from overripe grapes affected by noble rot and/or shrivelled on the vine. Harvest is by hand in successive *tries*: pickers take only suitable bunches or berries and return later for fruit that needs more time. [@source.boagri-cadillac-specification-2026]

Concentrated fruit gives little, sugar-rich juice. Fermentation is often slow and stops while sugar remains. Barrel, tank, fermentation length and blending remain producer choices; the appellation prescribes no single style recipe.
:::

:::detail{#tries-zonder-zekerheid parent="wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.boagri-cadillac-specification-2026"}
### Every pass is also an economic decision

Waiting may bring more concentration, but also rain, grey rot or loss. Sending a team through the same vineyard several times is labour intensive. The producer therefore reads weather, ripeness and health continually and may select little or no Cadillac in a difficult year.

This is why liquoreux is not simply a product of “late harvest”. Its style grows from repeated choices and from accepting that not every grape becomes appellation wine.
:::

:::section{#appellationregels depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-cadillac-specification-2026"}
## Appellation rules — sweet white from strict selection

Cadillac protects only still white wine with residual sugar. The rules connect origin to permitted parcels and varieties, overripe fruit, hand picking by tries and a period of maturation. They establish a verifiable floor, not how refined or long-lived every bottle will be.
:::

:::detail{#actuele-drempels parent="appellationregels" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.boagri-cadillac-specification-2026"}
### Why Cadillac is not legally identical to its neighbours

The 2026 specification requires at least 4,500 vines per hectare, 255 grams of sugar per litre of must, a base yield of 37 hectolitres per hectare and at least 51 grams of fermentable sugar per litre in the bottled wine. Maturation continues at least until 15 March after harvest; consumer release may begin on 31 March. [@source.boagri-cadillac-specification-2026]

Those figures are stricter than or otherwise different from several neighbouring sweet-wine appellations. They help identify the legal wine, but are not a flavour score.
:::

:::section{#classificatie-en-producenten depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-cadillac-specification-2026"}
## Classification and producers — origin without a château ladder

Cadillac has no official château classification. A familiar name or high price therefore carries no formal rank within the appellation. Judge producers through their sites, selection, style and record across vintages; an arbitrary list would confuse reputation with regulation.
:::

:::section{#wijnstijl-en-flesontwikkeling depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-cadillac-specification-2026"}
## Wine style and bottle development — richness needs counterweight

Cadillac commonly moves between ripe yellow fruit, citrus, honeyed notes and a full sweet texture. Acidity, a slight bitter edge and aromatic freshness can keep the wine from feeling heavy. Botrytis, shrivelling, barrel and vintage shift that balance.

With bottle age, fresh fruit may give way to dried fruit, spice, wax, nuts or caramel-like notes. Not every wine develops in the same way or for the same time: producer, harvest selection, acidity, storage and bottle condition remain decisive.
:::

:::section{#etiket-en-aankoop depth="foundation" source_refs="source.dgccrf-wine-labels-2025 source.boagri-cadillac-specification-2026"}
## Labels and buying — read the word after Cadillac

If **Cadillac** alone appears as the appellation, expect sweet white wine. If it says **Cadillac Côtes de Bordeaux**, the wine is red. Then check producer, vintage, bottler and bottle size; they tell you more about the specific bottle than the place name alone.

Compare Cadillac with Loupiac, Sainte-Croix-du-Mont or [[appellation.cerons|Cérons]] to understand landscape and legal differences, not to create an automatic hierarchy.
:::

:::section{#hedendaagse-ontwikkelingen depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inrae-bordeaux-climate-adaptation source.boagri-cadillac-specification-2026"}
## Contemporary developments — sweet wine remains a choice

Warmer growing seasons may accelerate ripening, but noble rot needs a usable window of moisture and drying. Drought, heat, heavy rain and harvest labour do not make that window more predictable. Producers must monitor acidity, grape health, canopy shade and picking time together.

There is also a market decision: the same wider district can produce wines under other appellations. Cadillac therefore remains a deliberate selection, not the automatic destination of every white grape.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Cadillac is sweet white wine from a long Right Bank ribbon. Its full name separates it from red Cadillac Côtes de Bordeaux; successive hand selections and strict ripeness thresholds give the appellation its own place among Bordeaux's liquoreux wines.
:::
