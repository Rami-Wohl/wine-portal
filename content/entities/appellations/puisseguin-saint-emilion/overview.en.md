Puisseguin-Saint-Émilion lies about ten kilometres northeast of [[appellation.saint-emilion|Saint-Émilion]], across an incised landscape of limestone plateau, clay-limestone slopes and wooded valleys. The appellation protects red wine only.

Merlot dominates and can be both fleshy and fresh here. Cabernets and Malbec allow growers to tune structure, fragrance and dark fruit.
:::

:::section{#ligging-en-afbakening depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-puisseguin-saint-emilion-specification-2023"}
## Location and boundaries — old Puisseguin on plateau and slope

The legal zone follows the territory of Puisseguin as it stood before its 1989 merger with Monbadon. The whole modern commune therefore does not automatically belong to the appellation. Only officially delimited vineyard parcels within that zone qualify.

The name belongs to the four so-called satellites, but Puisseguin-Saint-Émilion is not part of the Saint-Émilion PDO. Commune name and hyphens are legally essential.
:::

:::detail{#gemeentefusie-en-herkomst parent="ligging-en-afbakening" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-puisseguin-saint-emilion-specification-2023"}
### A municipal merger does not move a wine region by itself

When Monbadon joined Puisseguin administratively, the commune grew but the appellation did not automatically expand. The specification preserves the older territorial boundary. This is precisely why administrative and wine-geographic maps must not silently be equated.
:::

:::section{#geschiedenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-puisseguin-saint-emilion-overview source.inao-puisseguin-saint-emilion-specification-2023"}
## History — a hill name with a legal suffix

The district bears traces of old settlement and viticulture. Puisseguin is traditionally connected with *puy*, a hill or height, and Seguin, a name from the early Middle Ages. Trade through Libourne later linked the wine to the wider Bordeaux market.

Like the other satellites, Puisseguin obtained through court decisions in 1921 and 1923 the right to append Saint-Émilion to its commune name. The AOC was recognised in 1936.
:::

:::section{#landschap-klimaat-en-bodem depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-puisseguin-saint-emilion-specification-2023 source.inao-puisseguin-saint-emilion-overview"}
## Landscape, climate and soils — limestone, clay and incised valleys

The plateau rests on limestone; slopes carry abundant clay-limestone and local molasse. Lower or more gently rolling sectors can contain sand and silt. Woodland and valley floors trace the relief, but wet, poorly drained ground is excluded from parcel delimitation.

Many slopes face south or southeast and receive ample light. This supports ripening, while clay holds water. Drainage matters in wet years; in dry ones, the same reserve may help.
:::

:::detail{#ondergrondse-groeves parent="landschap-klimaat-en-bodem" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-puisseguin-saint-emilion-overview"}
### Limestone beneath the vineyard

Former stone quarries left underground spaces that some producers use as cellars. Their steady temperature is practical for storage, but a quarry alone proves nothing about the quality of a parcel above it.
:::

:::section{#druivenrassen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-puisseguin-saint-emilion-specification-2023"}
## Grape varieties — Merlot on cool clay

[[grape.merlot|Merlot]], [[grape.cabernet-franc|Cabernet Franc]], [[grape.cabernet-sauvignon|Cabernet Sauvignon]] and [[grape.malbec|Malbec]] are principal varieties. Carménère and Petit Verdot are accessory and jointly limited.

Merlot can develop ample fruit and fleshy texture on clay-limestone. Cabernet Franc helps retain freshness and aroma, Cabernet Sauvignon can add backbone in warm positions and Malbec colour and dark spice.
:::

:::section{#wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-puisseguin-saint-emilion-specification-2023"}
## Viticulture and winemaking — healthy fruit as the starting point

Minimum density is 5,500 vines per hectare. Sorting becomes compulsory when more than five per cent of fruit is unripe or unhealthy. Full-surface chemical weed control is prohibited and estates must meet a recognised environmental approach.

Skin fermentation builds colour and tannin; malolactic conversion, [[concept.assemblage|blending]] and maturation follow. Barriques are common but the specification does not require an oak flavour.
:::

:::section{#appellationregels depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-puisseguin-saint-emilion-specification-2023"}
## Appellation rules — red with spring maturation

Puisseguin-Saint-Émilion is for still red wine. Base yield is 53 hectolitres per hectare and minimum natural alcohol is 11%. Wine matures until at least 15 March after harvest and may reach consumers from 31 March. [@source.inao-puisseguin-saint-emilion-specification-2023]
:::

:::section{#classificatie-en-producenten depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-puisseguin-saint-emilion-overview"}
## Classification and producers — châteaux without borrowed rank

The Saint-Émilion Grand Cru Classé classification does not apply to Puisseguin-Saint-Émilion. No separate official château hierarchy exists here. Family estates, cooperative production and independent properties can make different styles.

An attractive château or old cellar is context, not proof of rank. Look for parcel information, grapes, harvest and maturation.
:::

:::section{#wijnstijl-en-flesontwikkeling depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-puisseguin-saint-emilion-overview"}
## Wine style and bottle development — fleshy without compulsory weight

Ripe plum, cherry and blackberry, spice and clear but rounded tannin form a common stylistic direction. Cabernet and oak may add structure; earlier harvest or gentler extraction keeps other examples more agile.

Tobacco, leather and earth can emerge with bottle age. Serve around 16–18°C and let producer, cuvée and vintage guide cellaring.
:::

:::section{#etiket-en-aankoop depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-puisseguin-saint-emilion-specification-2023"}
## Labels and buying — Puisseguin determines the origin

Read Puisseguin-Saint-Émilion as one complete appellation. The words Saint-Émilion confer no right to Saint-Émilion or Saint-Émilion Grand Cru. The broader indication “Grand Vin de Bordeaux” may appear but is not a classification.
:::

:::section{#hedendaagse-ontwikkelingen depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inrae-bordeaux-climate-adaptation source.inao-puisseguin-saint-emilion-specification-2023"}
## Contemporary developments — sun, clay and new limits

Southern aspects aid cooler years but increase sunburn and rapid sugar accumulation during heat. Ground cover, shade and careful harvest dates matter more. The recent specification also links the appellation more explicitly to recognised environmental measures.
:::

:::key-idea{#kernidee depth="foundation"}
Puisseguin-Saint-Émilion is an independent red appellation on limestone and clay-limestone northeast of Saint-Émilion. Its complete name, pre-merger boundary and Merlot-led but varied blends make it distinct.
:::
