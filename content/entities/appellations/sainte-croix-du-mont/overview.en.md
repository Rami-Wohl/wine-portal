:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-sainte-croix-du-mont-overview source.boagri-sainte-croix-du-mont-specification-2026"}
**Sainte-Croix-du-Mont** is a small sweet-white-wine appellation confined to one commune on the right bank of the Garonne. Vineyards occupy slopes and a limestone plateau above the river. Its local fossil oyster bank is tangible geological history, yet the wine is shaped by grapes, autumn weather and selection—not by a fossil somehow tasting through into the glass.

Sémillon usually leads the blend. Sauvignon Blanc, Sauvignon Gris and Muscadelle can add freshness and fragrance, while hand-picked *tries* decide which overripe berries are harvested.
:::

:::section{#ligging-en-afbakening depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-sainte-croix-du-mont-specification-2026"}
## Location and boundaries — one commune on the high right bank

The appellation lies roughly forty-five kilometres southeast of Bordeaux and is limited to the commune of Sainte-Croix-du-Mont. Only officially delimited parcels qualify. Harvesting, vinification, preparation and maturation normally take place within the commune, with a defined nearby area as a practical exception. [@source.boagri-sainte-croix-du-mont-specification-2026]

The broader [[appellation.sauternes|Sauternes]] and Barsac landscape lies across the Garonne; [[appellation.loupiac|Loupiac]] and [[appellation.cadillac|Cadillac]] sit downstream. Proximity creates kinship, but does not make the names interchangeable.
:::

:::section{#geschiedenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-sainte-croix-du-mont-specification-2026"}
## History — trade before delimitation

From the seventeenth century, sweet white wines from the Garonne banks gained a clear place in northern European trade. Dutch buyers sought white wines able to combine sweetness, keeping capacity and sea transport. That network helped a regional practice grow long before the present appellation existed in law.

Winegrowers in Sainte-Croix-du-Mont formed a syndicate in 1908. The AOC was recognised in 1936. This did not invent a new style; it gave an existing origin a verifiable name and collective framework. [@source.boagri-sainte-croix-du-mont-specification-2026]
:::

:::detail{#handel-en-erkenning parent="geschiedenis" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.boagri-sainte-croix-du-mont-specification-2026"}
### Old renown is not a modern classification

Historic exhibitions, trade references and comparisons with more famous sweet wines show a reputation before 1936. They do not constitute a current château classification. The appellation has no official producer ranking, so quality still needs judging by producer, site and vintage.

The age of the village or vineyard likewise proves no uninterrupted, unchanging style. Grapes, harvest practice and legal requirements shifted over time.
:::

:::section{#landschap-klimaat-en-bodem depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-sainte-croix-du-mont-specification-2026"}
## Landscape, climate and soils — plateau, valleys and a fossil oyster bank

The village and some vineyards sit on a limestone plateau above the Garonne, reaching roughly 120 metres. South- and southwest-facing slopes descend towards the river; small streams cut wooded valleys into the terrain. This is varied low hill country, not mountains or a continuous sheer cliff. [@source.boagri-sainte-croix-du-mont-specification-2026]

Beneath the church and château, a limestone layer packed with fossil oysters is locally exposed. Caves in the outcrop also found practical use. Elsewhere limestone, molasse, clay, loam and old gravel alternate. This variation affects drainage and vigour, but does not prescribe a literal ‘limestone taste’.
:::

:::detail{#water-en-mist parent="landschap-klimaat-en-bodem" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.boagri-sainte-croix-du-mont-specification-2026"}
### Mist opens a window; sun and wind must close it

Moisture from the Garonne and its tributaries can encourage [[concept.botrytis|Botrytis cinerea]] on autumn mornings. Drier, airy conditions must then allow berries to shrivel gradually. When that alternation works, sugar, acid and flavour become concentrated.

Persistently wet weather can bring harmful rot; a dry autumn may lead mainly to passerillage on the vine. Both routes are permitted, but neither removes the need for strict selection.
:::

## Grape varieties — Sémillon at the core, with three possible partners

[[grape.semillon|Sémillon]] is usually the principal grape, capable of combining richness, texture and susceptibility to noble rot. [[grape.sauvignon-blanc|Sauvignon Blanc]] and [[grape.sauvignon-gris|Sauvignon Gris]] can contribute acidity and aromatic tension; [[grape.muscadelle|Muscadelle]] can add floral and spicy accents.

All four are permitted, but the rules prescribe no universal blend. Actual proportions depend on plantings, ripeness, selection and producer choice.
:::

:::section{#wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-sainte-croix-du-mont-specification-2026"}
## Viticulture and winemaking — harvesting berry by berry

The appellation uses overripe grapes affected by noble rot and/or shrivelled on the vine. Harvesting is exclusively by hand in successive tries. Pickers revisit the same parcel, taking only what is suitable at that moment. [@source.boagri-sainte-croix-du-mont-specification-2026]

Concentrated fruit yields little juice. After pressing, the sugar-rich must ferments until yeast activity stops or is stopped, leaving residual sugar. Barrel, tank and maturation colour the style; the decisive step remains what was selected in the vineyard.
:::

:::detail{#tries-zonder-zekerheid parent="wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.boagri-sainte-croix-du-mont-specification-2026"}
### Every extra pass is another wager on the weather

Picking later can bring greater concentration, but also losses to rain, rot or desiccation. Successive tries demand considerable labour and make yearly volume variable. A small crop may directly reflect what remained healthy and sufficiently concentrated.
:::

:::section{#appellationregels depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-sainte-croix-du-mont-specification-2026"}
## Appellation rules — sweet white wine from a defined place

Sainte-Croix-du-Mont is reserved for still white wine with residual sugar. The rules specify area, parcels, grapes, planting, overripeness, hand-picked tries, yield and minimum maturation, among other matters. They protect origin and method, not one flavour or guaranteed quality level.
:::

:::detail{#actuele-drempels parent="appellationregels" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.boagri-sainte-croix-du-mont-specification-2026"}
### The current figures behind the name

The 2026 specification requires at least 5,000 vines per hectare. Must contains at least 238 grams of sugar per litre for Sémillon and 229 grams for the other grapes. The base yield is 40 hectolitres per hectare; bottled wine retains at least 45 grams of fermentable sugar per litre and matures until at least 15 January after harvest. [@source.boagri-sainte-croix-du-mont-specification-2026]

These are legal floors and ceilings. They do not reveal how much botrytis a cuvée contains, how strictly selection was made or how long the wine can mature well.
:::

:::section{#classificatie-en-producenten depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-sainte-croix-du-mont-specification-2026"}
## Classification and producers — reputation without an official ladder

Sainte-Croix-du-Mont has no official château classification. Historic renown or a familiar producer therefore carries no formal rank. Differences in site, tries, maturation and consistency are real, but need judging by estate and vintage.
:::

:::section{#wijnstijl-en-flesontwikkeling depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-sainte-croix-du-mont-overview source.boagri-sainte-croix-du-mont-specification-2026"}
## Wine style and bottle development — from golden fruit to savoury depth

Young wines may combine white and yellow fruit, citrus, flowers, honey and candied notes with a full, sweet texture. Sufficient acidity keeps richness from becoming heavy. Botrytis, passerillage and any oak maturation create a broad range.

Bottle age can bring dried apricot, marmalade, wax, toast, spice and nuts. Development and longevity depend on concentration, acidity, producer, vintage, closure and storage—not on the appellation name alone.
:::

:::section{#etiket-en-aankoop depth="foundation" source_refs="source.dgccrf-wine-labels-2025 source.boagri-sainte-croix-du-mont-specification-2026"}
## Labels and buying — read the whole name

Sainte-Croix-du-Mont on a label means sweet white appellation wine from the delimited commune. Then look for producer, vintage, bottler and bottle size. The name is not a subcategory of [[appellation.sauternes|Sauternes]], although that zone lies across the river.

Compare it with [[appellation.loupiac|Loupiac]] or Cadillac through landscape, selection and style, not as an automatic quality ladder. Producer and vintage remain meaningful within every name.
:::

:::section{#hedendaagse-ontwikkelingen depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inrae-bordeaux-climate-adaptation source.boagri-sainte-croix-du-mont-specification-2026"}
## Contemporary developments — concentration is not the same as balance

Warm years can raise sugar quickly while acidity, skin health and botrytis follow their own timetable. Drought, heat and heavy autumn rain also alter the chance of a useful mist-and-sun rhythm. Canopy, soil care and harvest timing therefore require sharper judgement.

Selective harvesting is costly too. Climate, available pickers and demand jointly determine which lots truly carry the appellation. Making less can be wiser than forcing fruit into a predetermined volume.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Sainte-Croix-du-Mont connects a striking limestone slope above the Garonne with a fragile autumn process. The fossil oyster bank makes the place visible; overripe fruit, hand-picked tries and balance make the wine.
:::
