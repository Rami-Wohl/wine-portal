Graves Supérieures is the historic appellation for white Graves with perceptible residual sugar: richer than dry white, but generally less intensely concentrated than [[appellation.sauternes|Sauternes]]. It comes from the same broad production area as [[appellation.graves|Graves]] and relies chiefly on Sémillon, Sauvignon and Muscadelle.

The name is in legal transition. France has requested cancellation of the separate European registration and intends to incorporate the style into Graves. The EU published that request on 3 June 2026. On the verification date, 22 September 2026, the Union's legal register still listed Graves Supérieures as registered, with an amendment in progress and no removal flag. This page therefore preserves both the wine style and the transition without anticipating the outcome.
:::

:::section{#ligging-en-afbakening depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-graves-specification-2025 source.inao-graves-overview"}
## Location and boundaries — Graves' sweeter side

The geographical basis follows the long Graves zone on the Garonne's left bank south of Bordeaux. It should not be confused with Sauternes and Barsac, farther south with their own boundaries and more exacting traditions of botrytis selection. Nor does “supérieures” mean that every Graves Supérieures outranks every Graves; it is a separate historic product name.
:::

:::section{#geschiedenis-en-juridische-status depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-graves-specification-2025 source.legifrance-graves-transition-2025 source.eurlex-graves-superieures-cancellation-2026"}
## History — a name moving towards consolidation

Graves and Graves Supérieures were recognised as appellations in 1937. The latter reserved space for white wine with residual sugar from overripe, selectively harvested grapes. In practice it remained a small, little-known category beside dry Graves and the celebrated sweet origins around the Ciron.

A French order of September 2025 approves a new Graves specification incorporating the sweeter style. The order takes effect only when the European Commission approves cancellation of Graves Supérieures. The June 2026 EU notice opened a formal stage and specified a three-month opposition window; publication of a request is not final cancellation.
:::

:::detail{#waarom-de-overgang-telt parent="geschiedenis-en-juridische-status" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.legifrance-graves-transition-2025 source.eurlex-graves-superieures-cancellation-2026 source.eambrosia-graves-superieures-register-2026"}
### Deferred rules require date stamps

The French order explicitly says that the new Graves specification applies only from European approval of cancellation. Two official texts can consequently appear relevant at once: the existing joint specification for Graves and Graves Supérieures, and a new Graves text waiting to take effect. For education, the safe formulation is dated: on 22 September 2026 the name remained registered in eAmbrosia and the amendment procedure was still in progress.
:::

:::section{#appellationregels depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-graves-specification-2025 source.legifrance-graves-transition-2025"}
## Appellation rules — current specification beside a future text

Under the existing specification, Graves Supérieures protects still white wine with residual sugar from overripe, manually selected grapes. A new French Graves text is ready to absorb that product category, but has a deferred effective date. A current bottle must therefore be read under the legal situation governing its production and labelling, not only the direction of reform.
:::

:::section{#landschap-klimaat-en-bodem depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-graves-specification-2025 source.graves-winegrowers-wines"}
## Landscape, climate and soils — ripeness without requiring the Ciron

Gravel terraces, sand and clay-rich subsoils create different water and warmth regimes across Graves. The mild Atlantic climate can allow long ripening, while autumn rain makes selection risky. Graves Supérieures is made from overripe grapes; botrytis may occur, but the appellation does not depend on the specific mist pattern used to explain Sauternes.
:::

:::section{#druivenrassen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-graves-specification-2025"}
## Grape varieties — Sémillon for texture, Sauvignon for tension

[[grape.semillon|Sémillon]], [[grape.sauvignon-blanc|Sauvignon Blanc]], Sauvignon Gris and [[grape.muscadelle|Muscadelle]] form the palette. Sémillon can bring ripe, waxy texture and susceptibility to noble rot. Sauvignon helps retain aroma and acidity; Muscadelle can add a floral accent. The blend must connect sweetness and concentration to sufficient freshness.
:::

:::section{#wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-graves-specification-2025"}
## Viticulture and winemaking — repeated passes, small volumes

Grapes are hand-picked in successive selections. Only sufficiently ripe or concentrated bunches and berries are taken; others remain for a later pass. After pressing, sugar-rich must ferments slowly. Residual sugar remains because fermentation ends or is stopped before all sugar is converted.

The process consumes labour and time and lowers yields. The goal is not sweetness alone, but a wine in which ripe fruit, texture and acidity support each other.
:::

:::detail{#wettelijke-drempels parent="wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-graves-specification-2025"}
### The specification defines concentration

The existing specification requires at least 221 grams of sugar per litre of must, minimum natural potential alcohol of 13.5 per cent and a base yield of 40 hectolitres per hectare. Harvesting must be manual in successive selections. These figures are legal limits, not automatic measures of intensity or quality in an individual bottle.
:::

## Classification and producers — Supérieures is not a rank

The name has no official producer hierarchy. “Supérieures” denotes the separate historic appellation and its sweeter white style, not a rank above dry Graves. Producer, harvest selection and balance must be assessed wine by wine.
:::

:::section{#wijnstijl-en-glas depth="foundation" source_refs="source.graves-winegrowers-wines source.inao-graves-specification-2025"}
## Wine style and bottle development — sweet, but not without freshness

Expect ripe pear, peach, honey, flowers and sometimes candied citrus or a waxy note. The best balance comes from acidity and a lightly bitter or spicy counterpoint, not sugar alone. Serve cool but not icy, perhaps with blue cheese, desserts no sweeter than the wine, pâté or spicy savoury dishes.

The category has far less market visibility than Sauternes. Producer and vintage are consequently more useful buying anchors than a fixed expectation of prestige or ageing.
:::

:::section{#etiket-en-aankoop depth="foundation" source_refs="source.dgccrf-wine-labels-2025 source.eurlex-graves-superieures-cancellation-2026"}
## Labels and buying — a name that remains on old bottles

Read Graves Supérieures as a protected origin and wine style, not a general superlative. If cancellation becomes final, existing bottles and historic labels do not disappear. Newly produced comparable wine may then appear under amended Graves rules. For current purchases, check producer, vintage, sweetness information and the legal name on that particular bottle.
:::

## Contemporary developments — follow the register, not the prediction

Consolidation may make this small style more visible under a better-known name, while removing an historic term from new production. Until the competent authorities formally complete the procedure, reliable wording remains date-bound. This status must therefore be reverified at every future content review.
