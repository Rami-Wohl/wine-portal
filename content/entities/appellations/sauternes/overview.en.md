:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-overview source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026"}
Sauternes is sweet white wine built on a rare tension: rich but not leaden, opulent yet, at its best, carried by freshness. The appellation lies about forty kilometres southeast of [[region.bordeaux|Bordeaux]], around the small river Ciron. Morning damp can give way to drier, ventilated afternoons — just the alternation in which [[concept.botrytis|noble rot]] may concentrate ripe grapes without merely spoiling them. [@source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026]

That natural process is not an advanced curiosity; it is the key to the wine. Sémillon, Sauvignon Blanc and their companions are not picked all at once. Harvesters return several times and take only suitable berries or parts of bunches. The costly, shrivelled fruit yields little juice but can produce flavours recalling citrus, apricot, flowers, saffron, honey and candied fruit.

## Location and boundaries — five communes between river and forest

The protected zone lies on the left bank of the Garonne, around Langon and at the edge of the Landes forest. It comprises five communes: Barsac, Bommes, Fargues, Preignac and Sauternes. “Sauternes” may therefore mean the village, the appellation or the wine in the glass. [@source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026]

[[appellation.barsac|Barsac]] needs special attention. It is one of the five communes in Sauternes' geographical zone but also has its own appellation. Wine from the relevant delimited Barsac vineyards may appear as Barsac or Sauternes under the applicable rules. The two names overlap legally; they are not two neatly separated neighbours.
:::

:::detail{#vijf-gemeenten parent="ligging-en-afbakening" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026"}
### A commune boundary is not yet a vineyard boundary

Not every field within those five communes can produce Sauternes. Official production land is delimited parcel by parcel, while a separate immediate-proximity zone applies to winemaking and maturation. A description or simple overview map can orientate, but never replaces the official delimitation documents.

The future Atlas view must preserve that hierarchy: first the position within Bordeaux, then the five communes and only then verified production parcels. Without official geometry we do not draw a falsely precise wine map.
:::

:::section{#geschiedenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026 source.msha-bordeaux-wine-history"}
## History — how late-picked grapes won worldwide fame

Viticulture has been documented in the Sauternais since the Middle Ages. Its later renown did not spring from one accidental “discovery” of mouldy grapes, but from a long interaction between place, trade and increasingly refined selection. Maritime trade grew in the seventeenth century, while the old prévôté of Barsac — covering much of today's area — was already valued separately. [@source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026]

A 1741 text records that harvest waited until the grapes looked almost rotten and returned several times to achieve greater sweetness. The core practice therefore predates the famous nineteenth-century ranking by more than a century. Nor did tradition then freeze: growers learned to read the difference between desirable concentration and destructive rot ever more sharply.
:::

:::detail{#handel-en-selectieve-oogst parent="geschiedenis" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026 source.msha-bordeaux-wine-history"}
### Trade gave a labour-intensive style a market

Selective picking requires many hands and yields little wine. Such a method can endure only when buyers value the difference and pay for it. Northern European trade, Bordeaux brokers and the reputations of individual estates helped establish sweet wine as a recognisable category.

That does not mean foreign demand single-handedly “invented” the wine. Climate and grapes offered possibilities, local knowledge made wine from them and trade gave that wine reach and economic value. The result is more credible history than the familiar anecdote about a single forgotten harvest date.
:::

:::detail{#van-1855-naar-aoc parent="geschiedenis" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.gcc1855-classification-history source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026"}
### 1855 ranked reputation; 1936 protected origin

For the 1855 Paris Universal Exhibition, Bordeaux brokers ranked 27 sweet crus from Sauternes and Barsac. The list ordered existing trading reputations and prices; it did not draw an appellation boundary or write production rules. [@source.gcc1855-classification-history]

The Sauternes AOC, recognised on 30 September 1936, did something else: it legally tied the name to origin and local production practices, including overripe grapes affected by noble rot and successive selections. Classification and appellation overlap, but answer different questions.
:::

:::section{#landschap-klimaat-en-bodem depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026 source.brgm-bordeaux-geology-2022"}
## Landscape, climate and soils — mist is only half the story

The Ciron flows from cooler woodland towards the broader Garonne. Around their meeting point, damp air may condense in autumn. Morning mist keeps berry skins moist; sun, wind and natural ventilation can dry them later in the day. That rhythm helps *Botrytis cinerea* behave as noble rot. [@source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026]

Yet “mist makes Sauternes” is too simple. Grapes must first be ripe and sound, moisture must not persist indefinitely and each site responds differently. An autumn may bring beautiful botrytis, uneven infection, grey rot or almost no useful concentration. The wine grows from a favourable landscape, not a guaranteed natural recipe.
:::

:::detail{#ciron-en-botrytis parent="landschap-klimaat-en-bodem" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026"}
### Noble rot is controlled dehydration, not ordinary decay

Botrytis makes the berry skin porous, allowing water to evaporate and concentrating sugar, acidity and flavour compounds. The fungus also changes the chemistry and therefore the aromatic character. Suitable berries shrivel slowly; harvesters sometimes call intensely concentrated examples *rôtis*.

In persistently wet weather, the same fungus can turn into unwanted grey rot, costing the fruit purity and structure. Growers do not simply wait for “more fungus”. The decisive skill is recognising which berry is usable at which moment.
:::

:::detail{#bodems-en-afwatering parent="landschap-klimaat-en-bodem" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026 source.brgm-bordeaux-geology-2022"}
### Gravel rises, limestone and clay govern water above all

The area rests on a complex substratum shaped by old changes in the Garonne's course and Quaternary deposits. Gravel-sand rises sit over Tertiary limestone, which approaches the surface around Barsac. Elsewhere gravel, sand, clay, siliceous layers and local iron-rich *alios* alternate. [@source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026]

Those soils do not literally taste of honey or saffron. They influence drainage, water storage, rooting, warmth and vigour. In a region that must keep ripe fruit on the vine deep into autumn, water relations help determine how soundly and evenly the grapes reach that season.
:::

:::section{#druivenrassen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026"}
## Grape varieties — Sémillon carries, Sauvignon tightens

[[grape.semillon|Sémillon]] is the classic backbone. It can bring texture and volume and responds especially valuably to noble rot here. [[grape.sauvignon-blanc|Sauvignon Blanc]] may add freshness, citrus tension and brighter aroma. [[grape.muscadelle|Muscadelle]] can contribute a more fragrant, floral accent.

That is a division of roles, not a compulsory recipe. Producers, parcels and seasons shift the proportions. A Sémillon-rich wine need not be heavy, while more Sauvignon does not guarantee freshness; ripeness, botrytis, pressing, fermentation and acid structure all matter.
:::

:::detail{#semillon-sauvignon-en-muscadelle parent="druivenrassen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026"}
### Four permitted varieties, not only three familiar names

The current specification permits Sémillon, Sauvignon Blanc, [[grape.sauvignon-gris|Sauvignon Gris]] and Muscadelle. Sauvignon Gris is legally a separate permitted variety, not another spelling. The rules prescribe no universal blend percentages for the bottle. [@source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026]

Varieties and parcels do not ripen together. A selective pass may therefore chiefly affect one grape or site. The final blend joins lots with different concentration, acidity and aroma — a second selection after the harvesters' work.
:::

:::section{#wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026"}
## Viticulture and winemaking — every sound berry is earned separately

For Sauternes, grapes must be overripe and show noble rot. Harvest is manual, in *tries successives*: successive passes through the vineyard. Instead of clearing a parcel in one day, pickers take only suitable berries or bunches and leave the rest hanging. [@source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026]

That explains quality, risk and price together. Shrivelled berries contain little juice; repeated rounds demand much labour; rain between passes may change the outlook. In a difficult year, a parcel may yield almost no fruit fit for strict selection.
:::

:::detail{#tries-successives parent="wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026"}
### Harvest is a sequence of decisions, not a moment

Botrytis rarely develops everywhere at once. Within one bunch a berry may remain firm, another be ideally concentrated and a third already lost. Pickers read that mosaic and return after new damp-and-dry cycles. Each decision is to take, leave or reject.

After pressing, the must is exceptionally rich in sugar. Yeasts struggle in it; fermentation is often slow and stops while considerable sugar remains. The result must do more than taste sweet: alcohol, acidity, concentration and bitter or spicy botrytis notes need to balance.
:::

:::detail{#van-pers-tot-vat parent="wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026"}
### The appellation does not prescribe a château recipe

Press fractions may be handled separately and fermentation may take place in barrel or tank. Many ambitious producers use oak for fermentation or [[concept.elevage|maturation]], but new-oak share, duration, barrel selection and [[concept.assemblage|blending]] differ. The official text presents barrel fermentation as common practice, not an exclusive obligation.

A natural fermentation stop under the pressure of sugar and alcohol is historically recognisable, but modern cellars monitor temperature, microbiology and stability closely. “Naturally sweet” means the sugar came from the grape and remains after fermentation; it does not mean the cellar requires no technical care.
:::

:::section{#appellationregels depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026"}
## Appellation rules — noble rot is part of the promise

Sauternes is legally reserved for still white wine with residual sugar from its delimited origin. The rules require overripe grapes with noble rot and manual harvesting through successive selections. Botrytis is therefore not merely a common stylistic choice here, but part of what the name protects. [@source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026]

The appellation also sets permitted varieties, vineyard practices, ripeness, yield, vinification and minimum maturation. It does not guarantee château quality, a particular blend or aroma. A simple and a great Sauternes share a legal foundation, not necessarily the same depth.
:::

:::detail{#wat-sauternes-juridisch-belooft parent="appellationregels" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026"}
### Rules protect a method and a place

The current text prohibits plant-protection products specifically targeting botrytis and total chemical weeding of parcels. That fits an appellation needing the beneficial form of botrytis, but does not mean every producer farms organically. [@source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026]

Nor does “hand harvested” reveal how strictly an estate selects, how many passes were actually required or how much fruit was rejected. Law creates a floor; reputation is built in the space above it.
:::

:::detail{#cijfers-en-grenzen parent="appellationregels" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026"}
### Current thresholds in proportion

Under the 2026 specification, planting density is at least 6,500 vines per hectare. Must must contain at least 221 grams of sugar per litre, the base yield is 25 hectolitres per hectare and finished wine contains at least 45 grams of fermentable sugar per litre. Minimum maturation runs until 15 March after harvest. [@source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026]

The figures clarify the legal category but cannot predict perceived sweetness. Acidity, alcohol, glycerol, temperature and aroma affect how sweet a wine feels. Many bottles also sit well above the minima.
:::

:::section{#classificatie-en-producenten depth="foundation" source_refs="source.gcc1855-classification-overview source.gcc1855-classification-history"}
## Classification and producers — Yquem at the summit, but not alone

The 1855 classification gave sweet white Bordeaux its own list of 27 crus from Sauternes and Barsac. [[producer.chateau-d-yquem|Château d'Yquem]] alone received the rank *Premier Cru Supérieur*. Below it sit eleven Premiers Crus and fifteen Deuxièmes Crus. [@source.gcc1855-classification-overview]

That simple structure belongs to foundation knowledge because it shapes how Sauternes is read worldwide. Yet it is not a current competition. The list grew from nineteenth-century trading reputation and was not designed to reassess every present estate, wine or year.
:::

:::detail{#yquem-en-de-twee-rangen parent="classificatie-en-producenten" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.gcc1855-classification-overview"}
### A route through producers, not a buying chart

Alongside Yquem, Premiers Crus such as [[producer.chateau-guiraud|Château Guiraud]], [[producer.chateau-rieussec|Château Rieussec]] and [[producer.chateau-suduiraut|Château Suduiraut]] offer different entry points to Sauternes. In Barsac, Château Climens and Château Coutet are important anchors. The full list belongs with the [[classification.bordeaux-1855|1855 entity]].

Producers differ in site, grape proportions, strictness of selection, barrel regime and desired balance. Rank records historical position; the concrete bottle shows how place, season and decisions came together.
:::

:::detail{#reputatie-is-geen-volledigheid parent="classificatie-en-producenten" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.gcc1855-classification-history"}
### What the list leaves outside the frame

Unclassified estates can make compelling wine, while a classified château may produce other [[concept.cuvee|cuvées]] beside its principal wine. Ownership, vineyard holdings and market position have changed since 1855, while the hierarchy has largely remained.

The classification applies to named crus, not to the appellation as a whole and not automatically to every bottle from the same ownership group. Use it as historical navigation, never as a substitute for producer, wine name, [[concept.vintage|vintage]] and condition.
:::

:::section{#wijnstijl-en-flesontwikkeling depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-overview source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026"}
## Wine style and bottle development — sweetness lifted by acidity

Young Sauternes can be golden and exuberantly scented with citrus, peach, apricot, flowers, honey and fresh spice. It tastes sweet and often full or creamy, but good balance requires lively acidity and sometimes a fine bitterness. Without that counterforce richness tires; with it, richness gains length. [@source.inao-sauternes-overview]

Botrytis brings more than sugar concentration. It can produce associations with saffron, beeswax, candied peel or ginger. No bottle needs to show all of them. The grape mix, infection, year and maturation alter the register.
:::

:::detail{#zoetheid-met-spanning parent="wijnstijl-en-flesontwikkeling" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026"}
### Why two equally sweet wines can feel different

Residual sugar is only one axis. Higher acidity makes the opening more vivid; alcohol and glycerol add weight; botrytis may contribute fragrance as well as a faintly bitter or savoury edge. Cool serving temperature tempers sweetness, though serving too cold closes the aroma.

The better comparison is not “which has more sugar?” but “how do sweetness, acidity, alcohol, texture and aromatic length relate?” Sauternes then becomes a lesson in balance, not merely a dessert-wine category.
:::

:::detail{#tijd-in-de-fles parent="wijnstijl-en-flesontwikkeling" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026"}
### From gold to amber, uncertainty included

With bottle age, colour may deepen and fresh citrus and stone fruit can move towards marmalade, dried fruit, honey, nuts, wax, tobacco or spice. Concentrated bottles from strong years may develop for decades, but not every Sauternes needs age or automatically improves.

Acidity, concentration, closure, bottle size and storage shape its life. A half bottle generally matures faster than a magnum; warmth and light can tire wine prematurely. An old name without reliable provenance is no guarantee.
:::

:::section{#etiket-en-aankoop depth="foundation" source_refs="source.dgccrf-wine-labels-2025 source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026"}
## Labels and buying — begin with the exact name

Read “Sauternes” first, then producer, cuvée, vintage, bottle size and bottler. The appellation promises sweet still white wine under protected rules. “Grand cru classé” or an 1855 rank requires checking the exact château and wine name; a gold label or château picture proves nothing. [@source.dgccrf-wine-labels-2025]

Half bottles are common and practical because a small serving can already be intense. Buy older bottles chiefly on condition: fill level, seepage, colour, storage and provenance matter at least as much as a famous year.
:::

:::detail{#droog-wit-is-geen-sauternes parent="etiket-en-aankoop" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026"}
### A dry white from a Sauternes château carries another origin

Several estates also make dry white wine. It may share an owner, grapes or vineyard surroundings, but does not meet the sweet Sauternes category and is sold under another permitted origin. Château identity and appellation are not the same.

Barsac, in turn, may appear as Barsac or Sauternes when wine and origin meet the conditions. When comparing, always record the precise appellation and cuvée rather than the estate alone.
:::

:::detail{#serveren-en-combineren parent="etiket-en-aankoop" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-sauternes-overview"}
### Beyond dessert and foie gras

Serving cool but not icy — roughly 10–12 °C as a starting point — lets fragrance and freshness coexist. A small white-wine glass often works better than a tiny liqueur glass because aroma needs room.

Salt, fat, spice and blue cheese can offer useful contrasts; fruit desserts work when the dish is not plainly sweeter than the wine. Pairings are not laws, however. Sauternes can also be explored alone, as an aperitif or beside savoury food.
:::

:::section{#hedendaagse-ontwikkelingen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inrae-bordeaux-climate-adaptation source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026"}
## Contemporary developments — a famous wine with a vulnerable harvest

Sauternes remains dependent on a narrow sequence of autumn conditions and on people passing through the vineyard repeatedly. Production is therefore vulnerable to rain, heat, drought, labour costs and a market in which sweet wine no longer has an automatic place at the table.

Producers respond through finer parcel knowledge, sorting, adjusted vineyard care, different bottle sizes, dry companion wines and new serving contexts. Those choices change the commercial face, but the appellation's core remains concentrated botrytised fruit selected by hand.
:::

:::detail{#klimaat-arbeid-en-markt parent="hedendaagse-ontwikkelingen" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inrae-bordeaux-climate-adaptation source.inao-sauternes-specification-2026"}
### More warmth does not simply make botrytis easier

Warmer seasons may accelerate ripeness, but can also lower acidity sooner or stress grapes through drought before beneficial botrytis arrives. Wetter harvest periods may instead favour grey rot. The challenge is not maximum sugar, but sound fruit with sufficient tension at the right moment.

Canopy, soil care, harvest date and strict selection therefore grow still more important. Because every extra pass costs money and usable volume remains uncertain, climate adaptation here is economic as well as viticultural. Volatile sales or area figures belong in dated analysis, not as permanent facts on this overview page.
:::
