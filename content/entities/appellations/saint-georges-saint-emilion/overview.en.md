:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-saint-georges-saint-emilion-overview source.legifrance-saint-georges-saint-emilion-specification-2011"}
Saint-Georges-Saint-Émilion is the smallest of the four satellite appellations, lying on a limestone plateau and south-facing slopes above the Barbanne. The old village of Saint-Georges now belongs administratively to Montagne; in wine law it retains its own name and boundary.

The appellation makes red wine only, generally centred on Merlot with Cabernet Franc and Cabernet Sauvignon as structural partners.
:::

## Location and boundaries — a former commune within Montagne

The appellation occupies the territory of the former commune of Saint-Georges, absorbed into Montagne in 1973. Its plateau faces south across the Barbanne towards the hills of Saint-Émilion. Only officially delimited parcels carry the name.

[[appellation.montagne-saint-emilion|Montagne-Saint-Émilion]] surrounds or borders this small zone but is another PDO. A modern postal address alone therefore does not reveal which appellation owns a parcel.
:::

:::detail{#administratie-en-wijngebied parent="ligging-en-afbakening" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.legifrance-saint-georges-saint-emilion-specification-2011"}
### Administration changed; origin remained

The municipal merger did not make Saint-Georges a vanished wine name. The appellation preserves an older geographic identity within the new commune. It clearly shows why wine geography does not automatically move with present administrative boundaries.
:::

:::section{#geschiedenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-saint-georges-saint-emilion-overview source.legifrance-saint-georges-saint-emilion-specification-2011"}
## History — a small plateau with a long name dispute

Gallo-Roman remains point to ancient settlement and farming. Medieval religious orders and trade through Libourne contributed to viticulture's development. Facing Saint-Émilion made the name relationship understandable but not legally automatic.

A 1921 decision allowed Saint-Émilion to be used provided Saint-Georges stayed before it. The AOC was recognised in 1936. Since the 1973 municipal merger, the appellation also preserves visibility for the former place name.
:::

:::section{#landschap-klimaat-en-bodem depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-saint-georges-saint-emilion-overview source.legifrance-saint-georges-saint-emilion-specification-2011"}
## Landscape, climate and soils — a southern edge of limestone and clay

The village and many vineyards lie on limestone and clay-limestone, with slopes falling towards the Barbanne. Southern aspects capture light and warmth; relief helps shed water. Clay also keeps moisture for dry periods.

The Barbanne separates this plateau from the old jurisdiction of Saint-Émilion but forms no sudden climate wall. Parcel elevation, soil depth and wind create local differences.
:::

:::detail{#expositie-en-evenwicht parent="landschap-klimaat-en-bodem" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-saint-georges-saint-emilion-overview"}
### Southern exposure helps and limits

Extra sun can guide Cabernet and Merlot to ripeness in cooler years. During hot years the same position needs protection from dehydration and sunburn. Aspect remains an advantage only when canopy and harvest date keep it in balance.
:::

:::section{#druivenrassen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.legifrance-saint-georges-saint-emilion-specification-2011"}
## Grape varieties — Merlot with Cabernet structure

[[grape.merlot|Merlot]] dominates and generally gives ripe fruit, volume and rounded tannin. [[grape.cabernet-franc|Cabernet Franc]] can bring fragrance and freshness; [[grape.cabernet-sauvignon|Cabernet Sauvignon]] firmness and ageing structure. [[grape.malbec|Malbec]], Carménère and Petit Verdot also belong to the broader legal grape family.

Which counterpoint is actually audible depends on parcel, vintage and [[concept.assemblage|blend]].
:::

:::section{#wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.legifrance-saint-georges-saint-emilion-specification-2011"}
## Viticulture and winemaking — ripeness on a sunny slope

Minimum planting density is 5,500 vines per hectare. Pruning and canopy limits distribute the crop and help bunches ripen evenly. Skin fermentation builds colour and tannin; malolactic conversion and maturation then follow.

Barriques are used but are not evidence of origin. Extraction and oak should suit the fruit rather than force a preconceived image of power.
:::

:::section{#appellationregels depth="foundation" source_refs="source.legifrance-saint-georges-saint-emilion-specification-2011"}
## Appellation rules — red from the former commune

Saint-Georges-Saint-Émilion protects still red wine only from the delimited zone of former Saint-Georges. Base yield is 53 hectolitres per hectare and minimum natural alcohol is 11%. Wine matures until at least 15 March after harvest. [@source.legifrance-saint-georges-saint-emilion-specification-2011]
:::

:::section{#classificatie-en-producenten depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-saint-georges-saint-emilion-overview"}
## Classification and producers — small is not a classification

The appellation has no official château classification and is outside Saint-Émilion Grand Cru Classé. Limited area can make a small group of estates conspicuous, but familiarity is no legal rank.

Assess producers through parcel, blend, vintage and method. An estate may also make Montagne-Saint-Émilion; each bottle keeps its own origin.
:::

:::section{#wijnstijl-en-flesontwikkeling depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-saint-georges-saint-emilion-overview"}
## Wine style and bottle development — ripe fruit with clear shape

Plum, cassis, cherry and spice can accompany a fleshy texture and firm but ripe tannins. Cabernet and oak may give more line; tobacco, leather and earth can emerge with age.

The small area does not make style uniform. Serve around 16–18°C and let cuvée and vintage guide air and cellaring.
:::

:::section{#etiket-en-aankoop depth="foundation" source_refs="source.legifrance-saint-georges-saint-emilion-specification-2011"}
## Labels and buying — four parts that must not disappear

Saint-Georges-Saint-Émilion is the complete protected name. “Saint-Émilion” alone means another origin; “Montagne” in the address does not change the appellation either. Shared words do not confer the Saint-Émilion classification.
:::

:::section{#hedendaagse-ontwikkelingen depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inrae-bordeaux-climate-adaptation"}
## Contemporary developments — a sunny edge in warmer years

Under climate warming, the south-facing slope can shift from asset to risk. Canopy shade, ground cover, water infiltration and precise harvest dates help keep fruit and freshness together. Small scale makes parcel decisions visible but not automatically simple.
:::
