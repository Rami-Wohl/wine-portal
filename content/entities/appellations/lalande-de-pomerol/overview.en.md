Lalande-de-Pomerol begins north of [[appellation.pomerol|Pomerol]], beyond the Barbanne. They share Merlot, a Right Bank climate and a family of names, but Lalande-de-Pomerol is an independent appellation with its own boundaries and rules.

Gentle terraces around Lalande meet higher clay-limestone and gravel ground in Néac. “A lighter Pomerol” is therefore too simple a summary of both wine and place.
:::

## Location and boundaries — Lalande and Néac above the Barbanne

The appellation covers delimited vineyard parcels in Lalande-de-Pomerol and Néac. The Barbanne marks the familiar divide from Pomerol to the south; Lussac and Montagne lie farther east.

A stream or commune boundary does not tell the whole parcel story, but it clarifies identity: Lalande-de-Pomerol is not a northern district of Pomerol. The complete name is the protected origin.
:::

:::detail{#twee-gemeenten parent="ligging-en-afbakening" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.legifrance-lalande-de-pomerol-specification-2012"}
### Two communes, several landscapes

Gently rolling gravel and sandy terraces dominate around Lalande. Relief becomes clearer in Néac, with more clay-limestone and gravel over clay. Estates whose parcels span the zone may therefore have different building blocks for their blend.
:::

:::section{#geschiedenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-lalande-de-pomerol-overview source.legifrance-lalande-de-pomerol-specification-2012"}
## History — a white crusader's cross in wine country

Viticulture predates the appellation by centuries. From the Middle Ages, the Order of Saint John of Jerusalem held a commandery in the district; the Maltese cross survives as a regional emblem. Libourne later connected its wines with river trade and export.

The AOC was defined in 1936. After phylloxera, the Libournais-based agronomist Albert Macquin played an important role in spreading grafting onto American rootstocks, part of the vineyard's reconstruction.
:::

:::section{#landschap-klimaat-en-bodem depth="foundation" source_refs="source.legifrance-lalande-de-pomerol-specification-2012 source.inao-lalande-de-pomerol-overview"}
## Landscape, climate and soils — from terrace gravel to clay-limestone

In Lalande-de-Pomerol, sand, gravel and pebbles sit in a sandy-clay matrix on middle river terraces. Néac adds clay-limestone and gravel with more clay. Gentle relief and delimited, better-drained parcels distinguish the vineyard from wet low ground.

The temperate Atlantic climate offers long growing seasons but also humidity and disease pressure. Clay can retain water through dry periods; gravel warms more quickly and drains readily. Neither guarantees ripeness or quality by itself.
:::

:::detail{#barbanne-geen-smaakgrens parent="landschap-klimaat-en-bodem" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.legifrance-lalande-de-pomerol-specification-2012"}
### The Barbanne is not a wall of flavour

The stream helps separate origins, but wine does not abruptly change character at the water. Soil sequences, aspect and producer choices work at a finer scale. The legal boundary is exact; sensory transition remains gradual and varied.
:::

:::section{#druivenrassen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.legifrance-lalande-de-pomerol-specification-2012"}
## Grape varieties — Merlot with room for Cabernet

[[grape.merlot|Merlot]] is the leading grape and can give rounded fruit and supple texture on clay and sandy clay. [[grape.cabernet-franc|Cabernet Franc]] often adds fragrance, freshness and finer structure; [[grape.cabernet-sauvignon|Cabernet Sauvignon]] and [[grape.malbec|Malbec]] can play smaller roles in firmness, colour and spice.

The legal list is broader than a typical bottle. Proportion, harvest date and parcel determine the final [[concept.assemblage|blend]].
:::

:::section{#wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.legifrance-lalande-de-pomerol-specification-2012"}
## Viticulture and winemaking — ripe fruit, not softness alone

Base density is 5,500 vines per hectare, with transitional provisions for older plantings. Growers watch early-ripening Merlot closely: too early can leave greenness, too late can cost freshness. Cabernet generally needs more warmth and time.

Skin fermentation builds colour and tannin; concrete, steel and oak can then bring different accents. New oak is not an appellation rule, and a supple style need not be simple or oak-free.
:::

:::section{#appellationregels depth="foundation" source_refs="source.legifrance-lalande-de-pomerol-specification-2012"}
## Appellation rules — red wine only

Lalande-de-Pomerol protects still red wine from delimited parcels in Lalande-de-Pomerol and Néac. The specification governs grapes, density, pruning, ripeness, maximum alcohol after enrichment and maturation, among other matters.

Base yield is 53 hectolitres per hectare. Wine must mature at least until 15 March after harvest and may reach consumers from 31 March. [@source.legifrance-lalande-de-pomerol-specification-2012]
:::

:::section{#classificatie-en-producenten depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-lalande-de-pomerol-overview"}
## Classification and producers — no shadow ranking of Pomerol

Lalande-de-Pomerol has no official château classification. Producers range from small family estates to larger businesses and may also make wine in other Libournais appellations.

Price comparison with Pomerol is tempting but is not a meaningful classification. Judge a bottle by producer, parcels, blend, vintage and maturation.
:::

:::section{#wijnstijl-en-flesontwikkeling depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-lalande-de-pomerol-overview"}
## Wine style and bottle development — plum, suppleness and sometimes more grip

Many wines show plum, cherry and dark berries with rounded tannins. Clay- and Cabernet-rich blends or firmer extraction can bring more grip and ageing capacity; sandy, fruit-led work may open sooner.

Serve around 16–18°C. Air helps a young firm bottle, but prolonged decanting is no universal rule. Bottle development remains specific to cuvée and vintage.
:::

:::section{#etiket-en-aankoop depth="foundation" source_refs="source.legifrance-lalande-de-pomerol-specification-2012"}
## Labels and buying — three words, one independent origin

Read Lalande-de-Pomerol in full. “Pomerol” within the name does not make the wine Pomerol, and “Lalande” alone is not a valid abbreviation. The Maltese cross may appear as a district reference but does not replace the appellation.

Then look for producer, vintage and bottler. An estate may work in several appellations; the precise origin line remains decisive.
:::

:::section{#hedendaagse-ontwikkelingen depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inrae-bordeaux-climate-adaptation"}
## Contemporary developments — Merlot under heat and drought

Early-ripening Merlot can accumulate sugar rapidly in hot years before every structural element is balanced. Canopy shade, soil care and harvest planning matter more; clay helps buffer water but cannot cancel prolonged drought. In other seasons, humidity still governs disease pressure and fruit selection.
:::

:::key-idea{#kernidee depth="foundation"}
Lalande-de-Pomerol is not a derivative class of Pomerol but a red appellation in Lalande and Néac. Merlot connects the district, while its mosaic of gravel, sand and clay-limestone explains differences within the name.
:::
