:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-cerons-overview source.boagri-cerons-specification-2023"}
**Cérons** is a small sweet-white-wine appellation on the left bank of the Garonne. It covers parts of Cérons, Illats and Podensac within the broader [[appellation.graves|Graves]] landscape, just north of [[appellation.barsac|Barsac]]. Low gravel rises, sandy hollows and clay-limestone soils meet the damp autumn rhythm of river and streams.

That does not make Cérons a miniature copy of its famous southern neighbour. It has its own boundaries and rules, and growers decide afresh each year which fruit can justify a costly, selectively harvested sweet wine.
:::

:::section{#ligging-en-afbakening depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-cerons-specification-2023"}
## Location and boundaries — three communes between Graves and Barsac

The area covers Cérons, Illats and Podensac, roughly thirty kilometres southeast of Bordeaux, inside the larger Graves production area. The Saint-Cricq stream marks a landscape transition towards Barsac in the south, although a stream line alone does not describe the complete legal parcel boundary. [@source.boagri-cerons-specification-2023]

Only precisely delimited parcels qualify. Harvesting, vinification, preparation and maturation are tied to the geographical area and a defined nearby zone. The same surroundings can carry several wine names, but not at whim: each appellation sets its own conditions.
:::

:::section{#geschiedenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-cerons-specification-2023"}
## History — a crossroads beside river and road

The old place name *Sirione* is associated with a settlement and transport route. It attests to a long-inhabited landscape, not automatically to two thousand years of unchanged sweet-wine production. The later identity grew within the trading world of Bordeaux, the Garonne and historic Barsac.

From the early modern period, northern European demand—with a major Dutch role—encouraged white wines offering sweetness and keeping capacity. Cérons was recognised as an AOC in 1936, giving it a legally delimited name of its own. [@source.boagri-cerons-specification-2023]
:::

:::detail{#handel-en-erkenning parent="geschiedenis" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.boagri-cerons-specification-2023"}
### A small name within a changing vineyard

The 2023 specification describes far more delimited potential land than the surface actually declared for Cérons in recent years. The wider local vineyard also shifted markedly towards red wine in the second half of the twentieth century. These are time-bound production signals, not reasons to treat the sweet origin as historically unimportant.

An old regional practice survived as a small, independent appellation while production preferences around it changed.
:::

:::section{#landschap-klimaat-en-bodem depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-cerons-specification-2023"}
## Landscape, climate and soils — gravel rises in a low transition zone

Cérons occupies low, gently undulating ground. River deposits left gravel on modest croupes, with sandier hollows and woodland between them. Towards Barsac, limestone, marl and clay become more influential. Old stone quarries recall that substrate. [@source.boagri-cerons-specification-2023]

This transition explains differences in drainage and water availability. Gravel warms and drains differently from clay or calcareous ground, but no soil guarantees ripeness, botrytis or quality. Site, season and human work remain decisive.
:::

:::detail{#water-en-mist parent="landschap-klimaat-en-bodem" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.boagri-cerons-specification-2023"}
### Water only works in partnership with air

The Garonne, smaller streams and damp low ground can bring autumn morning mist. This may help [[concept.botrytis|Botrytis cinerea]] establish on ripe grapes. Dry intervals and ventilation must then keep desirable shrivelling from turning into harmful rot.

The landscape supplies conditions, not an automatic result. Passerillage on the vine can also provide concentration, and both processes may occur within one harvest.
:::

## Grape varieties — Sémillon and its aromatic partners

[[grape.semillon|Sémillon]] is generally the base, able to build richness and texture and suit noble rot. [[grape.sauvignon-blanc|Sauvignon Blanc]] and [[grape.sauvignon-gris|Sauvignon Gris]] can add acidity and tension; [[grape.muscadelle|Muscadelle]] can make the profile more floral and spicy.

The law permits all four but prescribes no fixed proportions. Plantings, harvest selection and producer determine what enters a cuvée.
:::

:::section{#wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-cerons-specification-2023"}
## Viticulture and winemaking — selecting concentration, not manufacturing it

Cérons is made from overripe grapes with noble rot and/or passerillage on the vine. Harvesting must be by hand in successive tries. Pickers return to choose suitable bunches or parts while other fruit ripens further or is rejected. [@source.boagri-cerons-specification-2023]

Concentrated grapes yield little juice. Fermentation of the sugar-rich must leaves residual sugar; barrel, tank and maturation can shape texture and aroma. They never replace selection in the vineyard.
:::

:::detail{#tries-zonder-zekerheid parent="wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.boagri-cerons-specification-2023"}
### Sweet wine competes for time, labour and healthy fruit

Each trie needs a picking team and exposes remaining grapes to another spell of weather. Yield and composition are difficult to predetermine. Suitable fruit from the area may, under different conditions, also enter dry white or red Graves. The sweet cuvée must genuinely justify its extra selection.
:::

:::section{#appellationregels depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-cerons-specification-2023"}
## Appellation rules — a sweet name within Graves

Cérons is reserved for still white wine with residual sugar. Area, parcels, grapes, vine density, overripeness, hand-picked tries, yield and minimum maturation are regulated. This makes the name verifiable but not uniform: producer, parcel and vintage continue to shape style.
:::

:::detail{#actuele-drempels parent="appellationregels" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.boagri-cerons-specification-2023"}
### The current figures behind the name

The 2023 specification requires at least 5,000 vines per hectare and, for every permitted grape, at least 221 grams of sugar per litre of must. The base yield is 40 hectolitres per hectare. Bottled wine retains at least 45 grams of fermentable sugar per litre and matures until at least 15 January after harvest. [@source.boagri-cerons-specification-2023]

Its different sugar threshold from neighbouring appellations shows why ‘sweet Bordeaux’ is not one legal recipe. These remain minimum conditions, not a flavour specification or quality score.
:::

:::section{#classificatie-en-producenten depth="foundation" source_refs="source.boagri-cerons-specification-2023"}
## Classification and producers — small does not mean ranked

Cérons has no official château classification. Its limited production also makes any loose selection of familiar names quickly time-bound. Judge producers instead through vineyard work, strictness of selection, balance and performance over time.
:::

:::section{#wijnstijl-en-flesontwikkeling depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-cerons-overview source.boagri-cerons-specification-2023"}
## Wine style and bottle development — richness with a Graves accent

Cérons may show ripe citrus, peach, apricot, flowers, honey and candied fruit, carried by sweetness and fresh acidity. Some wines remain lighter; stricter botrytis selection and maturation may bring greater density, spice or toast. ‘Gravel’ is not a literal aroma.

Bottle age can develop dried fruit, marmalade, wax, spice and nuts. How well and long this proceeds depends on balance, concentration, producer, vintage and storage. A small origin is not a short expiry date, but neither does it guarantee long ageing.
:::

:::section{#etiket-en-aankoop depth="foundation" source_refs="source.dgccrf-wine-labels-2025 source.boagri-cerons-specification-2023"}
## Labels and buying — Cérons is the wine name, not just the village

Cérons on a label denotes the sweet white appellation. Also check producer, vintage, bottler and bottle size. [[appellation.graves|Graves]] on another bottle may come from the same broad surroundings, but indicates a different category and rules.

[[appellation.barsac|Barsac]] begins immediately south and is better known; it is not a higher tier under which Cérons sits. Compare through origin, harvest selection, balance and producer rather than name status alone.
:::

:::section{#hedendaagse-ontwikkelingen depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inrae-bordeaux-climate-adaptation source.boagri-cerons-specification-2023"}
## Contemporary developments — a deliberate choice to make little wine

A warmer season can combine early sugar accumulation with lower acidity or a later, uncertain botrytis window. Drought and heat can stress skins; heavy autumn rain can undo selection work. Soil care, canopy and the ability to react quickly at harvest become more important.

Cérons is also a small commercial category beside dry and red Graves. Labour and demand therefore enter the decision to make sweet wine. Its identity is strongest when the name is used only for fruit that truly bears the cost of intensive selection.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Cérons is the small sweet voice of a transitional landscape within Graves: three communes, low gravel croupes and clay-limestone soils, with hand-picked tries linking autumn moisture to a balanced wine.
:::
