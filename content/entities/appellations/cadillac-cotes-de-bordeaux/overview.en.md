Cadillac Côtes de Bordeaux follows the hills above the Right Bank of the Garonne for sixty kilometres, from the edge of Bordeaux beyond Cadillac-sur-Garonne. The appellation protects red wine only, usually centred on Merlot with Cabernet and Malbec as possible counterpoints.

The full name prevents the main confusion: Cadillac Côtes de Bordeaux is red; [[appellation.cadillac|Cadillac]] without *Côtes de Bordeaux* is a separate appellation for sweet white wine.
:::

## Location and boundaries — a long, narrow Garonne bank

The legal zone covers 39 communes on the Right Bank of the Garonne. The strip is about sixty kilometres long and no more than a few kilometres wide. It runs from urban fringes near Bordeaux through wine villages and river bends towards the south.

Cadillac is a supplementary geographical denomination within [[appellation.cotes-de-bordeaux|Côtes de Bordeaux]]. Only officially delimited parcels within the zone may carry the complete name.
:::

:::detail{#een-lint-geen-eenheid parent="ligging-en-afbakening" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.inao-cotes-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
### A ribbon is not uniform

North and south, river terraces and higher plateaus, town and countryside all sit within the long strip. “Cadillac soil” is therefore too coarse. Slope, elevation, substrate and distance from the river need a more precise reading.
:::

:::section{#geschiedenis depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-cotes-bordeaux-specification-2026 source.msha-bordeaux-wine-history"}
## History — wine hills along the trading river

The Garonne carried wine from these slopes to Bordeaux's port. Medieval trade and later investment by Bordeaux citizens encouraged vineyards and country houses, the so-called *folies*, along the river.

Premières Côtes de Bordeaux was recognised in 1937 and later revised. Since 2009, the red origin has carried the name Cadillac Côtes de Bordeaux within the collective AOP. The old name also survives in a separate sweet-wine appellation, so historic labels require careful reading. [@source.inao-cotes-bordeaux-specification-2026]
:::

:::section{#landschap-klimaat-en-bodem depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-cotes-bordeaux-specification-2026 source.cadillac-cotes-de-bordeaux-overview"}
## Landscape, climate and soils — a limestone edge above the Garonne

The hills follow the river's meanders. Clay-limestone, clay-gravel and stony terrace soils alternate, with leached silty soils on plateaus. Slopes can provide drainage and different aspects, while clay stores water for dry periods.

The Garonne and Atlantic influence moderate temperature, but humidity and rain remain disease risks. Southern sectors also sit close to sweet-white appellations, without making that style part of Cadillac Côtes de Bordeaux.
:::

:::detail{#hoogte-water-en-stad parent="landschap-klimaat-en-bodem" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.cadillac-cotes-de-bordeaux-overview"}
### Elevation, water and town

Relief supplies sun and airflow, but steeper ground needs erosion management. Urban pressure challenges vineyard landscapes in the northern communes; farther south, farming and river views remain more dominant. The appellation is also a story of landscape preservation.
:::

:::section{#druivenrassen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-cotes-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
## Grape varieties — Merlot along a long strip

[[grape.merlot|Merlot]] generally forms the rounded, fruity core. [[grape.cabernet-sauvignon|Cabernet Sauvignon]] can add structure on warm gravel, [[grape.cabernet-franc|Cabernet Franc]] fragrance and freshness, and [[grape.malbec|Malbec]] colour and spice. Carménère and Petit Verdot are accessory.

The list is shared with other red Côtes, but the [[concept.assemblage|blend]] gains meaning through parcel and year. A long appellation cannot yield one fixed proportion.
:::

:::section{#wijnbouw-en-wijnmaken depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-cotes-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
## Viticulture and winemaking — red given time to cohere

The winemaker follows ripening parcel by parcel, ferments with skins and decides how much colour and tannin to extract. Malolactic conversion is compulsory; tank, concrete and oak can then create different balances between fruit, oxygen and wood.

A supple cuvée does not necessarily require less care than a powerful one. Harvest selection, measured extraction and a blend not dominated by greenness or oak matter more than weight.
:::

:::section{#appellationregels depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-cotes-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
## Appellation rules — only red under the full name

Cadillac Côtes de Bordeaux protects still red wine. The specification determines communes, parcels, grapes, ripeness, yield, cellar practice and maturation. Dry or sweet white from the wider district therefore needs another appellation name.

The legal name guarantees scope and production conditions, not a fixed taste or château class.
:::

:::detail{#actuele-drempels parent="appellationregels" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.inao-cotes-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
### Current thresholds

Minimum planting density is 4,500 vines per hectare. Red with a geographical denomination has a base yield of 52 hectolitres per hectare and minimum natural alcohol of 11.5%. It matures at least until 15 March and can reach consumers from 31 March after harvest. [@source.inao-cotes-bordeaux-specification-2026]
:::

:::section{#classificatie-en-producenten depth="foundation" source_refs="source.cadillac-cotes-de-bordeaux-overview"}
## Classification and producers — many estates, no official ladder

Cadillac Côtes de Bordeaux has no official château classification. Family estates, larger wine businesses and varied cuvées share the name. Neighbouring appellations on the same estate may follow other colours and rules.

Read each wine precisely: which origin is written, who bottled it, and which grapes and maturation are named? A future producer collection can show differences without suggesting a rank.
:::

:::section{#wijnstijl-en-flesontwikkeling depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-cotes-bordeaux-specification-2026 source.cadillac-cotes-de-bordeaux-overview"}
## Wine style and bottle development — rounded fruit along a river ribbon

The reds can combine plum, black cherry, red berries and spice with rounded to firm tannin. Merlot-led examples are often approachable young; more Cabernet, extraction or oak can demand time and later bring tobacco and earth.

Serve around 16–18 °C. A juicy wine works young with roast poultry or mushrooms; a firmer example can suit beef or stew. Cellaring remains a producer-, cuvée- and vintage-specific decision.
:::

:::section{#etiket-en-aankoop depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-cotes-bordeaux-specification-2026"}
## Labels and buying — one missing phrase changes the wine

“Cadillac Côtes de Bordeaux” means red. “Cadillac” means sweet white; “Premières Côtes de Bordeaux” may likewise indicate a sweet-white origin or a historic reference. Read the whole line, not only the largest word.

Then check producer, vintage, cuvée and bottler. Cadillac alone is not shorthand for this red appellation.
:::

:::section{#hedendaagse-ontwikkelingen depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inrae-bordeaux-climate-adaptation source.cadillac-cotes-de-bordeaux-overview"}
## Contemporary developments — vines between river and urban growth

Cadillac Côtes de Bordeaux must answer both climate risk and land use. Warmer years can ease ripening, but drought, sunburn and intense rain require shade, water-conscious soils and erosion control.

Clear label explanation also matters: because several appellations use Cadillac, understandable communication of origin is part of the quality experience.
:::

:::key-idea{#kernidee depth="foundation"}
Cadillac Côtes de Bordeaux is the red-wine appellation of a long sequence of Right Bank slopes along the Garonne. Its complete name separates it from sweet white Cadillac; producer, parcel and blend supply the real precision within this long ribbon.
:::
