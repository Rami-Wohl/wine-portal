**Château Canon** occupies the limestone plateau immediately beside the village of [[appellation.saint-emilion|Saint-Émilion]]. CHANEL has owned the estate since 1996, and the [[classification.saint-emilion|2022 classification]] lists it as a Premier Grand Cru Classé—without distinction A.

[[grape.merlot|Merlot]] and [[grape.cabernet-franc|Cabernet Franc]] grow on limestone with clay in its fissures and above old quarries. The [[concept.grand-vin|Grand Vin]] is often associated with ripe fruit, fine structure and freshness; Croix Canon is a separate wine from dedicated plots on the western slope.
:::

## History and ownership — from Clos Saint-Martin to Canon

The property was known as [[producer.clos-saint-martin|Clos Saint-Martin]] in the eighteenth century. Jacques Kanon acquired it in 1760; his name later evolved into “Canon”. After several owners and a long period of uneven performance, CHANEL bought the estate in 1996. Restoration of buildings, cellars and vineyard followed. [@source.chateau-canon-press-kit]

That investment matters, but it is not a tasting note. It helped keep historic land under coherent management and supplied resources for replanting and selection; results still depend on season, grapes and decisions.
:::

:::detail{#herstel-zonder-nieuw-oorsprongsverhaal parent="geschiedenis-en-eigendom" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.chateau-canon-press-kit"}
### Recovery without a new origin story

The post-1996 period is often told as a rebirth. It is more useful to place continuity and change side by side: position, name and historic rank already existed, while ownership, investment and technical precision changed. This prevents recent quality from being credited entirely to one owner.
:::

:::section{#wijngaard-en-terroir depth="foundation" source_refs="source.chateau-canon-terroir"}
## Vineyard and [[concept.terroir|terroir]] — above a porous world of stone

Canon stands high on the plateau. Fossil-rich limestone forms the subsoil; clay fills fissures and can retain water. Historic extraction left a network of quarries beneath the vineyard. Plantings above are currently about 62% Merlot and 38% Cabernet Franc. [@source.chateau-canon-terroir]

Merlot can bring ripe fruit and texture while Cabernet Franc adds structure, fragrance and length. Limestone chiefly influences root environment and water supply. It is therefore more useful to discuss ripening conditions than “minerality” as though stone dissolved directly into wine.
:::

:::detail{#water-en-wortels parent="wijngaard-en-terroir" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.chateau-canon-terroir"}
### Water and roots beneath the plateau

Porous limestone can absorb rain, while clay in fissures retains part of it. This buffer may support ripening during dry periods, but root depth, soil structure and vine age determine whether a plant can access it. The plateau provides resilience, not immunity from heat or drought.
:::

:::section{#grand-vin depth="foundation" source_refs="source.chateau-canon-wines source.chateau-canon-expertise"}
## Grand Vin — fruit held in tension as a house ideal

Château Canon is the principal wine of the classified estate. Its recognizable ideal is clear fruit with breadth, fresh length and refined [[concept.tannin|tannin]]—carried rather than heavy. Merlot and Cabernet Franc each have room in the [[concept.assemblage|blend]]; exact proportions follow the [[concept.vintage|vintage]].

The producer readily connects that profile to limestone. This is useful as a tasting frame, but not a guarantee. Year, harvest timing, [[concept.extraction|extraction]], vessel choice and bottle age can all shift the balance.
:::

:::detail{#zachte-extractie parent="grand-vin" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.chateau-canon-expertise"}
### Staying in contact with the lot

During fermentation, the team uses practices including manual pump-overs. The aim is to wet the cap evenly and adjust [[concept.extraction|extraction]] through tasting. Each lot remains visible before the final blend is made. Precision here means more moments for judgement, not automatically more intervention.
:::

:::section{#overige-wijnen depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.chateau-canon-wines source.chateau-canon-press-kit"}
## Other wines — Croix Canon has its own ground

Croix Canon comes from eleven hectares on the western slope and its foot. It has a dedicated vat room and cellar in the restored twelfth-century Mazerat chapel. The same team makes it, but it is not simply the remainder after selecting the Grand Vin. [@source.chateau-canon-wines]

That makes [[concept.second-wine|second wine]] misleading when read as a production rank. Croix Canon shares the name, appellation and quality ambition, but has dedicated plots and its own generally more approachable profile.
:::

:::section{#classificatie-en-reputatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
## Classification and reputation — Premier Grand Cru Classé, without A

Canon appears in the current 2022 edition as a Premier Grand Cru Classé. Only [[producer.chateau-figeac|Château Figeac]] and [[producer.chateau-pavie|Château Pavie]] carry distinction A in that edition. “Premier” therefore gives Canon a high current rank, but not the highest distinction. [@source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022]

Because the Saint-Émilion classification is revised periodically, this status must always be read with an edition. Rank records the château's formal assessment; it does not automatically predict which vintage or bottle an individual drinker will prefer.
:::

:::detail{#canon-als-plateaureferentie parent="classificatie-en-reputatie" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.chateau-canon-terroir source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
### A plateau reference, not a template for everyone

Canon helps explain why the limestone plateau occupies such a strong place in Saint-Émilion's image. Yet neighbouring estates differ in slope, clay cover, plant material and choices. Canon is a clear case through which to read the plateau, not the standard recipe every Saint-Émilion must follow.
:::

:::section{#etiket-serveren-en-bewaren depth="foundation" source_refs="source.chateau-canon-wines source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
## Label, serving and storage — Canon is not Croix Canon

First check the full name: Château Canon and Croix Canon are different wines. Also verify appellation, [[concept.vintage|vintage]] and classification edition. Young Grand Vin may benefit from air; mature bottles require gentle pouring, attention to sediment and, above all, a trustworthy cool storage history.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Canon makes the plateau tangible: vines above limestone quarries, Merlot beside abundant Cabernet Franc, and a style ideal in which ripe fruit is bounded by freshness and fine structure.
:::
