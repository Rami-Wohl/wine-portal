**Château Ausone** is an exceptionally small seven-hectare estate set like a stone amphitheatre into the slope of [[appellation.saint-emilion-grand-cru|Saint-Émilion Grand Cru]]. Limestone, terraces and a large share of [[grape.cabernet-franc|Cabernet Franc]] give it a distinctive place within [[region.bordeaux|Bordeaux]]. The Vauthier family has tended the vineyard since 1690; Alain Vauthier and his daughter Pauline now represent its eleventh winegrowing generation.

The [[concept.grand-vin|Grand Vin]], Château Ausone, is known for concentration that need not sacrifice freshness or fine structure. The château was a Premier Grand Cru Classé A from 1955, but withdrew before the [[classification.saint-emilion|2022 classification]]. Its A rank is therefore historic, not current.
:::

## History and ownership — an old place and a careful name

Ausone takes its name from Decimus Magnus Ausonius, the fourth-century poet who wrote about Bordeaux and its wine. Historic forms such as *Casteau Dauzone* and *Château Dosone* evolved into Château Ausone. That makes the literary connection real; it does not prove that Ausonius occupied this exact estate. An independent historical source stresses that the popular tradition lacks conclusive evidence. [@source.chateau-ausone-history] [@source.decanter-chateau-ausone-travel]

Family continuity is much more tangible. The Vauthiers have managed the vineyard since 1690. Alain Vauthier and his eldest daughter Pauline make the wine today. At an estate this small, ownership is not abstract: family, daily decisions and a few hectares of land remain close together. [@source.chateau-ausone-practices]
:::

:::detail{#steen-als-archief parent="geschiedenis-en-eigendom" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.chateau-ausone-cellars"}
### Stone as archive

Galleries quarried through centuries run beneath the estate. A Romanesque chapel and an underground religious space connect the slope to medieval [[appellation.saint-emilion|Saint-Émilion]]; parts of the quarries became cellars in the eighteenth century. Heritage here is more than façade: the same rock supplied buildings, sheltered people, stores wine and forms the vine's subsoil. [@source.chateau-ausone-cellars]
:::

:::detail{#romeinse-verleiding parent="geschiedenis-en-eigendom" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.chateau-ausone-history source.decanter-chateau-ausone-travel"}
### The temptation of a perfect origin

A famous poet, archaeological remains and an ancient wine landscape form an irresistible origin story. Yet name history, local archaeology and individual land ownership must remain separate. Ausonius lived in the region and celebrated wine; the idea that his villa occupied today's château remains a tradition. Iconic estates particularly require this distinction between meaning and evidence.
:::

:::section{#wijngaard-en-terroir depth="foundation" source_refs="source.chateau-ausone-terroir source.chateau-ausone-practices source.decanter-chateau-ausone-profile"}
## Vineyard and terroir — seven hectares on limestone

The vineyard occupies small east-southeast-facing terraces. Fossil-rich limestone lies above, while clay joins the limestone on the slope. Rock can absorb and release water, while clay retains moisture. This helps explain how ripeness and freshness may coexist, but it is not an automatic recipe for quality. [@source.chateau-ausone-terroir]

Cabernet Franc now forms the majority of plantings, with [[grape.merlot|Merlot]] as the smaller partner. Vines average about fifty years and some exceed a century. Old plants, slope and limited area allow the team unusual proximity to each plot. [@source.chateau-ausone-practices]
:::

:::detail{#cabernet-franc-op-kalksteen parent="wijngaard-en-terroir" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.chateau-ausone-terroir source.chateau-ausone-practices"}
### Cabernet Franc on the slope

Cabernet Franc ripens later than Merlot and can bring perfume, freshness and linear [[concept.tannin|tannin]]. Shelter and water reserves in limestone and clay help it ripen without making sheer softness the only objective. Merlot generally adds roundness and fruit. The final [[concept.assemblage|blend]], however, changes with the [[concept.vintage|vintage]] and never simply mirrors vineyard percentages.
:::

:::detail{#terroir-zonder-toverformule parent="wijngaard-en-terroir" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.chateau-ausone-terroir source.chateau-ausone-practices"}
### [[concept.terroir|Terroir]] without a magic formula

“Limestone” easily becomes a flavour word, but rock does not translate directly into chalk taste. Water supply, root environment, exposure, plant material, yield and harvest timing interact. Ausone's useful terroir story is therefore causal and modest: the slope creates conditions and choices; it cannot guarantee style or quality.
:::

:::section{#grand-vin depth="foundation" source_refs="source.decanter-chateau-ausone-profile source.chateau-ausone-practices"}
## Grand Vin — small volume, long horizon

Château Ausone is the principal wine. At its best it combines dark and red fruit with floral, herbal and stony associations, a compact core and striking freshness. Young bottles can appear closed and stern: this is generally not a wine that tells its full story immediately.

That profile remains an expectation, not a guarantee. Vintage, bottle age and storage change what a drinker encounters. Ausone's fame and scarcity also describe demand and reputation, not the pleasure of every individual bottle.
:::

:::detail{#selectie-en-opvoeding parent="grand-vin" depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.decanter-chateau-ausone-profile source.chateau-ausone-cellars"}
### Selection and maturation

Lots from the small vineyard are followed separately before the Grand Vin is assembled. [[concept.elevage|Maturation]] then continues in cool, humid underground cellars. Oak, oxygen and time can refine the structure, but should not reduce Cabernet Franc's perfume to a generic wood style.
:::

:::section{#overige-wijnen depth="intermediate" source_refs="source.decanter-chateau-ausone-profile"}
## Other wines — Chapelle d’Ausone

Chapelle d’Ausone first appeared with the 1995 vintage. It comes largely from younger vines that may later supply the Grand Vin. It generally contains relatively more Merlot and can include [[grape.cabernet-sauvignon|Cabernet Sauvignon]]. It is therefore not merely a lighter copy, but a different selection with its own varietal balance.

The usual term [[concept.second-wine|second wine]] is useful provided it is not read as “waste stream”. Selection protects the Grand Vin's identity and gives other fruit an appropriate destination.
:::

:::section{#classificatie-en-reputatie depth="foundation" source_refs="source.decanter-cheval-blanc-ausone-withdrawal-2021 source.inao-saint-emilion-classification-2022"}
## Classification and reputation — historically A, not assessed in 2022

Ausone belonged to the Premiers Grands Crus Classés A from the first Saint-Émilion classification in 1955. Together with [[producer.chateau-cheval-blanc|Château Cheval Blanc]], it chose in 2021 not to apply for the 2022 edition. The château was not demoted: it did not participate. [@source.decanter-cheval-blanc-ausone-withdrawal-2021]

Old labels, books and auctions may correctly show the then-valid A rank. A current description must always date it. Reputation may survive outside the classification, but cannot replace the formal list.
:::

:::detail{#schaars-maar-niet-buiten-kritiek parent="classificatie-en-reputatie" depth="advanced" source_refs="source.decanter-chateau-ausone-profile source.decanter-cheval-blanc-ausone-withdrawal-2021"}
### Scarce, famous and still open to scrutiny

Seven hectares produces little wine. Scarcity, long continuity and a former top rank reinforce one another in the market. That makes Ausone culturally influential, but does not create an independent measure of quality. A learning platform should explain why the name carries such weight while preserving room for differences among years, bottles and preferences.
:::

:::section{#etiket-serveren-en-bewaren depth="foundation" source_refs="source.decanter-chateau-ausone-profile source.decanter-cheval-blanc-ausone-withdrawal-2021"}
## Label, serving and storage — time is part of the wine

Always read producer, appellation, [[concept.vintage|vintage]] and classification wording together. Young Ausone may benefit from air, but no fixed decanting time suits every age and bottle condition. For mature wine, provenance, fill level, cool stable storage and gentle pouring matter more than ceremony.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Ausone is great through concentration at small scale: seven hectares, a limestone slope, abundant Cabernet Franc and centuries of family continuity. Its Roman story and historic A rank both need a date and a critical footnote.
:::
