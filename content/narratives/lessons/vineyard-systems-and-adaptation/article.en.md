:::summary{#orientatie depth="foundation"}
Managing a vineyard means connecting decisions: how do you care for the soil, protect the fruit and organise the work? Names such as organic or regenerative help identify an approach. To assess it, you also need to know what happens, what is checked and which results have been observed.
:::

:::objectives{#leerdoelen depth="foundation"}
- Distinguish a growing practice, a certification check and a measured result.
- Explain the difference between adapting to climate change and reducing emissions.
- Develop a vineyard decision from goal and observation to action and follow-up.
:::

:::section{#een-systeem-van-keuzes depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-sustainability-principles-2016"}
## What makes growing a system?

A **growing system** connects choices about plant material, soil, water, growth and protection. People, resources and organisation are part of it too. The OIV approaches sustainability through environmental, social and economic considerations together. A business therefore has more to weigh than a single favourable soil reading. [@source.oiv-sustainability-principles-2016]

The connections become concrete in a question such as: how do we protect the soil, keep enough water available to the vine and keep the work feasible? Start with that goal. A name for the approach then helps you investigate its commitments. The reference on [[concept.vineyard-systems-sustainability-adaptation|growing systems and sustainability]] explores the approaches further.
:::

:::section{#namen-en-afspraken depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-sustainability-principles-2016 source.ec-organic-production-products source.demeter-biodynamic-standard-2026 source.newton-regenerative-definitions-2020"}
## What commitments come with the name?

**Conventional** is a broad term for mainstream growing; ask which actual practices it describes. **Integrated production** connects prevention, observation and targeted management. It helps assess interventions in relation to one another. [@source.oiv-sustainability-principles-2016]

**Organic** production in the European Union follows defined rules, including those on permitted inputs. Organic winemaking has specific rules too: a claim about grape growing and a claim about the wine do not automatically have the same scope. [@source.ec-organic-production-products]

**Biodynamic** growing includes additional principles and practices. The international Demeter standard for 2026 describes requirements for biodynamic preparations and certification, for example. That standard states what the label requires; it is not itself a study proving a preparation’s effects. [@source.demeter-biodynamic-standard-2026; sections 2.5–2.6 and 6.2]

**Regenerative** growing often emphasises restoring soil and ecosystems. Newton and colleagues’ review found differing definitions: some mainly name practices, others mainly results. Ask which definition and any associated standard a provider uses. [@source.newton-regenerative-definitions-2020]
:::

## From commitment to demonstrated result

“We use ground cover” describes a practice. “The inspection confirms compliance” concerns the associated commitments. “Erosion decreased in these places” is an outcome claim requiring suitable observations. This distinction between process and outcome also helps assess claims about regenerative agriculture. [@source.newton-regenerative-definitions-2020]

Decide beforehand what to monitor, record the starting conditions and use comparable observations over an appropriate period. Record circumstances and setbacks too. The OIV guide connects goals with indicators and with planning, doing, checking and adjusting. [@source.oiv-sustainability-implementation-2020; sections 3.2.7–3.2.9] One change after one season does not automatically establish its cause.
:::

:::section{#aanpassen-en-uitstoot-beperken depth="foundation" source_refs="source.oiv-sustainability-implementation-2020"}
## Adapting and limiting climate change

**Adaptation** means responding to changing conditions, such as heat or drought risk. **Mitigation** aims to limit climate change, for example by reducing greenhouse gas emissions. Both can form part of sustainable management, but answer different questions. [@source.oiv-sustainability-implementation-2020; action area 2.8]

Some choices concern the current season, such as managing the [[concept.pruning-training-canopy-crop-load|canopy]] or ground cover. Others have long-lasting effects, such as plant material, training and the location of a new vineyard. Assess options against local conditions and the intended product. The OIV discusses these different levels together; they do not yield one package suitable everywhere. [@source.oiv-sustainability-implementation-2020; action area 2.8]
:::

## Develop one vineyard decision

Imagine a hypothetical vineyard with runoff after rain and a dry period around ripening. The grower is considering more vegetation between the rows. Ground cover can reduce erosion, but living vegetation can also use water relevant to the vine. Effects depend on conditions and management. [@source.abad-cover-crops-viticulture-soil-2021] [@source.abad-cover-crops-agronomic-performance-2021]

Develop the decision through six questions:

1. **Goal:** what damage do you want to reduce?
2. **Place:** where does runoff occur, and where might water scarcity matter?
3. **Stage:** what does the vine need at this point in the season?
4. **Observation:** what information about runoff, soil and vines is missing?
5. **Action:** what limited change would you investigate, and why?
6. **Follow-up:** how will you monitor both the desired effect and possible drawbacks?

The references on the [[concept.vine-nutrition-soil-management-vineyard-floor|vineyard floor]] and [[concept.vine-water-relations|vine water relations]] help explore the trade-off. Without observations, you cannot supply a result or declare one system the winner.
:::

:::in-the-glass{#waarom-in-het-glas depth="foundation"}
Follow a growing decision through the vine and harvested fruit to the winery. Ask which change in fruit condition or composition has been established and how processing responds. That gives the connection with wine substance. A growing label alone predicts neither a particular flavour nor a quality level.
:::

:::key-idea{#centraal-inzicht depth="foundation"}
Assess a growing system through concrete choices, clear commitments and suitable observations. Make the goal visible, investigate trade-offs and use the outcome to choose again.
:::
