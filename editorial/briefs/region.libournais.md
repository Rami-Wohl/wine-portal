# Content brief — Libournais

## Editorial identity

The Libournais is presented as the historical, commercial and pedagogical wine region around Libourne, not as a single legally delimited appellation. The page must help readers understand why Saint-Émilion, Pomerol and Fronsac belong together without flattening their differences.

## Completeness questions

- Can a first-time reader place the Libournais within Bordeaux and distinguish it from an AOP?
- Does the history explain Libourne's river port, trade and relationship with the surrounding vineyards?
- Are landscape, relief and the broad contrasts among limestone, clay, gravel and sand explained without inventing a uniform terroir map?
- Are Merlot and Cabernet Franc placed in context, without reducing every wine to a single “right-bank style”?
- Are the Saint-Émilion, Pomerol and Fronsac/Canon-Fronsac families oriented clearly, including the satellite appellations?
- Is the Saint-Émilion classification distinguished from geography and from terms such as grand cru?
- Does the page explain the practical meaning—and limits—of “right bank” and “Libournais” on labels and in conversation?
- Are contemporary climate and water questions included at the appropriate depth?
- Do the visuals clarify two contrasting landscapes rather than merely decorate the page?

## Depth calibration

- **Foundation / approximately WSET 2:** orientation, major appellation families, main grapes, broad wine character, Libourne as trading centre.
- **Intermediate / approximately WSET 3:** soil and relief contrasts, appellation structure, viticulture and cellar choices, classification and label pitfalls.
- **Advanced:** ambiguity of regional boundaries, historical trade networks, limits of terroir shorthand and contemporary adaptation questions.

## Source strategy

Use official appellation and trade-body material for current structures, municipal and academic history for Libourne's commercial role, and geological or research sources only where specificity requires them. Widely accepted synthesis does not need a citation after every sentence; contested, legal, quantitative and highly specific claims do.

## Visual plan

Reuse the Saint-Émilion panorama to show limestone relief and the Pomerol vineyard-and-church image to show the flatter plateau. Do not present either image as representative of the entire Libournais.
